## Decision

A sentence is **cited** when a record and its decision, or a requirement, is named with it; it then says what that source says and adds nothing. Every other sentence is **this record's own** — among them that the step is ended with a `failure`, the kind `operation_answer_invalid`, that it is the tool's own and only the tool function raises it, that a returned value and a raised error are answered alike, that what asks the process to end is not answered, the rule for the message, and every weighing. No sentence is the owner's.

### 1. Scope and terms

- This record is about **a check** — "one call that reaches ⑥" (cited: ADR-0021 decision 1) — of `correctness.check` about one attempt of a run of the first case or of a run whose hw_profile names the mock (cited for the runs: ADR-0021 decision 1; ADR-0038 decision 6), **whose operation gave an answer of neither form**: it returned "a value that is neither a byte string nor one of the two strings of decision 4", or it raised "something that is no tool failure" (cited: ADR-0043 decision 5).
- **Not in scope**, in the words of ADR-0043 decision 5: "a tool failure an operation raises (decision 6 [of ADR-0043]), and the death of the process while the operation runs, after which the step is open" (cited). Decision 8 says what else is not decided.
- **Three ways into the case.** *A returned value*: the operation returned, and what it returned has neither form. *A raised error*: the operation raised something that is no tool failure and that the language of the code counts as an error of the program (observation: in Python, an `Exception`). *A raised request to end*: the operation raised something that is no tool failure and no such error, by which the process is asked to end or is interrupted (observation: in Python, a `BaseException` that is no `Exception`, such as `SystemExit` and `KeyboardInterrupt`). Where the line between the last two runs in code is the code task's.
- It fixes **four points and nothing else**: what the log holds after the call (decision 3); what the call answers (decision 4); whether the three ways are answered alike (decision 5); and what the message of the tool failure may carry (decision 6). Decision 7 says what follows for the mock by citation and by these four.
- The terms of ADR-0011 (identity, start line, end line, open, ended), ADR-0015 (reserved kind), ADR-0016 (backend, operation, script, the moments ① to ⑦), ADR-0021 (a check), ADR-0040 (`execution`, `point`) and ADR-0043 (the check parameters, an answer of an operation, an answer of neither form) are used unchanged.

### 2. What is established when the case arises

Each sentence is cited; nothing is added.

- **The start line of the identity is complete**: a start line is "complete before the step begins to consume the resource" (ADR-0011 decision 2), and the operation is called at ⑥, after the start line of ⑤ (ADR-0040 decision 7).
- **The operation was called and its call is over, and no result exists**: an answer is "what a call of it returned or raised" (ADR-0043 decision 1), and an answer of neither form "is never made into a result" (ADR-0043 decision 5).
- **Nothing is established about the attempt**: such an answer "shows a defect of the backend and establishes nothing about the run of the kernel's process" (ADR-0043 decision 5).
- **The process lives**: its death is not meant (ADR-0043 decision 5, quoted in decision 1).
- **What a step consumed is consumed whether or not it has an outcome**: "A step that ended without an outcome has spent as well" (ADR-0011 decision 7).

### 3. What the log holds after the call

- **After a returned value and after a raised error, the tool function ends the step without an outcome: it appends the end line of the identity, and that line holds a `failure`.** The identity is then ended. The third way is decision 5.
- **The member exists for this.** The `failure` of an end line "holds the `kind` and `message` of the tool failure when the step ended without an outcome" (cited: ADR-0011 decision 3). An end line holds, beside `step`, `execution` and `spend`, "exactly one of `result` and `failure`" (cited, same decision); no result exists (decision 2), so the line holds a `failure`.
- **The `spend` of that line** is what ADR-0040 decision 6 says of every end line of this tool: "the empty object — also on an end line with `failure`", as long as no record names an axis that is accounted through the log (cited). This record adds no rule to it.
- **The candidates were weighed against four tests**: (i) what a caller can tell from the answer alone, and whether that is true in every case the answer is given for; (ii) what a later call with the same identity is given, and whether that differs from what follows the death of a process during a check (cited for the death: ADR-0040 decision 2); (iii) what the log then records for whoever decides later (NFR-2), without saying what that party does; (iv) whether the answer needs anything no landed record has. A result of any kind is no candidate (cited: ADR-0043 decision 5).

| Candidate | (i) The answer | (ii) The same request later | (iii) The log | (iv) Needs |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| (a) Nothing appended; the tool function lets the case pass and the frame answers `tool_crashed` | The reserved meaning is "The tool function raised something that is no tool failure" (cited: ADR-0015 decision 2). For a returned value a tool function that works as its contract says would have to raise such a thing on purpose, so the frame's net for a defect would be used as a designed answer. The kind is answered at other moments of a call as well, so it says neither that the step ran nor which state the identity is in | `step_open` — the answer after the death of a process | The start line only: "may have run; outcome and amount unknown" (cited: ADR-0011 decision 2), although the tool function knows that the call of the operation is over and that no outcome exists | nothing |
| (b) An end line with a `failure` of a kind of this tool's own | Decision 4 | The same tool failure again | The end line: the kind, the message, the spend | one name |
| (c) An end line with a `failure` of a kind the frame reserves for every HW tool | As (b), in every HW tool | As (b) | As (b) | one name, and a sentence about the other three HW tools |
| (d) An end line with a `failure` of `tool_crashed` or `bad_result` | Both are answered by the frame without an end line as well (observation: `hilclaw.tools.call`), so a caller could not tell an ended identity from any other crash; and the tool function would raise a reserved kind "for another situation" (cited: ADR-0015 decision 2) | The same tool failure again | As (b), under a kind that says the tool function crashed | nothing |

- **The choice between the two states is an ended identity.** An open step says that it is not known "Whether the resource was consumed, how much, and whether an outcome existed" (cited: ADR-0011 decision 4). After an answer of neither form the tool function knows more than that: the call of the operation is over and there is no outcome. The state ADR-0011 has for exactly that is ended with a `failure` — "the step is over without an outcome" (cited: ADR-0011 decision 4). An open identity would record less than the tool layer knows, and nothing in the log would tell it from the death of a process.
- **What the choice costs, and what the other would cost.** *Ended*: the identity is used up by an answer of neither form. The step "is not run again under this identity" (cited: ADR-0011 decision 4), a complete line is never edited or removed (cited: ADR-0006 decision 5), and a call under an ended identity "returns what the end line holds" (cited: ADR-0011 decision 3); under the landed records no later line therefore puts an outcome in its place — the room ADR-0011 decision 7 leaves for "An end line appended later for an open identity" does not exist for it. A further check of that attempt at that point would be a new identity with a higher `execution`, and "whether and when that is done" (cited: ADR-0011 decision 3) is OD-10, the retry part of OD-17 and, for this tool, sub-question ⑭. What a backend consumed before it answered is on no line beyond the `spend` the tool function states (above), and an ended identity is not among the open steps, of which ADR-0011 decision 7 says that "the spend view lists the open steps so that whoever compares a total with a budget knows it" (cited). ADR-0016 decision 2 names the cost of a used-up identity for a failure inside a step — it "would end the identity with a `failure`, and the corrected call would need a further `execution`, which nobody may yet grant (retry part of OD-17)" (cited) — and avoided it for the selection by placing it before the start line; here the start line is complete (decision 2). *Open*: the stop ADR-0040 decision 2 accepted for a death during a check ("an open step is a point the run does not pass by itself" — cited there from ADR-0011 Consequences) would follow every answer of neither form as well. **Neither cost is resolved here.** After either state no result exists for that check, and what follows is left to the same places (cited: ADR-0011 decision 4, "What follows a step that ended with a `failure` — a further execution or not — is left to the same places").

### 4. What the call answers: the kind `operation_answer_invalid`

- **The call is a tool failure of the kind `operation_answer_invalid`** — the tool failure its end line holds. A first call "returns what it holds" (cited: ADR-0040 decision 7, the row of ⑦), and a later call with the same identity that the lookup answers is given "the same tool failure again" (cited: ADR-0011 decision 3) and selects no backend, reads no tolerance and draws no seed (cited: ADR-0040 decision 7, the row of ③); no operation is called for it ("no backend is selected or called for it" — cited: ADR-0016 decision 2).
- **The sentence a caller may rely on.** *In the check of this identity the operation was called and its call is over; what it returned or raised was neither a report nor one of the two conditions of ADR-0043 decision 4, and no tool failure. No result was made. The answer is what a complete end line of the identity holds: the identity is ended.* It says nothing about the attempt, about what happened at a target, or about why the answer had neither form.
- **It is this tool's own kind, and this contract raises it in no other situation.** It is raised only as the `failure` of an end line — never before the start line. In that it is the reverse of `tolerance_not_stated`, `op_signature_not_stated` and `op_signature_not_covered`, each of which is never the `failure` of an end line (cited: ADR-0021 decision 4; ADR-0041 decisions 4 and 5).
- **When the append of that end line fails**, the call is `end_line_failed` and not this kind, and the identity is open or ended (cited: ADR-0026 decisions 3 and 4). The sentence above is therefore said of an answer that came from a complete line.
- **Only the tool function raises it.** ADR-0021 decision 5 lists no tool failure a backend may raise ("is not listed" — cited), ADR-0043 and ADR-0044 leave that item to a later slice (cited: their Consequences), and this record names none: `operation_answer_invalid` is not one a backend may raise. A backend that raises it does not answer the contract — "another kind" — and that "is not detected by the frame" (cited: ADR-0016 decision 6). What the tool function does with a tool failure an operation raises, this one included, is not decided (decision 8).
- **Why (b) and not (c).** ADR-0026 decision 3 reserved its kinds because "The situations belong to no tool: they arise in the part every journaled tool shares" (cited; the test is that of ADR-0015 decision 2). This situation is found by one tool's function against the form of one tool's contract: a backend returns "what the tool's contract lets a backend return" (cited: ADR-0016 decision 2 (a)), and the frame does not detect a backend that does not answer a contract (cited: ADR-0016 decision 6). No other HW tool has a form, and ADR-0040 decision 2 "says nothing of whether `sim.run`, `board.run` or `profiler.collect` is journaled" (cited), so a sentence "in every tool" would be written for contracts that do not exist. The argument ADR-0019 decision 2 took from `step_open` — a kind a tool names itself "could also stand in the `failure` of an end line", so that a call that did not start the step "could not be told from a step that ended" (cited) — guards kinds that are answered without a line; this kind is answered from an end line only. ADR-0021 decision 4 chose the tool's own kind for the same reason: "the situation belongs to one tool" (cited).
  - **What (b) costs against (c).** "a later record of the frame may add to the common part as long as no tool's contract uses the name" (cited: ADR-0016 decision 3). A record that later reserves a kind for this situation in every HW tool cannot take this name unchanged: it takes another, or it amends this record.
- **The name** is a *name* of ADR-0001 (`[a-z][a-z0-9_]{0,31}`; 24 characters). Checked on 2026-10-12 in `hilclaw/`, `tests/` and `ARCHITECTURE/`: no reserved kind (ADR-0015 decision 2; ADR-0016 decision 3; ADR-0019 decision 2; ADR-0026 decision 3), no kind of another record (`tolerance_not_stated`, `op_signature_not_stated`, `op_signature_not_covered`), no `step` value, no value of `condition` and no member name equals it, and the string occurred in none of the three trees before this record. The nearest is `operation_answer`, which ADR-0043 names in its Alternatives as an example of a fifth value of `condition` that was not taken; it is no kind, and this record gives it no meaning.

### 5. The three ways into the case

- **A returned value and a raised error are answered alike**: decisions 3 and 4 hold for both.
  - Why alike. What is established is the same for both (decision 2), and ADR-0043 decision 5 defines both as one case. Whether a backend that does not answer the contract returns or raises is how its code is written; apart from the sentence of ADR-0019 decision 4 that the next bullet reads and the two sentences of ADR-0026 that repeat it, the landed records say nothing different of the two, which is where ADR-0019 decision 2 and ADR-0026 decision 3 split a kind (cited: "it splits where the fixed records already say something different of each case" — ADR-0019 decision 2). The message says which of the two it was (decision 6).
  - **A landed sentence that ADR-0043 did not cite.** ADR-0019 decision 4: "**`tool_crashed`** stays what ADR-0015 decision 2 made it, and stays the answer for an exception that is no tool failure and no situation to which decisions 3 and 4 give one of the four kinds: …; such an exception out of the step, which leaves the step open" (cited). For a raised error that sentence already said what a caller is answered where a tool function lets the error come out of its step: `tool_crashed`, and an open identity — candidate (a). **This record reads it as what holds for an exception that comes out of a step, not as a rule that a tool's contract may not end its own step**: by this record the tool function of this tool does not let a raised error of an operation come out of the step, and ends the step with a `failure` (cited for the member: ADR-0011 decision 3). The sentence stands as written for every exception that does come out of the step — a defect in the tool function's own part of ⑥ included (Limits). ADR-0026 gave a kind to a case that had reached a caller as `tool_crashed` (cited: its decision 6, "none of them reaches a caller as `tool_crashed`"); that case ADR-0019 decision 4 had left undecided by name, which it does not do for an exception out of the step, so the reading above rests on the words "out of the step" and not on that precedent.
- **A raised request to end is not answered: the tool function does not turn it into an end line, and it passes through the tool function unchanged.** The identity stays open, and a later call with the same identity is a tool failure that reports the step as open (cited: ADR-0011 decision 3), of the reserved kind `step_open` (cited: ADR-0015 decision 2).
  - Why. Such a thing does not say that the backend answered out of form; it says that the process is asked to end or was interrupted, and what a process does before it ends cannot be relied on to complete. ADR-0026 decision 1 says of its own case that this is something "which no kind answers" (cited), and the same holds here: this record gives it no kind. The log is then as after "the death of the process while the operation runs" (cited: ADR-0043 decision 5), which is the state ADR-0011 has for it.
  - Observation, binding nothing: the tests of `hilclaw.hw_tool` let an operation raise a `SystemExit` or a `KeyboardInterrupt` to stand for "the death of the process while the operation runs", the one death point a script must be able to express (cited: ADR-0016 decision 5 (d)); an end line for it would take that means away.
  - What a front or the process does with what passed through is not this record's.

### 6. What the message carries

- `error.message` is a string, and the `failure` of an end line holds the `kind` and the `message` (cited: ADR-0010 decision 4; ADR-0011 decision 3). No member is added to the error object or to the line. The message of this tool failure is therefore written to `events.ndjson` with the end line, and returned by every later call that the lookup answers from that line.
- **What bounds it** (cited): "this record gives a result and a tool failure no content that holds the seed or that standard error (decision 5), and the tool function writes neither to any file" (ADR-0021 decision 3, "Until it is answered"). A backend "is handed the seed and captures what the process wrote" (ADR-0021 decision 6).
- **The message carries nothing taken from the content of the answer.** No part and no rendering of a returned value; of what was raised, neither its message nor its arguments nor where it was raised. An answer of neither form is an arbitrary value from a backend that holds the seed, and nothing lets the tool function establish that a part of it is free of the seed, of an element of the inputs or of the standard error of a process.
- **What it says.** That the answer of the operation had neither form; which of the two ways it was, a returned value or a raised error; and it may say of which kind the returned value or the raised error was, as the code names kinds of values (observation: in Python, the name of a type). Which of the two ways it was and that kind are all the message may show about the answer.
- The wording is the code's. In the manner of ADR-0026 decision 6, "a caller tells the cases apart by the kind and never by the message" (cited for the manner), and nothing may depend on the wording.
- **Not decided**: whether anything keeps the answer of neither form outside the log. Nothing here says that it is kept (OD-10).

### 7. What follows for the mock and for the tests

- **By citation.** The mock's operation "does not look at what the script answers", and "what the script raised passes through the operation unchanged" (ADR-0044 decision 2); "a script of a test can also give an answer of neither form" (ADR-0044 decision 5). For check parameters it cannot compute a report for, what the default script does "is the code task's", and "If it raises something that is no tool failure, that is an answer of neither form" (ADR-0044 decision 3).
- **With decisions 3 to 5**: an answer of neither form that a script gives is answered by the way it came — a returned value or a raised error by an end line with `operation_answer_invalid`, a raised request to end by an open identity. This holds for a script of a test and for the default script alike; this record adds nothing to what the default script does.
- **The list of ADR-0021 decision 2 is unchanged.** It names what "a script must be able to express": "each thing an operation may return" under the contract (cited). An answer of neither form is nothing the contract lets an operation give, so it is no further item of that list. A conformance test can exercise it all the same, because the mock passes the answer of a script on unseen.

### 8. What is not decided by this

- **What anybody does after the answer**: a further `execution`, who may ask for one and when, a retry, waiting, giving up, a triage, a quarantine of a backend, notifying a person (OD-10; the retry part of OD-17; OD-18 sub-question ⑭). Nothing in this record says what a caller does on `operation_answer_invalid`.
- **The tool failures of a backend**: which kinds a backend may raise, and what the tool function does with a tool failure an operation raises — whether its step is ended with it, under which kind, with which message. That "belongs to the slice that names those kinds" (cited: ADR-0043 decision 6). What the landed records carry for it is cited and not added to: the `failure` of an end line holds the `kind` and `message` of a tool failure (ADR-0011 decision 3); "Which tool failures can stand as the `failure` of an end line of this tool — those a backend may raise — is a later slice's" (ADR-0040 decision 7); a backend "adds no result member and no failure kind of its own" (ADR-0016 decision 2 (b)); "Which tool failure a backend may raise when it cannot carry out the experiment at all … is not listed" (ADR-0021 decision 5). The third clause of sub-question ⑩ stays open.
- **The spend**: whether an elapsed time or any amount is stated on such an end line beyond ADR-0040 decision 6.
- **What is kept of the answer outside the log** (decision 6); sub-question ⑪, which is about a failed end line.
- **Fault injection in a live session** (sub-question ⑧), **class (iii) and a target on another machine** (sub-question ⑩, first clause; #5 B-2), and **the other HW tools**: whether a tool function of `sim.run`, `board.run` or `profiler.collect` establishes a form at all, and what it answers.

### 9. The three ways, side by side

| The way | The log after the call | The call answers | The same request afterwards | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A returned value of neither form | The start line and an end line with the `failure` `operation_answer_invalid`, its message and the `spend` of ADR-0040 decision 6; the identity is ended | Tool failure `operation_answer_invalid` | The same tool failure again; no operation is called and nothing is appended | Decisions 3 and 4; the repeated call: ADR-0011 decision 3 |

- In the first two rows the end line can fail to be appended; the call is then `end_line_failed` (decision 4).

### Walk-through

The names of ADR-0040 to ADR-0044: the run `r1`, the attempt `round` 3, `candidate` 2, `attempt` 1, and "the request" `{"run_id": "r1", "round": 3, "candidate": 2, "attempt": 1, "execution": 1, "point": "sim", …}`. The hw_profile of `r1` names the mock, and `runs/r1/instance.json` states a covered op_signature and a tolerance. Each row starts from an identity with no line. In the fourth row the mock is bound to the default script; in every other row in which a script is named, a test built the registry and bound the mock to its own script. The sixth, seventh and eighth rows are for contrast and are cited; the last row is not decided. **No value of `draws`, no seed and no tolerance value is shown.**

### 10. Limits

- **The kind says that the form was wrong, and nothing about the attempt or about what happened at a target**: a backend can have carried the kernel out, and consumed what that consumes, before it answered out of form. The `spend` of the line does not show it (decision 3).
- **An answer of the right form from a backend that did not do what the contract says is taken as it is**: "A wrong answer of the right form is taken as it is" (cited: ADR-0043 decision 8). This record changes nothing of it.
- **A defect of the tool function is not told from a defect of the backend by the answer.** A tool function that tells the forms wrongly ends a step with `operation_answer_invalid` although the operation answered the contract; the sentence of decision 4 holds for a tool function that works as its contract says. A defect in the tool function's own part of ⑥ that comes out of the step as an exception is `tool_crashed`, and the identity stays open (cited: ADR-0019 decision 4). Where the code draws the line between what an operation raised and what the tool function's own code raised is the code task's, and an error on that line can fall on either side.
- **Until a rule says what follows, the run does not get past such a check by itself.** After an end line with this `failure` no result exists for the identity, and "only a pass counts" (cited: ADR-0021 decision 5); what follows is not decided (decision 8). An identity that was ended for a cause that would not occur again — a size the front process could not hold at that moment — stays ended, and nothing in the answer tells such a cause from a lasting one.
- **Nothing in this record keeps the answer of neither form after the call** (decision 6). Whoever looks for the cause has the kind and the message, and by decision 6 the message shows at most which of the two ways it was and of which kind the answer was.
- **The message is in the log for good** (cited: ADR-0006 decision 5, a complete line is never edited or removed). A tool function whose message breaks decision 6 is not detected by anything here. The kind of a value is named by the backend's code: a backend that builds that name from the seed is the dishonest backend the cut does not keep out (cited: ADR-0021 decision 6).
- **A raised request to end leaves the identity open** although the operation's call is over, as a death does; the log does not tell the two apart.
- The limits of ADR-0011 decision 9, ADR-0016 decision 6, ADR-0021 decision 6, ADR-0026 decision 7, ADR-0040 decision 8, ADR-0043 decision 8 and ADR-0044 decision 7 stand unchanged.

## Alternatives considered

| Point | Alternative | Why not |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | (a) Nothing appended; the frame answers `tool_crashed` | See decision 3: the log would record less than the tool function knows, nothing would tell the case from a death, and for a returned value the frame's net for a defect would be a designed answer. |
| 3 | (e) Nothing appended, and a kind of the tool's own | See decision 3: the cause would be answered once and be in no file. |
| 3 | A broken result with a fifth condition, or with `"report_form"` | Not weighed again: "an answer of neither form is never made into a result" (ADR-0043 decision 5), and `condition` has "exactly four values" (ADR-0042 decision 3). |
| 4 | (c) A kind the frame reserves for every HW tool | See decision 4: the situation is found against the form of one tool's contract, and no other HW tool has one. |
| 4 | (d) `tool_crashed` or `bad_result` as the `failure` of the end line | See decision 3: both are answered without an end line as well, and the tool function would raise a reserved kind for another situation. |
| 4 | Two kinds, one for a returned value and one for a raised error | See decision 5: apart from ADR-0019 decision 4 as that decision reads it, and the two sentences of ADR-0026 that repeat it, the records say nothing different of the two; the message says which it was. |
| 5 | A raised error stays `tool_crashed` with an open identity (ADR-0019 decision 4 as it applies today), and only a returned value is given the kind | One case of ADR-0043 decision 5 would leave the log in two states by how a backend's code is written, and a script that raises would stop the work on the attempt as a death does. |
| 5 | A raised request to end is ended with an end line as well | See decision 5: no kind answers it, the append would be made by a process that is asked to end, and the tests would lose their means to stand for a death. |
| 6 | The message shows the returned value or the text of the error, shortened | A shortened part of an arbitrary value is still a part of it; ADR-0021 decision 3 keeps the seed and the standard error out of a tool failure and out of every file the tool function writes. |
| — | Decide it together with the tool-failure kinds of a backend | That slice needs OD-10 and, for a real target, #5 B-2. This case needs neither: it arises with the mock and in the local configuration, and the tool function cannot be written without an answer to it. |
| — | Leave it to the code task | The code would choose the state of the log and a value of `error.kind`, which is what sub-question ⑮ registered as open. |

## Consequences

- **What the next slices of the second record must still add** — the list of ADR-0044 Consequences, without the item that record struck off itself: every other member of the request, the input schema and the description; where `draws` comes from; where the kernel of the attempt, the flags and the time limits come from, and with them what class (iii) adds to the form of ADR-0043; the tool-failure kinds a backend may raise (ADR-0043 decision 6 puts with that slice what the tool function does with a tool failure an operation raises); the spend beyond ADR-0040 decision 6. Of OD-18 sub-question ⑩ the first three clauses, the kernel language and another accelerator; ~~sub-question ⑮~~ (this record).
- **Code** (observations; **this record binds no code**). The tool function of `correctness.check` can now say what its step does with `hilclaw.check_answer.NeitherFormError` and with an error an operation raised: the step of `hilclaw.journaled_step` ends without an outcome when it raises `StepFailed` with amounts, a kind and a message, so `hilclaw.journaled_step` needs no change by this record, and neither does `hilclaw.tools`: the kind is not reserved, and `RESERVED_KINDS` stays as it is. The message of `NeitherFormError` shows a returned string shortened; by decision 6 it cannot be used as the message of the `failure` as it is. **Not yet possible**: the tool function and its registration, which wait on the rest of the second record.
- **Not edited and now less exact** (follow-ups for the integrator; the list holds what this record found): ADR-0043 decision 5 ("What the call then answers is not decided", "No landed sentence gives this tool a tool-failure kind for the case, and this record gives it none, new or existing", and the sentence that calls the choice "a question of recovery"), the last row but one of its Walk-through ("What the call answers: sub-question ⑮"), the two rows of point 5 of its Alternatives on a new failure kind and on `tool_crashed`, the item ⑮ of its Consequences and its Boundaries; ADR-0044 decision 3 (last bullet: "what a call then answers is sub-question ⑮"), decision 5 ("What the call then answers is sub-question ⑮"), the row of `"pass"` of its Walk-through, decision 7 ("where it is an answer of neither form, sub-question ⑮"), the item ⑮ of its Consequences and its Boundaries; ADR-0040 decision 7 ("those a backend may raise"); ADR-0019 decision 4 ("such an exception out of the step, which leaves the step open" — for this tool a raised error of an operation no longer comes out of the step) and the two sentences of ADR-0026 that repeat it (decision 1, "an exception out of the step"; decision 6, "An `OSError` out of the step leaves the step open"); ADR-0021 decision 5 ("Tool failures of this contract so far"). In `hilclaw/check_answer.py` the docstring sentences that say the case has no tool-failure kind and that what follows it is open stay true of that module, while a record now names the kind. No sentence of UsageCases became false.
- **A-13 reading**: document mode; no code, dependency, settings file or schema file changes. **This record gives one value of `error.kind` a meaning and a place: the `kind` of the `failure` of an end line of this tool.** That member exists: ADR-0011 decision 3 defined it for the tool failure of a step that ended without an outcome, it holds a *name*, and no record lists its values ("this record fixes the form and **no list of kinds**" — ADR-0010 decision 4); ADR-0040 decision 7 foresaw that a later slice says which tool failures stand there. No member of `STATE.json`, `instance.json`, a spec, `artifacts.json`, a result file or a line of `events.ndjson` is added, removed or changed, and no rule by which a line is valid changes. **This is therefore read as no "데이터 plane 스키마 변경".** One difference from the earlier records is stated openly: the kinds that ADR-0019, ADR-0021, ADR-0026 and ADR-0041 named are never the `failure` of an end line, and this is the first kind a record names for that place. If the owner reads A-13 otherwise, that is an objection to this whole record.
