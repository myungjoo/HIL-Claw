## Decision

### 1. Scope and terms

- This record is about **a call of `correctness.check` about one attempt of a run that ADR-0021 and ADR-0038 decision 6 cover**: a run of the first case — `cpu_standin`, kind `gemm`, fp32, a C kernel (cited: ADR-0021 decision 1) — and a run whose hw_profile names the mock (cited: ADR-0038 decision 6, which renders the owner's answer HQ-0005 (4)).
- Of the contract of that call it fixes **five points and nothing else**: that the tool is journaled (decision 2); its `step` values and the meaning of `execution` (decision 3); the member that states the execution point (decision 4); the run and identity members a request requires (decision 5); and the `spend` of an end line as far as the fixed inputs carry it (decision 6 — the fifth point is stated from the cited sentences only and is no rule of this record). Decision 7 puts the order of a call together from the cited records and adds no rule.
- **The five points rest on nothing that only one backend has.** The journaled path, the check of the request and the identity are the tool function's part, which is the same for every backend (cited: ADR-0016 decision 2 (a)). **Where a point is nevertheless limited to these runs**: the weighing of decision 2 where it speaks of what a check costs at the target, which is known for the local configuration and for the mock only; and decision 5, which rests on the scope of ADR-0021 ("a call of `correctness.check` about one attempt").
- The terms of ADR-0011 (journaled step, identity, start line, end line, open, ended), ADR-0015 (journaled tool, reserved kind), ADR-0016 (backend, operation, target, the moments ① to ⑦), ADR-0030 (the moment ⓢ), ADR-0021 (a check — "one call that reaches ⑥") and ADR-0038 (execution point, sim point, board point, the local configuration) are used unchanged.

### 2. The tool is journaled

- **`correctness.check` is a journaled tool: one call that reaches the operation is one journaled step** (the term is cited: ADR-0015 decision 1, "a tool one call of which is one journaled step"). That this is for the tool's contract to say is cited above (Context).
- **The definition.** A journaled step is "one execution of a step whose repetition costs a limited resource, or whose outcome must survive the death of the process that obtained it" (cited: ADR-0011 decision 1). The two halves are weighed apart.
- **The first half, a limited resource.** At the board point of a target that is a board, a check carries the kernel out at the board (cited for the operation's part: ADR-0021 decision 2), and a board is a shared, limited resource (cited: C-4); so the first half holds there. In the local configuration a check consumes time of the host, and a check at the mock consumes nothing at any target. Wall-clock time is an axis of FR-3 for which a run may state a budget (cited: FR-3 ③; [ADR-0033](ADR-0033-od8-instance-record-wall-clock-budget.md) decisions 2 and 3), but whether an elapsed time is accounted through the log at all is open (cited: ADR-0011 decision 7); **for the runs this record is about, the fixed inputs do not let one say that the first half holds.**
- **One contract for every backend.** The journaled path is the tool function's part, "the same for every backend", and "a caller need not know which backend answered" (both cited: ADR-0016 decision 2 (a) and (b)). Whether the tool is journaled shows in the contract a caller sees — the member `execution`, what a repeated call answers, the answer `step_open` — so the tool cannot be journaled at one backend and not at another. This record is about no board, but its answer is taken with the board in view: an unjournaled contract would have to be replaced, request and answers alike, by the record of the first backend whose target is a board.
- **The second half, an outcome that must survive.** The outcome of a check is what the hard floor of FR-9 rests on, and FR-1 has the caller act on it — repair, elimination, promotion (cited: FR-9, FR-1). "The outcome of a call is not a function of its request: the same request is judged on other inputs at each check", and "Two checks of one attempt can disagree by their inputs alone" (both cited: ADR-0021 decision 6). An outcome lost with its process can therefore not be obtained again; it can only be replaced by the outcome of another check. **This half holds for the runs this record is about**, and it is the half that carries the choice for them:
  - *Not journaled.* A turn that dies after a check and before it recorded what it learned leaves nothing of that check. The resumed turn (NFR-1) checks again, on other inputs, and may be given another verdict than the one the dead turn saw and perhaps began to act on. Nothing shows that the first check took place.
  - *Journaled.* The resumed turn presents the same identity and is given what the end line holds, the same verdict (cited: ADR-0011 decisions 3 and 4). Every check that reached a complete start line stays in the log under its identity, one that failed included (cited: ADR-0006 decision 5, a complete line is never edited or removed), so that it can be traced which checks of an attempt were made and what each established (NFR-2).
- **What a caller may conclude from a pass.** *Stored under an identity*: that one check — at that point, of that attempt, on the inputs of one drawn seed, with the tolerance the file stated at that call — passed, and that a later call that presents that identity and is answered by the lookup is given this outcome (when a call is answered before the lookup, or by a situation the lookup finds: decision 7, the rows of ①, ②, ⓢ and ③). Not more: decision 8. *Not stored* (the other answer): that one call returned a pass; whether other calls about the same attempt returned a fail would be recorded nowhere by the tool layer.
- **The cost of the choice: a death during a check.** With a journaled tool the identity is then open, what the resumed turn may do is "Neither skip nor run it under this identity" (cited: ADR-0011 decision 4), and "Until a recovery rule exists (OD-10, the retry part of OD-17), an open step is a point the run does not pass by itself" (cited: ADR-0011 Consequences). In the local configuration a check that would cost seconds to repeat thus stops the work on that attempt until somebody decides. With an unjournaled tool the resumed turn would simply call again. **This is the reason for the opposite answer.** It is weighed so: the stop is visible and loses nothing; it ends with the recovery rule that OD-10 and the retry part of OD-17 must give for every journaled tool; and the alternative is a second check that nobody decided and nothing records.
- **What an end line stores.** The `result` of an end line "is the result object of the call itself" (cited: ADR-0011 decision 3), so whatever a later slice makes a member of a result is written to `events.ndjson`. What a result may hold is already bound: never an element of the inputs or of the reference, and — until the question of ADR-0021 decision 3 is answered — not the seed, the command line or the standard error of the kernel's process (cited: ADR-0021 decision 5). The `failure` of an end line holds the `kind` and `message` of a tool failure (cited: ADR-0011 decision 3); of the content of a tool failure ADR-0021 says this much and no more: "this record gives a result and a tool failure no content that holds the seed or that standard error" (cited: ADR-0021 decision 3). What else a tool failure of a backend may hold belongs to the slice that names those kinds. Beside `step`, `execution` and `spend`, an end line holds exactly one of the two (cited: ADR-0011 decision 3), so journaling the tool writes nothing to the log that a result or a tool failure may not hold.
- **What the earlier records wrote "if the tool is journaled"** agrees with this answer and now holds without the condition: a start line holds no seed (ADR-0021 decision 3); `tolerance_not_stated` is answered before the start line and never as the `failure` of an end line (ADR-0021 decision 4); a script of the mock must be able to express the death of the process while the operation runs (ADR-0021 decision 2); a repeated call is answered from the log, reads no `instance.json` for the selection and draws no seed (ADR-0021 Walk-through); the two checks of one attempt are two identities that differ in `step` (ADR-0038 decision 4). All cited; none is changed.
- **How far the fixed inputs carry the answer, and what is provisional.** For a backend whose target is a board, the definition of ADR-0011 decision 1 with C-4 carries it. For the runs this record is about, only the second half and the one-contract argument carry it, and setting them against the stop of an open check is this record's own judgement: **the answer is provisional for the local configuration and the mock in this sense** — it is looked at again when the recovery of an open step is decided (OD-10, the retry part of OD-17), if that decision leaves an open check of the local configuration as something only a person can resolve. It is not left open here, because the next slices and the code are cut by it.
- This record says nothing of whether `sim.run`, `board.run` or `profiler.collect` is journaled.

### 3. The two `step` values and the meaning of `execution`

- A journaled contract of this tool "has two `step` values, one for each point, and the `step` of a call follows from the point its request states" (cited: ADR-0038 decision 4). **The values are `correctness_check_sim` for the check at the sim point and `correctness_check_board` for the check at the board point.**
- Each is a *name* of ADR-0001 (`[a-z][a-z0-9_]{0,31}`; 21 and 23 characters). They were formed from the name of the tool with its `.` replaced by `_`, then `_` and the value of the member of decision 4; a dotted spelling is no *name*. This says how these two values were formed and is no rule for the `step` values of another tool.
- **No other tool uses either value** (the rule is cited: ADR-0015 decision 4, "Two tools of the registry do not share a `step` value"). Checked on 2026-10-12: no record from ADR-0001 to ADR-0039 lists a `step` value of a tool (ADR-0011, ADR-0012 and ADR-0015 use the placeholder `example_step`); no module of `hilclaw/` binds a `step` value, and the two registered tools are not journaled (ADR-0039 decision 5); the tests use placeholders of their own. The string `correctness_check` occurs elsewhere only as the end of the MCP name `mcp__hilclaw__correctness_check` — in ADR-0010 decision 4, in the passage of OpenDecisionProposals that renders it, and in a test of the MCP front: that is a name of the tool, not a `step` value, and neither value equals it.
- **The request still carries no `step`** (cited: ADR-0015 decision 4, as ADR-0038 decision 4 reads it): the tool function takes the `step` of a call from the checked value of the member of decision 4.
- **The meaning of `execution` for this tool.** `execution` "counts the executions of that step for that target" (cited: ADR-0011 decision 3): here, the checks of one attempt at one execution point. The first check of an attempt at a point is stated with 1 (cited, same decision: "it is 1 unless a decision outside this record asked for a further execution"). The checks at the two points are counted apart, since their `step` differs.
- **A further `execution` is a new check.** It is a new identity (cited: ADR-0011 decision 3, "Running the step again is a new identity with a higher `execution`"), so it is carried out as any call whose identity has no line: the tolerance is read again and the seed is drawn anew (cited: ADR-0021 decisions 3 and 4), and its outcome can differ from that of the earlier check by its inputs alone (cited: ADR-0021 decision 6). The earlier check stays ended in the log with its outcome.
- **A repeated call under an ended identity makes no new check**: it is answered with what the end line holds, reads no tolerance and draws no seed; under an open identity it is the tool failure `step_open` (cited: ADR-0011 decision 3; ADR-0015 decision 2; ADR-0021 Walk-through).
- **Who may ask for a further `execution`, and when, stays where ADR-0011 left it**: "whether and when that is done is not this record's (OD-10, the retry part of OD-17)" (cited: ADR-0011 decision 3). This record gives no rule, and a caller that raises `execution` by itself is not stopped (decision 8).
- **Not a further `execution`** (both cited): the check at the board point after the check at the sim point, which is another `step` (ADR-0038 decision 4); and a check of a repaired attempt, which is a check of another attempt (ADR-0038 Walk-through), whose envelope numbers differ in `attempt` (ADR-0002 decision 5: a repair call has "`attempt` raised by 1").

### 4. The member that states the execution point

- **Owner** (HQ-0005 (3)): "How a call states its execution point is the record's design." ADR-0038 decision 4 used that room for the form, which is cited here and not re-decided: the member is required and has no default, it has exactly two values, and "A request without it, or with another value, is a refused request" — `invalid_request` (ADR-0015 decision 2).
- **The member is named `point`, and its two values are the strings `"sim"`, for the sim point, and `"board"`, for the board point.** It stands at the top level of the request, like the five members of ADR-0015 decision 4.
- **Why these spellings.** `point` is the word of the term ADR-0038 decision 1 defines. `sim` and `board` are the spellings a spec already uses for these two places of the loop: the `stage` of a failure there "is a name: `compile`, `sim` or `board`, the three points where FR-1 lets a candidate fail" (cited: [ADR-0002](ADR-0002-od6-codegen-spec.md) decision 2), so a request for a check and an entry that reports a failure name the place with one word. The member is not named `stage`, because that member has a third value and says where an attempt failed, not at which point a check is asked for.
- **The value is compared as it is, and nothing is normalised.** The comparison is case-sensitive: `"Sim"`, `"BOARD"`, `" sim"`, `"sim.run"` and a value that is no string (`true`, `0`, `null`) are each "another value" and are refused — the manner in which ADR-0039 decision 3 treats its member `sha256` (cited for the manner only).
- **The name collides with no other.** Checked on 2026-10-12: `point` is none of the five member names of ADR-0015 decision 4 (`run_id`, `round`, `candidate`, `attempt`, `execution`); no member of the envelope of a line (`schema_version`, `time`, `kind`, `round`, `candidate`, `attempt`, `data` — ADR-0006 decision 3); no member of the `data` of a step line (`step`, `execution`, `spend`, `result`, `failure` — ADR-0011 decision 3); not the request member `sha256` of ADR-0039; and no record, no module of `hilclaw/` and no test gives a request, a result or a file a member of that name.
- The member carries a point and nothing from which an input follows (cited: ADR-0038 decision 5). **Whether an operation is handed the point is not decided** (cited: ADR-0038 decision 4).

### 5. The run and identity members of a request

- **A request of this tool requires six members: `run_id`, `round`, `candidate`, `attempt`, `execution` and `point`.** The first five have the meaning and the form of ADR-0015 decision 4 (cited, not re-decided): `run_id` is "The run the call is about: a directory name of `runs/`"; `round`, `candidate` and `attempt` say what the call is about "as the envelope numbers of a line"; `execution` is "an *integer* ≥ 1", "required and has no default".
- **All three numbers are required.** That is for this contract to say: "which numbers a tool requires is its contract" (cited: ADR-0015 decision 4). A check is about one attempt (cited: ADR-0021 decision 1), and an attempt is named by all three: "`candidate` only with `round`, `attempt` only with `candidate`" (cited: ADR-0015 decision 4).
- **This contract has no check about a candidate, a round or a run as a whole.** What is checked is the kernel of one attempt (cited: ADR-0021 decision 2, "the verification executable of the attempt"), and no record describes a check of anything else. A request that leaves `attempt` out is therefore a refused request, like one with an invalid run or identity (cited: ADR-0015 decision 4).
- The form of `round` admits 0, the bring-up round (cited: ADR-0006 decision 3). This record does not say which attempts are checked, at which point or in which order: that is FR-1's and the loop's (PLAN P2-1).
- A request in which one of the six is missing or ill-formed is refused as `invalid_request` in ①, before the data root is asked and before anything is read (cited: ADR-0015 decisions 2 and 4; ADR-0038 decision 4).
- **The member list of a request is not closed by this record.** Whether a request carries anything else — for `draws`, the dimensions, the kernel of the attempt, flags or time limits — is a later slice's; so are the input schema and the description, and so is what the tool function does with a member that no record names. What ADR-0021 decisions 3 and 4 keep out of a request — a seed, a tolerance — stays out.

### 6. The `spend` of an end line

- `spend` "states every amount the step consumed of the axes that are accounted through the log" and is "possibly empty" (cited: ADR-0011 decision 7).
- **No record names an axis that is accounted through the log.** ADR-0011 "names no axis, no unit and no party that measures" (its decision 1) and says "Whether a given axis is accounted through the log at all is not decided here" (its decision 7). Two records gave an axis of FR-3 a place in the file of a run — [ADR-0032](ADR-0032-od8-instance-record-round-budget.md) the rounds (`round_budget`) and ADR-0033 the wall-clock time (`wall_clock_budget_s`) — and each says of itself, in its decision 3, that it "names no axis of the spend view" and does not say whether its quantity is such an axis. All cited. Which other axes exist is FR-3's, OD-2's and OD-19's.
- **As long as that is so, the `spend` of an end line of this tool is the empty object** — also on an end line with `failure`, which "carries `spend` like any other" (cited: ADR-0011 decision 7). This follows from the cited sentences and is no rule of this record.
- **This record names no axis and no amount**, and it does not say whether the rounds or the time a check takes are accounted through the log. Which axes a check consumes, in which amounts, and from where the tool function has an amount, belong to the record that defines an axis of the log and to the slice of this contract that follows it.

Which tool failures can stand as the `failure` of an end line of this tool — those a backend may raise — is a later slice's.

| A request without `attempt` | Refused in ① | `invalid_request` |
| The first request while `instance.json` of `r1` states no tolerance | ④: the backend is selected, no tolerance is found; no seed, no line | Tool failure `tolerance_not_stated`; the identity still has no line |
| Another run, whose hw_profile names the mock; either point | The same path and the same identities; the script stands in for the kernel (ADR-0038 decision 6) | As above |

### 8. Limits

- **A stored pass says nothing about other inputs** (cited: ADR-0021 decision 6) — and nothing about a tolerance the file states later: a repeated call reads no tolerance, and "a file changed between two checks gives them two tolerances" (cited: ADR-0021 decisions 4 and 6).
- **A caller can re-check until a pass is stored.** A caller that raises `execution` by itself is given a new check on new inputs each time, and nothing stops it (cited: ADR-0011 decision 9; ADR-0015 decision 7). A kernel that is right for some inputs and wrong for others (ADR-0021 decision 6) then passes at some `execution`. This record does not stop it and does not say which of several ended checks of one attempt at one point counts. **Said plainly: this is a hole in the hard floor of FR-9.** Journaling makes it visible — the failed checks stay in the log — and does not close it. It is for the retry part of OD-17 (who may ask for a further execution) and the rest of OD-18 (what checks it) to close, and the part that is particular to this tool is registered as sub-question ⑭.
- **The point a call states is believed** (cited: ADR-0038 decision 8). Nothing checks that both points of an attempt are checked, or in which order.
- **An open check stops the work on its attempt** until a recovery rule exists (decision 2), also where repeating it would cost seconds.
- **A death during the judging** leaves the step open although the target has finished its part; the outcome is lost. No form closes the window between an outcome and its end line (cited: ADR-0011 decision 2), and the judging of this tool, whose Python-side work is bounded by nothing (cited: ADR-0021 decision 2), lies in it.
- **A check about an attempt of another round than the stored one.** For a round before the stored one the lookup needs lines before the stored position (cited: ADR-0011 decision 6; OD-17 sub-question ①); a round above the stored one is not refused (cited: ADR-0015 decisions 4 and 7). This record adds no rule for either. Whether the re-verification FR-9 asks for before a target is counted as reached is a further `execution` of one of the two steps, or something a later record defines, is not decided (OD-16 sub-question ⑤).
- **A wrong identity**: a caller that states the numbers of another attempt records the check under them (cited: ADR-0015 decision 7). Which kernel is then checked depends on where the kernel of the attempt comes from, which is a later slice's.
- **Two calls at once** can both find no line (cited: ADR-0015 decision 7; ADR-0011 decision 9 for two turns).
- The limits of ADR-0021 decision 6 and ADR-0038 decision 8 stand unchanged.

## Alternatives considered

| Point | Alternative | Why not |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | The tool is not journaled | See decision 2: a verdict lost with its process cannot be obtained again, only replaced by the verdict of another check; the tool layer would record no check; and the contract would have to be replaced for the first board. What speaks for it — no stop after a death during a cheap check — is weighed there. |
| 2 | Journaled only at a backend whose target is a board | The journaled path is the tool function's part and the same for every backend (ADR-0016 decision 2 (a)); the request and the answers would differ by backend, which a caller need not know (ADR-0016 decision 2 (b)). |
| 2 | Leave it open until the recovery of an open step is decided | The result members, the script cases of the mock and the builder of the code are cut by it; every later slice would carry both answers. |
| 3 | One `step` value, the point told another way | The rows of point 4 in the Alternatives of ADR-0038 (`execution` as the carrier; nothing stated; one call for both points; two tools; derivation from the data plane; `step` in the request). |
| 3 | Values without the name of the tool (`sim_check`, `board_check`) | Each tool's contract chooses its `step` values, and those of the run tools are not chosen; a value that begins with the name of its tool cannot meet one of them. |
| 4 | A boolean member (for example `board`: `true` or `false`) | An absent boolean reads as `false`, a default in all but name, where the member has none (ADR-0038 decision 4); and a request would not show its point to a reader who does not know the convention. |
| 4 | The values `"sim.run"` and `"board.run"` | They are the names of two other tools. A check is no call of a run tool, and whether a check follows a call of the run tool of its point is not decided; such a value would suggest a tie to that tool's step or result that no record defines. |
| 4 | The name `stage`, as in a spec | See decision 4: three values there, and another meaning. |
| 4 | The name `execution_point` | Beside `execution` it reads as a part of that member, which it is not; `point` is the term's own word. |
| 4 | Accept other cases or surrounding white space | One point would have several spellings in requests, and the `step` — a part of the identity — follows from it; ADR-0015 decision 4 keeps `execution` without a default for the same reason. |
| 5 | `attempt` optional, for a check about a candidate | No record describes such a check (ADR-0021 decision 1); the identity of a check of an attempt would have two forms. |
| 5 | Close the member list here and refuse every other member | The members for `draws`, the dimensions and the kernel are a later slice's; a closed list would be reopened by it. |
| 6 | Name an axis here (for example a count of checks) | No record names an axis that is accounted through the log (decision 6); which axes exist is FR-3's, OD-2's and OD-19's. |

## Consequences

- **What the next slices of the second record must still add** — the rest of the list of ADR-0038 decision 7: every other member of the request, the input schema and the description; where `draws`, the dimensions, the kernel of the attempt, the flags and the time limits come from; the members of the result and the tool-failure kinds a backend may raise; the spend beyond decision 6; the operation and the default script of the mock; and, from ADR-0038 decision 4, whether an operation is handed the point. With them the first three clauses of OD-18 sub-question ⑩.
- **Observations for those slices** (not decisions). Whatever a result holds is stored in a line of `events.ndjson` and is returned again by every repeated call; ADR-0006 decision 7 asks of such a line that the result file can be written from it. The script cases of ADR-0021 decision 2 now include the death of the process while the operation runs.
- **Code slices that can now be cut** (code mode, later tasks): a builder for a journaled HW tool whose `step` follows from the checked request, with the check of the six members of decision 5 and with a place between the selection and the start line for the tolerance and the answer `tolerance_not_stated` (ADR-0021 Consequences), tested with a test-only step. **`hilclaw.hw_tool.tool_function` takes one `step`**: whether the tool becomes two bound functions behind one name or a builder that takes the `step` from the checked request is the code task's and is not decided here. **Not yet possible**: the tool function of `correctness.check` and its registration, the operation of the mock and its script cases, the default script, the operation of `cpu_standin`, and the conformance tests of P1-1 — each needs a later slice. PLAN P1-1 stays open.
- **Docstring sentences that become less exact** (not edited): in `hilclaw/journaled_tool.py`, "`step`: the `step` name the tool's contract fixes", to which `hilclaw/hw_tool.py` refers for its own `step` — the contract of this tool fixes two. None becomes false: "`step` is the contract's and is never read from a request" stays true, since the request states a point and no `step`.
- **Not edited and now less exact** (follow-ups for the integrator): ADR-0011 decision 1 ("The sources name one such step, the board measurement … which other steps are journaled is not decided here") and ADR-0015 decision 5 ("the sources name the board measurement only") — a record now names one more; ADR-0021 decision 1 (the first item of the list a second record must add) and its Boundaries ("whether the tool is journaled; its `step` value or values; the meaning of `execution` for it"), its sentences under the condition "if the tool is journaled", and "a repeated call if the tool is not journaled" in its decision 6; ADR-0038 decision 4 ("Whether the tool is journaled is not decided here"; "this record names neither"), its decision 7, the row of its Walk-through that begins its last sentence with "If the tool is journaled", and its Alternatives row "Decide here that the tool is journaled". No sentence of UsageCases became false.
- **A-13 reading**: document mode; no code, dependency, settings file or schema file changes. The record names one member of a request with its two values, and two `step` values. A request is the interface between layer 1 and layer 2 (ADR-0010 and ADR-0015 Consequences). The `step` values are values of the member `step` in the `data` of the two kinds of line ADR-0011 defined; that record fixed the member and its form and left the values to each tool ("this record lists no value, and the name of the board measurement belongs to its tool contract", its decision 3). **This is no "데이터 plane 스키마 변경"**: no member of `STATE.json`, `instance.json`, a spec, a result file, `artifacts.json` or a line of `events.ndjson` is added, removed or changed in type, form or meaning; the version of a line "is not raised by a new value of `kind`, nor by a change in the `data` of a kind" (ADR-0006 decision 8), and here not even the `data` of a kind changes; and no schema file of the repository lists `step` values.

## Boundaries (not decided here)

- **The rest of the request and the whole result**: every other request member, the input schema, the description, what is done with a member no record names; every member, name and type of a result; whether an operation is handed the point.
- **Where the other parameters come from**: `draws` and whether a seed is recorded after a check (OD-16 sub-question ⑤); the dimensions; the kernel of the attempt and who builds it (OD-23; the first three clauses of OD-18 sub-question ⑩); flags; time limits (OD-14).
- **Backends**: the tool-failure kinds a backend may raise; the operation and the default script of the mock, and the mock in a live session (OD-18 sub-question ⑧); the operation of `cpu_standin`; a real simulator or board and how a backend is reached (#5 B-2, B-3 — owner-only).
- **The budget**: which axes exist, their units and who measures; the amounts of a check; enforcement (FR-3, OD-2, OD-19).
- **What follows an outcome or an open step**: recovery of an open step, retry, who may ask for a further `execution` and what checks it (OD-10, the rest of OD-17); repair, elimination, triage and promotion (FR-1, OD-15, OD-19, OD-29); the Round-Manager (PLAN P2-1); hooks and gates (the rest of OD-18; OD-3).
- **Other tools and targets**: `sim.run`, `board.run` and `profiler.collect`, among it whether each is journaled and its `step` values; a target that lacks a point (OD-26); the configuration of OD-18 sub-question ⑫.
- **Files and names**: any member of `instance.json`, `STATE.json`, `events.ndjson`, a spec or a result file; any event kind; any new reserved `kind`; any tool-failure kind of this tool beyond `tolerance_not_stated`.
- **Owner-only (DRIVER §9)**: the #5 A and B items; OD-3; OD-25; C-6.
- **Sub-question this record registers** (under OD-18): ⑭ For `correctness.check`: when the log holds more than one ended check of one attempt at one execution point — several `execution` values — which of them counts for the hard floor of FR-9, and what keeps a caller from asking for further executions until one passes (with the retry part of OD-17, OD-10, the hooks of the rest of OD-18, and OD-16 sub-question ⑤ for `draws` and the re-verification before a target counts as reached).
- Any change to ADR-0001 to ADR-0039, to UsageCases, to `hilclaw/`, to tests, to CI, to `pyproject.toml`, to any settings file, or to the `HIL-Claw-data` repository; any new external dependency.
