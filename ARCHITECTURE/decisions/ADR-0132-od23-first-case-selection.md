## Decision

A sentence is **cited** when a source is named with it. Every other sentence is **this record's own**.

### 1. Scope

- **The selection work of a round ≥ 1 of a run of ADR-0120 decision 1**: forming the round's hypotheses (FR-17) and writing its `rounds/<RRRR>/decision/hypotheses.json` (ADR-0059 decision 1), and the order of that write against the round's other steps.
- **Outside**: round 0, which has no selection and no record (ADR-0059 decision 5); the content, form and writer of `decision/decision.md`, of which decision 2 states only the consequence; the composition and writing of the round's specs (rest of OD-6; ADR-0126 decision 4); everything under Boundaries.

### 2. (a) The holder: the `technique-selector` sub-agent

- **The Round-Manager delegates the selection work of a round ≥ 1 to one sub-agent of its turn session, `technique-selector`, and that sub-agent holds the selection role of ADR-0004 decision 4** for these runs. The Round-Manager forms no hypothesis and writes no hypothesis record itself.
- **`decision/decision.md`, the role's other file** (cited: ADR-0004 path table), **has no writer in these runs**, and so is not written by them until a record gives one. That follows from two facts: no record gives the file's content or form — ADR-0004 gives only its place and purpose — so no tool could check it; and the selector holds no file-writing tool (decision 3), for the reasons of K4 premise 2. This record reads FR-11's "어떤 기법을 왜 선택했는지" for these runs as recorded by the `statement` and `basis` of each hypothesis, which are part of the decision log (cited: FR-11); `decision.md` stays the human-readable narrative of ADR-0059 decision 1, and the record that gives it a writer names the tool. So for these runs **no agent writes any file of `decision/` by its own means**.
- **Why a sub-agent** (K2 row): forming a hypothesis is judgement, which K2 condition 4 does not bar from a sub-agent; the agent that writes the record then holds no `state.write` or `run.commit`; its reading of earlier rounds, attempts and cases stays out of the Round-Manager's context, as K2 meant.
- **The reload cost of the OD-23 note, weighed.** The note counts a fresh context, and its reload of state and catalog, per sub-agent per round. For the selector that cost is real: one more fresh context per round ≥ 1 and the reads it makes. It is accepted for the reasons above. Inlining would save the selector's context and its reads, but no context across rounds: under ADR-0118 decision 2 every turn is a fresh session process, so the Round-Manager starts each round with a fresh context in any case.

### 3. (b) Its tools

- **The technique-selector may use exactly `Read`, `Grep`, `Glob`, `mcp__hilclaw__state_read`, `mcp__hilclaw__instance_read`, `mcp__hilclaw__hypotheses_read`, `mcp__hilclaw__checks_read` and `mcp__hilclaw__hypotheses_write`** (decision 4). The reads give it the run's state and instance, the records of earlier rounds and the check results and standing of earlier attempts. Reading is not restricted (K4 premise 1).
- **No file-writing means and no shell**: its list holds no `Write`, `Edit`, `NotebookEdit`, `Bash` and no tool that delegates further, so under K4 premise 2 "it writes only its record" rests on the tool list, not on a prompt; whether a session honours the list is item (xxiii). **Not its tools**: `state.write`, `run.commit`, `run.fresh`, `verdicts.write`, `correctness.check`, `blob.push`, `blob.fetch` and, once registered, `sim.run`, `board.run` and any coder-call tool.
- **The tools reach it by its `tools` list**, by their MCP names (ADR-0010 decision 4) — the second of the three ways ADR-0118 decision 6 leaves open, chosen here for the technique-selector as ADR-0128 decision 3 chose it for the evaluator.
- **`Skill` is not listed** while the session holds no skill (catalog, OD-22); K2's "Skill" is added by the record that gives the first one. Later reads are added by the records that create them (decision 5).

### 4. (b) What writes the record: the registry tool `hypotheses.write`

| Part | Content |
|---|---|
| Name (MCP name) | `hypotheses.write` (`mcp__hilclaw__hypotheses_write`); the name holds no `_`, so the mapping of ADR-0010 decision 4 stays reversible |
| Request | Exactly `run_id` (ADR-0015 decision 4) and `record`: the whole content of the file as one JSON object; both required; any other member, or one missing, is refused |
| Result | The empty object |
| Checks, in order | ① the request, and `record` encoded into bytes that `hilclaw.hypotheses_file.parse` admits — round 0, another `schema_version` and every other refusal of `parse` among it — whose `run_id` is the request's (`invalid_request`, ADR-0015 decision 2); ② the data root (`no_data_root`); ③ **only when the record's `cases_read` holds an entry**, the rules between the record and the cases of its key directory, by the steps of `hilclaw.hypotheses_cases.check_run` taken one by one so that their failures can be told apart (`hilclaw.instance.read`, `hilclaw.case_lookup.find`, `hilclaw.hypotheses_cases.check`): the read of the run's `instance.json` (`run_file_missing`, `run_file_invalid`, `run_file_unsupported`, `run_file_unreadable`, as `instance.read` names them), any other failure of the file system in the lookup, the listing of the key directory among it (`cases_unreadable`), a broken rule (`cases_mismatch`); ④ the write by `hilclaw.hypotheses_file.write` (`hypotheses_exist` when the final name is taken, `hypotheses_not_written` for any other failure of the file system) |

- **It admits exactly what the validators on `main` admit**, whatever they admit when it runs: `parse`, and for a record that names cases `check_run`. It adds no rule of its own and narrows or widens no `hypotheses.json`: the origin `injected` is refused while `hilclaw.hypotheses` refuses it, and admitted with no change of this record once it is admitted there. It does not hold `round` against `STATE.json`, `candidates` against `instance.json` or the specs, or a `ref` against the tree (Alternatives; ADR-0059 Boundaries; OD-8 ㉕ (ii)).
- **Check ③ is skipped when `cases_read` is absent or empty**: by the in-file rule of ADR-0073 decision 2 such a record holds no `case` entry, so no rule between files applies, and the record is not refused for a read it does not need. A report of the lookup refuses nothing by itself; a `cases_read` entry naming a case the lookup reported breaks the rule (cited: `hilclaw.hypotheses_cases`).
- **Every check before the write creates nothing**, so a refused record can be corrected and sent again. **A taken name is answered `hypotheses_exist`**, with nothing replaced and the file under the name not read (cited: ADR-0059 decision 1); what a second write for a round means stays open (Boundaries).
- **Kinds.** `invalid_request` and `no_data_root` are the reserved kinds of ADR-0015 decision 2. The four `run_file_*` kinds are the reserved ones of ADR-0019 decision 3 with the meaning ADR-0124 decision 3 gives them for `instance.json`; **this record widens that reading to this tool's read of the same file**, for which the same property holds: the call appended no line, selected no backend and wrote nothing yet. **None of the `hypotheses_*` kinds of ADR-0129 is reused**: they name failures of reading the file, and this tool never reads it. **Four new kinds**: `cases_unreadable`, `cases_mismatch`, `hypotheses_exist` and `hypotheses_not_written`; `hypotheses_exist` and `hypotheses_not_written` follow `verdicts_exist` and `verdicts_not_written` of ADR-0128 decision 6, and `cases_mismatch` follows `verdicts_mismatch`, on purpose. Each is a name of the tool layer, **not a change of the data plane**; none is reserved.
- **Not journaled**: it costs no limited resource and its outcome is the file (as `verdicts.write`, ADR-0128 decision 6). The fixed tool set grows by this tool in the source (`default_registry`) and in no other way (cited: #5 A-12 point 3); it is reachable through the command-line front too (ADR-0010 decision 3).
- **Left to the code slice**: the module, the description and input schema, and the encoding of `record`, which gives the written form ADR-0059 decision 2 binds the writer to (`verdicts.write` has such a rule, an observation).

### 5. (d) The lookup output: a later read tool

- **The technique-selector reaches the output of the lookup of ADR-0073 decision 1 through a typed read tool of the registry that takes the `run_id`**, added to its list by the record that defines it — the case-lookup work of PLAN P2-7. That record gives its name, result and kinds, how the reports of the lookup are carried and whether a selection goes on after one (cited: ADR-0073 Boundaries). It is not defined here.
- **`hypotheses.write` holds what a record says of cases** (check ③), but it cannot give the selection the cases to read, so it is no answer to this point.
- **Until that tool exists**, the selector is given no lookup output, and its definition has it write no `cases_read` and no `case` entry — a fixed rule of decision 7, not a refusal: `hypotheses.write` would hold such a record by check ③ and write it, as the validators admit it. An absent `cases_read` says that the file records no identification, never that no case was read (cited: ADR-0073 decision 3). ADR-0073 decision 1's input is then not met for these runs; the cost is stated in Consequences.

### 6. (c) and (e) When the record is written, and its presence

- **The record of a round ≥ 1 is written before the round's first spec write and first coder call** (so before any spec copy). In "The round steps" of a round ≥ 1, after the entry steps: (1) the Round-Manager delegates the selection to the technique-selector; (2) when that sub-agent has returned, whatever its report says, the Round-Manager calls `hypotheses.read` with its run id and the round; (3) **only when that call returns the round's record do the round steps go on** to a spec write or a coder delegation of the round (P2-1).
- **When the read fails**, with any kind, the Round-Manager reports the kind in one line and ends the session: it starts no spec write and no coder call of the round and writes nothing further into the run. The turn ends before its round (cited: ADR-0118 decision 2 (ii)), and the next turn goes on with the same round (cited: ADR-0012 decision 3). **Within one start of the turn loop this is not repeated**: such a turn exits leaving `round` as it found it, and the loop then returns and tells its starter (cited: ADR-0122 decisions 2 and 3); the next start goes on with the same round. A selector that fails every time, or a record that is there but cannot be read, ends every such start the same way (a later selection then meets `hypotheses_exist`); a bound across starts is the rest of OD-19 and OD-17's. Under these steps a turn whose round already holds a record delegates the selection again, which meets `hypotheses_exist`, and goes on with the existing record; whether a resumed turn skips the delegation is OD-17's.
- **Why this order**: a hypothesis states what an experiment is expected to show before the experiment (cited: ADR-0059 decision 1), and a coder call is that experiment's first step; a record written after a spec or kernel exists could be fitted to it (NFR-2). Reading the record rather than the report keeps the round off another agent's account (K2 condition 3).
- **The Round-Manager gets `mcp__hilclaw__hypotheses_read`** for step (2), with the reasoning of ADR-0129 decision 3 for the tool; ADR-0129 decision 4 left its use to the round steps.
- **What "가설 없는 기법 선택을 허용하지 않는다" (FR-17) becomes for these runs**: the selection's only write is the record, through a tool that admits only a valid one; and no coder call of a round ≥ 1 begins before the record is readable. The second is a rule of the Round-Manager's definition, not a write zone and not a hook: a hook that refuses such a call, and a check of `cases_read` against what the lookup gave, are P2-4's and the rest of OD-18's.

### 7. (f) The delegation, the report and the definition

- **The delegation message names the run id and the round, and no place** (ADR-0123 decision 2, as ADR-0128 decision 4 has it for the evaluator). The run it names is the session's run (ADR-0123 decision 4).
- **The report** is one final text message: one line per tool request, naming the tool and the result or the failure kind, and for a written record one line per hypothesis `id` with its `tested_by`. The message is not the record; the file is (K2 condition 3).
- **The definition is `orchestration/agents/technique-selector.md`**, copied into the session directory as `.claude/agents/technique-selector.md` by `LAYOUT` (ADR-0118 decision 5), written by a later code-mode slice. Its frontmatter `tools` is the list of decision 3; it names no model (HQ-0041). Its fixed rules: every request carries the run id it was given, and only that one; write the record of the round it was given and nothing by any means but `hypotheses.write`, correcting and sending again on a refusal, except that on `hypotheses_exist` or `hypotheses_not_written` it sends nothing further and reports; write no `cases_read` and no `case` entry while no lookup tool is in its list (decision 5); request no check, measurement or coder call; report as above.

### Walk-through

| Situation | What follows |
|---|---|
| Round 1 at N = 1, after a round 0 whose bring-up attempt `0000/01/01` stands as the baseline (ADR-0119 decision 2) | The Round-Manager delegates `run_id` and round 1. The selector reads with `instance.read` and `checks.read`, and composes one system hypothesis with an `attempt` basis whose `ref` is round 0, candidate 1, attempt 1, an `expected` of `latency_minimisation` `lower`, `tested_by` `[1]`, `candidates` 1 and no `cases_read` (decision 5). `hypotheses.write` skips check ③, writes and answers `{}`. The Round-Manager's `hypotheses.read` returns the record, and the round steps go on |
| A record (once the lookup tool exists) with `cases_read` naming case (`X`, 2) and a `case` basis naming its hypothesis `h3` | Check ③ looks up the key of the run's instance; when that case is there and its record holds `h3`, the file is written. When the case is not there, was reported, or holds no `h3`: `cases_mismatch`, nothing written |
| A record that breaks ADR-0059 — a candidate tested by no hypothesis, an empty `basis`, or the origin `injected` while the validator refuses it | `invalid_request`; the data root is not asked and nothing is written; the selector corrects and sends again |
| A second write for the same round | `hypotheses_exist`; the first file stays and is not read. The Round-Manager's read returns the first record. What a second write means is open (Boundaries) |
| A record of round 0 | `invalid_request` (ADR-0059 decision 5). The Round-Manager delegates no selection in round 0 |
| The selector writes, by mistake, a record for a later round | Written: the tool does not hold `round` against `STATE.json` (decision 4). The Round-Manager's read for its own round answers `hypotheses_missing`, and the turn ends (decision 6). When the run reaches that later round, its selection meets `hypotheses_exist` and the round goes on with the record formed earlier, without the verdicts of the rounds between |
| The selector dies, or reports a failure, before a record is written | `hypotheses.read` answers `hypotheses_missing`; the Round-Manager reports it and ends the session; no coder call of the round begins |
| The selector names another run's id | The tool serves that run; nothing stops it (cited: ADR-0123 decision 4; ADR-0015 decision 7). A record without `cases_read` reads no file of that run, and the write creates the missing directories (cited: `hilclaw/hypotheses_file.py`): an id naming no run gets a run directory holding only this file, and a fresh run (ADR-0120 decision 4) stops being fresh. `state.write` has the same reach; what such a directory means stays OD-8 ⑩ and ⑰. A later hook could see the `run_id` (ADR-0010 decision 6) |

Until the lookup tool exists, no record of these runs names a case, so ADR-0073 decision 1's input, FR-18's lookup and FR-11's identification of the cases a decision referred to are not met by them, and S6 cannot be shown. A record written by mistake for a later round stands, and that round goes on with hypotheses formed before the verdicts of the rounds between (Walk-through); only the selector's definition guards against it. A write tool that creates directories is now held by a sub-agent, not only by the Round-Manager (Walk-through, another run's id). The order of decision 6 rests on the Round-Manager's definition until a hook exists. Three tools now touch one file, and a change of its validator reaches all three.
