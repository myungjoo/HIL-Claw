### 1. Scope and terms

- This record is about **a call of `sim.run` or `board.run` about one attempt of a run that ADR-0061 decision 1 covers**: a run whose hw_profile names `cpu_standin` — the first case, in the local configuration of ADR-0038 decision 1 — or names the mock (cited: ADR-0061 decision 1).
- Of that call it fixes **four points and nothing else**: whether each tool is journaled (decision 2); the `step` values and the meaning of `execution` (decision 3); the run and identity members a request requires (decision 4); and the `spend` of an end line as far as the cited sentences carry it (decision 5 — stated from those sentences only and no rule of this record). Decision 6 puts the order of a call together from the cited records and adds no rule.
- **One contract for both tools, kept.** "nothing but the execution point distinguishes the two operations" (**owner**, O1), and every point of ADR-0061 is stated once for both (cited: ADR-0061 decision 1). Every point below is stated once for both tools as well; they differ in the name of the tool, the execution point it stands for and, by decision 3, in their `step` value, which follows from the tool and not from anything a request says.
- **The points rest on nothing that only one backend has.** The journaled path, the check of the request and the identity are the tool function's part, "the same for every backend" (cited: ADR-0016 decision 2 (a)). Where a point is nevertheless limited to these runs: the weighing of decision 2 where it speaks of what a timing run costs at the target, which is known for the local configuration and the mock only.
- The terms of ADR-0011 (journaled step, identity, start line, end line, open, ended), ADR-0015 (journaled tool), ADR-0016 (backend, operation, target, the moments ① to ⑦), ADR-0030 (ⓢ), ADR-0038 (execution point, sim point, board point, the local configuration), ADR-0009 (timing run, timing executable) and ADR-0061 (the read of ④, the cut) are used unchanged.

### 2. Both tools are journaled

- **`sim.run` and `board.run` are each a journaled tool: one call that reaches the operation is one journaled step** (the term is cited: ADR-0015 decision 1, "a tool one call of which is one journaled step"). That this is for the tools' contract to say is cited above (Context).
- **The definition.** A journaled step is "one execution of a step whose repetition costs a limited resource, or whose outcome must survive the death of the process that obtained it" (cited: ADR-0011 decision 1). The two halves are weighed apart, for each tool.
- **The first half, a limited resource.** "The sources name one such step, the board measurement (cited: #4 §3b, C-4)" (cited: ADR-0011 decision 1); FR-10 describes `board.run` as "고비용·공유·quota" and `sim.run` as "저비용 조기검증", and C-4 says "board는 공유·제한 자원이고 sim은 저비용 조기검증 수단이다" (both cited). **This record reads the board measurement as the step of `board.run` at a target that is a board**: a call of the run tool of a point carries out the timing run of the attempt at that point and is the call whose figure is judged there (cited: ADR-0061 decisions 2 and 5), while `correctness.check` "carries no figure" and its run "is not the timing run" (cited: ADR-0061 decision 5); whether `profiler.collect` measures anything is said by no record. At such a target the first half holds for `board.run`. **For the runs this record is about, the fixed inputs do not let one say that the first half holds, for either tool**: in the local configuration both points are `cpu_standin` carrying out "the same local run" (**owner**, O1), so a call of either tool consumes time of the host; wall-clock time is an axis of FR-3 for which a run may state a budget (cited: FR-3 ③; [ADR-0033](ADR-0033-od8-instance-record-wall-clock-budget.md) decisions 2 and 3), but "Whether a given axis is accounted through the log at all is not decided here" (cited: ADR-0011 decision 7; decision 5 below); and a call at the mock consumes nothing at any target. The reading of the board measurement therefore carries nothing for these runs. **Nor do the fixed inputs let one say that the first half holds for `sim.run` elsewhere**: FR-10 and C-4 call the sim low-cost and name no limited resource it consumes.
- **The second half, an outcome that must survive.** The outcome of a call is what the round loop acts on: promotion to the board point, evaluation and the best (cited: FR-1), and, once a ceiling can be stated, the verdict the tool function computes (cited: ADR-0061 decisions 3 and 6). It is **not a function of its request**: a timing run is one measurement, its samples vary — the stopping rule of ADR-0007 decision 4 stops on their spread — and a second run of the same executable "is a measurement of its own, and samples are never pooled" (cited: ADR-0009 decision 9; ADR-0007 decision 4, "Samples of two measurements are never pooled into one"). Under ADR-0061 decision 2 a second call also builds again, so a new build is timed. An outcome lost with its process can therefore not be obtained again; it can only be replaced by the outcome of another measurement. **This half holds for both tools in the local configuration**, and it carries the choice there:
  - *Not journaled.* A turn that dies after a timing run and before it recorded what it learned leaves nothing of that run. The resumed turn (NFR-1) measures again and may be given another figure, another state (converged or not) or another verdict than the dead turn saw and perhaps began to act on. Nothing shows that the first run took place.
  - *Journaled.* The resumed turn presents the same identity and is given what the end line holds (cited: ADR-0011 decisions 3 and 4). Every call that reached a complete start line stays in the log under its identity (cited: ADR-0006 decision 5, "A complete line is never edited, removed or reordered"), so it can be traced which timing runs of an attempt were made at which point and what each established (NFR-2).
- **At the mock the second half does not hold either.** "The outcome of a mock call depends on its script and its call alone" (cited: ADR-0016 decision 5 (e)), so the same call is given the same outcome and nothing is lost with a process that a repeated call could not obtain again. For a run at the mock the answer rests on the one-contract argument below alone: the mock answers the contract every backend answers, and "the cases a contract test runs over the mock are the cases every backend is held to" (cited: ADR-0016 decision 2 (b)).
- **One contract for every backend, and for both tools.** Whether a tool is journaled shows in the contract a caller sees — the member `execution`, what a repeated call answers, the answer `step_open` — and the journaled path is "the same for every backend" while "a caller need not know which backend answered" (cited: ADR-0016 decision 2 (a) and (b)); so a tool cannot be journaled at one backend and not at another, and **`board.run` at `cpu_standin` takes the answer that `board.run` at a board needs**. ADR-0061 decision 1 has both tools follow one contract (cited); **`sim.run` is journaled with it**, for the second half above (in the local configuration) and so that the two calls of one attempt are answered alike on resume. Two tools with one contract of which one were journaled would have one contract in name only: the request (`execution`) and the answers would differ.
- **The cost of the choice: a death during a call.** With a journaled tool the identity is then open, what the resumed turn may do is "Neither skip nor run it under this identity" (cited: ADR-0011 decision 4), and "Until a recovery rule exists (OD-10, the retry part of OD-17), an open step is a point the run does not pass by itself" (cited: ADR-0011 Consequences). In the local configuration a timing run that would cost seconds to repeat thus stops the work on that attempt at that point until somebody decides. **This is the reason for the opposite answer**, and it is weighed as ADR-0040 decision 2 weighed it for the check: the stop is visible and loses nothing; it ends with the recovery rule that OD-10 and the retry part of OD-17 must give for every journaled tool; and the alternative is a second measurement that nobody decided and nothing records, which can replace an unfavourable outcome unseen (Limits).
- **What an end line stores.** The `result` of an end line "is the result object of the call itself" (cited: ADR-0011 decision 3), so whatever a later slice makes a member of a result of either tool is written to `events.ndjson`. What a result may hold is already bound: never the standard error of the kernel's process, the command line, an input value, and, until OD-13 sub-question ⑦ is answered, the seed or anything a caller could use to pick the seed of a later run (cited: ADR-0061 decision 7). The `failure` of an end line holds the `kind` and `message` of a tool failure (cited: ADR-0011 decision 3), and an end line holds exactly one of the two beside `step`, `execution` and `spend` (same decision), so journaling the tools writes nothing to the log that a result or a tool failure may not hold.
- **What ADR-0061 wrote "if the tool turns out journaled"** now holds without the condition: `objective_not_stated` and `objective_not_covered` are answered before the start line and never as the `failure` of an end line (cited: ADR-0061 decision 4). Nothing of it changes.
- **How far the fixed inputs carry the answer, and what is provisional.** For `board.run` at a target that is a board, the definition of ADR-0011 decision 1 with C-4 carries it. For the local configuration, only the second half and the one-contract argument carry it; for the mock, the one-contract argument alone; and setting them against the stop of an open step is this record's own judgement. **The answer is provisional in two senses**: (a) for the local configuration and the mock, it is looked at again when the recovery of an open step is decided (OD-10, the retry part of OD-17), if that decision leaves an open timing run of the local configuration as something only a person can resolve — the sense of ADR-0040 decision 2; (b) for `sim.run`, a record of a configuration in which the sim point is not the board point's run — the case ADR-0061 decision 1 names as what "could split" the two tools (sub-question ⑫ of OD-18, a simulator; #5 B-2, B-3) — says whether this contract still answers `sim.run` there, for example at a simulator whose outcome is a function of its request; a record that took `sim.run` out of the journaled tools would change the contract of `sim.run` at every backend (ADR-0016 decision 2) or split the contracts of the two tools (ADR-0061 decision 1), and would not make `sim.run` journaled at one backend and not at another. It is not left open here, because the next slices and the code are cut by it.
- This record says nothing of whether `profiler.collect` is journaled, and nothing of `correctness.check` beyond what ADR-0040 decided.

### 3. One `step` value per tool, and the meaning of `execution`

- **Each tool has exactly one `step` value: `sim_run_timing` for `sim.run` and `board_run_timing` for `board.run`.**
- **Why one each.** The identity is the envelope numbers with `step` and `execution` (cited: ADR-0011 decision 3). The two calls of one attempt — one at each point — have equal envelope numbers and must be two identities, or the second would be answered with the stored result of the first (cited, same decision), against "Each of the two operations carries out its run itself when it is called" (cited: ADR-0061 decision 2) and "It is not judged once for both" (cited: ADR-0052 decision 6, as ADR-0061 decision 5 applies it). They are, because "Two tools of the registry do not share a `step` value" (cited: ADR-0015 decision 4). Within one tool nothing is left to tell two `step` values apart: "The tool called says the point; no member of a request says it" (cited: ADR-0061 decision 5), and `execution` counts executions, not points. ADR-0040's two values of one tool followed from a member that states the point (cited: ADR-0038 decision 4; ADR-0040 decision 3); here that pair is split across two tools, one value each, and a second value of one tool would have nothing to follow from.
- **The spelling.** Each is a *name* of ADR-0001 (`[a-z][a-z0-9_]{0,31}`; 14 and 16 characters). They were formed from the name of the tool with its `.` replaced by `_`, then `_timing`: the prefix keeps them apart from the values of every other tool, as the values of ADR-0040 begin with the name of their tool; the suffix names what one execution is, one timing run of ADR-0009 (cited for the operation: ADR-0061 decision 2); and the value then equals no tool name in either spelling — not `sim.run`, not the MCP name `sim_run` that `hilclaw.mcp_front` gives the tool (observation), not `mcp__hilclaw__sim_run` — so that no rule "the `step` is the tool name" is suggested, which `correctness.check` does not follow. This says how these two values were formed and is no rule for the `step` values of another tool.
- **No other tool uses either value** (the rule is cited: ADR-0015 decision 4). Checked on 2026-10-15 in `hilclaw/`, `tests/` and `ARCHITECTURE/`: neither string occurs. The `step` values on `main` are `correctness_check_sim` and `correctness_check_board` (ADR-0040; bound in `hilclaw/check_identity.py`) and the placeholders of records and tests (`example_step`, `other_step` and the like); neither value equals one of them. The strings `sim_run` and `board_run` occur in `hilclaw/mcp_front.py` and in tests (`tests/test_mcp_front.py`, `tests/test_backends.py`, `tests/test_tools.py`, `tests/test_mock_backend.py`) as the MCP names of the two tools, as refused registry names or as Python argument names that stand for the tools; they are tool names, not `step` values, and neither value equals them.
- **The request still carries no `step`** (cited: ADR-0015 decision 4): the tool function of each tool has its one value from the contract.
- **The meaning of `execution` for these tools.** `execution` "counts the executions of that step for that target" (cited: ADR-0011 decision 3): here, the calls of one tool about one attempt that reach the operation — the timing runs of one attempt at the tool's point. The first is stated with 1 (cited, same decision: "it is 1 unless a decision outside this record asked for a further execution"). The runs at the two points are counted apart, since their `step` differs.
- **A further `execution` is a new measurement.** It is a new identity (cited: ADR-0011 decision 3, "Running the step again is a new identity with a higher `execution`"), so it is carried out as any call whose identity has no line: the read of ④ is made again, the sample form is derived again (cited: ADR-0061 decision 4, "It is derived at each call; no value is kept") and the `bound` is read again in that read (cited: ADR-0061 decision 6), from what the file then states, the timing executable is built again (cited: ADR-0061 decision 2, "at each call"), the seed is whatever OD-13 sub-question ⑦ will give (cited: ADR-0061 decision 3), and **its samples are not pooled with those of the earlier run** (cited: ADR-0007 decision 4; ADR-0009 decision 9). In the local configuration its outcome can differ from the earlier one by noise and by the build alone; at the mock, by what the script derives from the call (ADR-0016 decision 5 (e)). The earlier run stays ended in the log with its outcome.
- **A repeated call under an ended identity makes no new measurement**: it is answered with what the end line holds, reads no objective or `bound`, builds and runs nothing; under an open identity it is the tool failure `step_open` (cited: ADR-0011 decision 3; ADR-0015 decision 2; ADR-0016 decision 2). It computes no verdict, so no value kept from an earlier call stands in for the `bound` of a new verdict (ADR-0061 decision 6 is untouched): the stored result is the outcome of the one call of that identity.
- **Who may ask for a further `execution`, and when, stays where ADR-0011 left it**: "whether and when that is done is not this record's (OD-10, the retry part of OD-17)" (cited: ADR-0011 decision 3). This record gives no rule, and a caller that raises `execution` by itself is not stopped (Limits).
- **Not a further `execution`** (each cited): the call of `board.run` after the call of `sim.run` about the same attempt, which is another tool and another `step` (above); and a timing run of a repaired attempt, which is a run of another attempt, whose envelope numbers differ in `attempt` (ADR-0002 decision 5: a repair call has "`attempt` raised by 1"). Whether a re-measurement of an earlier best (S1-b-4, S10-3) or the re-measurement OD-13 sub-question ① proposes is a further `execution` of one of the two steps, or a step a later record defines, is not decided (OD-17 sub-question ①; OD-13 sub-question ①).

### 4. The run and identity members of a request

- **A request of either tool requires five members: `run_id`, `round`, `candidate`, `attempt` and `execution`.** They have the meaning and the form of ADR-0015 decision 4 (cited, not re-decided): `run_id` is "The run the call is about: a directory name of `runs/`"; `round`, `candidate` and `attempt` say what the call is about "as the envelope numbers of a line"; `execution` is "an *integer* ≥ 1", "required and has no default".
- **All three numbers are required.** That is for this contract to say: "which numbers a tool requires is its contract" (cited: ADR-0015 decision 4). A call times the kernel of one attempt — "exactly one timing run of ADR-0009 of the attempt's kernel" — and the output path the tool function forms is "the path of the `output/` of the attempt the request names" (both cited: ADR-0061 decisions 2 and 3); an attempt is named by all three ("`candidate` only with `round`, `attempt` only with `candidate`" — cited: ADR-0015 decision 4). No record describes a timing run of a candidate, a round or a run as a whole, so a request that leaves `attempt` out is a refused request, like one with an invalid run or identity (cited: ADR-0015 decision 4). The form of `round` admits 0, the bring-up round (cited: ADR-0006 decision 3); which attempts are timed, at which point and in which order is FR-1's and the loop's (PLAN P2-1).
- **No member states the point** (cited: ADR-0061 decision 5). A member named `point` in a request of either tool states nothing; whether such a request is refused is what the tool function does with a member no record names, a later slice's.
- A request in which one of the five is missing or ill-formed is refused as `invalid_request` in ①, before the data root is asked and before anything is read (cited: ADR-0015 decisions 2 and 4).
- **The member list of a request is not closed by this record.** Whether a request carries anything else, the input schema, the description and what the tool function does with a member no record names are a later slice's. What ADR-0061 keeps out of a request stays out: the point (its decision 5), the sample form ("the request carries none" — its decision 4) and the `bound` (its decision 6). The dimensions, `n_max` and the objective are taken from the read of ④ (cited: ADR-0061 decision 3), and this record adds no member for them; the seed's source is OD-13 sub-question ⑦.

### 5. The `spend` of an end line

- `spend` "states every amount the step consumed of the axes that are accounted through the log" and is "possibly empty" (cited: ADR-0011 decision 7). No record names such an axis: ADR-0011 names "no axis, no unit and no party that measures" (its decision 1), and ADR-0040 decision 6 says "No record names an axis that is accounted through the log" (cited) of the records then on `main`, to ADR-0039; no record from ADR-0040 to ADR-0061 names one (checked on 2026-10-15).
- **As long as that is so, the `spend` of an end line of either tool is the empty object** — also on an end line with `failure`, which "carries `spend` like any other" (cited: ADR-0011 decision 7). This follows from the cited sentences, as ADR-0040 decision 6 reasoned for the check, and is no rule of this record.
- **This record names no axis and no amount**, and it does not say whether the host time of a timing run, a board's time or a quota (FR-10: "quota") is accounted through the log. Which axes a call consumes, in which amounts, and from where the tool function has an amount, belong to the record that defines an axis of the log (FR-3, OD-2, OD-19) and to the slice of this contract that follows it.

| ⑤ | The start line, whose `data` is exactly `step` and `execution` | `start_line_failed` | ADR-0011 decision 3; ADR-0026 decision 3 |
| ⑥ | The operation is called (it builds, runs the timing run once and returns the report and the figure, or the condition); the tool function judges what it returned and, where a ceiling is stated, computes the verdict | — | ADR-0016 decision 2; ADR-0061 decisions 2 and 3 |
| ⑦ | The end line, with `spend` and exactly one of `result` and `failure`; the call returns what it holds | `end_line_failed` | ADR-0011 decisions 3 and 7; ADR-0026 decision 3 |

- In either order of the seed and the start line, the start line holds no seed, since its `data` is exactly `step` and `execution` (cited: ADR-0011 decision 3), and the operation is called after both.
- That the judging and the verdict lie before the end line is no added rule: the `result` of an end line is the result of the call (ADR-0011 decision 3), and a result of these tools reports a completed measurement or broken, with the verdict where a ceiling is stated (ADR-0061 decision 7). Which tool failures can stand as the `failure` of an end line of these tools — those the operation may raise, the kinds of ⑲ (나) — and what a call answers when the operation returns neither form are later slices' (ADR-0061 Consequences items (4) and (6)).

### Walk-through

The run `r1` names `cpu_standin`; the attempt `round` 3, `candidate` 2, `attempt` 1 of it. `…` stands for whatever else a later slice makes a request carry. **No member of a result is shown**, and no flag, time limit, seed or path.

|---|---|---|

### 7. Limits

1. **A caller can re-measure until it likes the outcome.** A caller that raises `execution` by itself is given a new build and a new timing run each time, and nothing stops it (cited: ADR-0011 decision 9; ADR-0015 decision 7). Since in the local configuration the outcome varies with noise and build (decision 2), a caller can keep the lowest figure, wait for a converged state, or — once a ceiling can be stated — for a *within* after an *over*. This record does not stop it and does not say which of several ended runs of one attempt at one point counts. **Journaling makes it visible** — the earlier runs stay in the log — **and does not close it.** No registered sub-question holds it: OD-18 sub-question ⑭ is about `correctness.check` only, OD-13 sub-question ① is about paired re-measurement and its judgement, ⑪ about imprecise timing environments, and ADR-0061 decision 2 leaves to OD-15, OD-29 and OD-13 sub-question ① which of the two measurements of an attempt — one per point — counts. **Registered as sub-question ⑳** (Boundaries); who may ask for a further execution stays the retry part of OD-17.
2. **A stored outcome says nothing about another run of the same kernel** (decision 2), nor about an objective or a `bound` the file states later: a repeated call reads neither.
3. **A death during the build, the timing run or the judging** leaves the step open although the target may have finished its part; the outcome is lost. No form closes the window between an outcome and its end line (cited: ADR-0011 decision 2), and the judging and the verdict, carried out by the tool function after the operation returns (cited: ADR-0061 decision 3), lie in it.
4. **An open step stops the work on its attempt at that point** until a recovery rule exists (decision 2), also where repeating it would cost seconds.
5. **The point a call states is the tool called, and it is believed.** Nothing checks that a call of `board.run` follows a promotion, that both points are measured, or in which order: there is no access control (cited: ADR-0010 decision 8; ADR-0038 decision 8).
6. **A wrong identity**: a caller that states the numbers of another attempt has that attempt's kernel timed and recorded under them (cited: ADR-0015 decision 7; ADR-0061 decision 3, the output path of the attempt the request names).
7. **A round other than the stored one**: for a round before it the lookup needs lines before the stored position (cited: ADR-0011 decision 6; OD-17 sub-question ①, whose example is "a later re-measurement"); a round above it is not refused (cited: ADR-0015 decisions 4 and 7). This record adds no rule for either.
8. **Two calls at once** can both find no line (cited: ADR-0015 decision 7; ADR-0011 decision 9 for two turns). What two timing runs at once on one machine do to each other's samples is not examined here.
9. The limits of ADR-0061 decision 8 stand unchanged.

## Alternatives considered

| Point | Alternative | Why not |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | Neither tool is journaled | See decision 2: in the local configuration an outcome lost with its process cannot be obtained again, only replaced by another measurement; the tool layer would record no run; and the contract of `board.run` would have to be replaced for the first board, where the first half holds. What speaks for it — no stop after a death during a cheap run — is weighed there. |
| 2 | `board.run` journaled, `sim.run` not | ADR-0061 decision 1 has one contract for both, which would then differ in its request (`execution`) and its answers; the second half holds for `sim.run` as well, and the round loop acts on the sim-point outcome (promotion — FR-1). Whether a configuration with a simulator splits them is that configuration's record (decision 2 (b)). |
| 2 | Journaled only at a backend whose target is a board | The journaled path is the tool function's part and the same for every backend (ADR-0016 decision 2 (a)); request and answers would differ by backend, which a caller need not know (ADR-0016 decision 2 (b)). |
| 2 | Leave it open until the recovery of an open step is decided | The result members, the script cases of the mock and the builder of the code are cut by it; every later slice would carry both answers. |
| 3 | One `step` value shared by both tools | Refused by ADR-0015 decision 4 ("Two tools of the registry do not share a `step` value"); the board-point call of an attempt would also find the sim-point identity ended and be answered with its result (ADR-0011 decision 3), against ADR-0052 decision 6. |
| 3 | Two `step` values per tool, as ADR-0040 | They followed from a member stating the point (ADR-0038 decision 4); here no member states it (ADR-0061 decision 5), so nothing would choose between them. |
| 3 | `sim_run` and `board_run` | Equal to the MCP names of the two tools: a reader could take a `step` value for a tool name, and a rule "the `step` is the tool name" would be suggested that `correctness.check` does not follow. |
| 3 | Values without the name of the tool (`sim_timing`, `board_timing`) | A value that begins with the name of its tool cannot meet the value of another tool, whose contract chooses its own (the reasoning of ADR-0040's row). |
| 4 | A request member that states the point | Refused by ADR-0061 decision 5: "The tool called says the point; no member of a request says it". |
| 4 | Fewer numbers required (`attempt` optional, a timing run of a candidate or a round) | A call times the kernel of one attempt and forms the output path of that attempt (ADR-0061 decisions 2 and 3); no record describes another run, and the identity of a run of an attempt would have two forms. |
| 4 | Close the member list here and refuse every other member | The input schema and any further member are a later slice's; a closed list would be reopened by it. |
| 5 | Name an axis here (the host time of a timing run, a count of runs) | No record names an axis that is accounted through the log (decision 5); which axes exist is FR-3's, OD-2's and OD-19's. |

## Consequences

- **What the next slices must still add** — the rest of ADR-0061 Consequences, item by item: (1) of the identity item, the input schema and the description, and what the tool function does with a member no record names; (2) the result members — the third clause of OD-13 sub-question ⑩ — which, the tools being journaled, are written to `events.ndjson` as the `result` of an end line: the point at which ADR-0061's A-13 reading said it would end, to be judged by that slice; (3) the timing seed (OD-13 sub-question ⑦); (4) the form of what crosses the cut, and what a call answers when an operation returns neither form — for a journaled tool, whether the identity is ended with a `failure` or left open (as ADR-0043 and ADR-0045 did for the check); (5) the mock's operation, its script and a default script — a script must be able to express the death of the process while the operation runs; (6) the answers and kinds of ⑲ (가) and (나), with which of them can stand as the `failure` of an end line, the log of ⑲ (다) and the setting values of ⑲ (라); (7) the spend beyond decision 5 (OD-2, OD-19). And sub-question ⑳.
- **Code slices that can now be cut** (code mode, later tasks; names and modules are the code task's; this record binds no code): the identity part of the run tools — the two `step` values and the check of the five members of decision 4 — with its tests, as `hilclaw/check_identity.py` is for the check. **An observation on the builders**: `hilclaw.hw_tool.tool_function` takes one `step`, which fits one value per tool, but has no place between the selection and the start line for the answers of ADR-0061 decision 4 and reads only a hw_profile in ④; `stepped_tool_function` has both places and takes a non-empty tuple of `step` names; which the code uses, or whether a builder is added, is the code task's and is not decided here. **Not yet possible**: the tool functions of either tool and their registration (result members, seed), the operation of `cpu_standin` for either tool (seed, setting values, container), the operation of the mock and its script cases, and the conformance tests of P1-1. PLAN P1-1 stays open.
- **Not edited and now less exact** (follow-ups for the integrator; the list holds what this record found): ADR-0011 decision 1 ("The sources name one such step, the board measurement … which other steps are journaled is not decided here") and its Boundaries ("which steps other than the board measurement are journaled") and ADR-0015 decision 5 ("the sources name the board measurement only") — records now name four journaled steps of three tools, and this record reads the board measurement as one of them; ADR-0038 decision 2 ("What the local run is — its mechanism, the request and the result of either tool, whether either is journaled — is the contract of the run tools and is not decided here") and its Boundaries bullet on the run tools; ADR-0040 decision 2's last bullet ("This record says nothing of whether `sim.run`, `board.run` or `profiler.collect` is journaled"), true of that record, and its Boundaries ("`sim.run`, `board.run` and `profiler.collect`, among it whether each is journaled and its `step` values"); ADR-0061 decision 4 ("before the start line if the tool turns out journaled"), its Limit 5 ("the identity of a call and the result members are open"), its Consequences item (1) and the "Not yet possible" list of its code slices ("the tool functions (identity, …)"), and its Boundaries ("whether either tool is journaled and its `step` values; the meaning of `execution`"). No code docstring was found that becomes less exact. No sentence of UsageCases became false.
- **A-13 reading**: document mode; no code, dependency, settings file or schema file changes. The record names two `step` values and the run and identity members of a request. A request is the interface between layer 1 and layer 2 (ADR-0010 and ADR-0015 Consequences), and its five members are those ADR-0015 decision 4 already defined. The `step` values are values of the member `step` in the `data` of the two kinds of line ADR-0011 defined; that record fixed the member and its form and left the values to each tool ("this record lists no value, and the name of the board measurement belongs to its tool contract", its decision 3). **This is no "데이터 plane 스키마 변경"**: no member of `STATE.json`, `instance.json`, a spec, a result file, `artifacts.json` or a line of `events.ndjson` is added, removed or changed in type, form or meaning; the version of a line "is not raised by a new value of `kind`, nor by a change in the `data` of a kind" (ADR-0006 decision 8), and here not even the `data` of a kind changes; no schema file of the repository lists `step` values; and no member of a result is named, so nothing new of a result reaches the log by this record.
- **Runtime acceptance items**: none beyond ADR-0052's and ADR-0056's.
