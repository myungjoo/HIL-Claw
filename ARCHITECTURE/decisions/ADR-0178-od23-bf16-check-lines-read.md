## Context

- **What a check writes** (cited: ADR-0177 decision 3). A check of a bf16 kind is journaled under `correctness_check_bf16_sim` at the sim point and `correctness_check_bf16_board` at the board point; fp32 `gemm` keeps `correctness_check_sim` and `correctness_check_board`. The value follows from `point` and from the read of ⓢ: the bf16 value when the `instance.json` read whole in ⓢ states an `op_signature` whose `dtype` is `bf16`, the value of ADR-0040 decision 3 otherwise. A record covered in ④ whose `dtype` is not the one the `step` was taken for is answered `op_signature_not_covered` with no line, "so that a line under a bf16 value always holds a bf16 result and a line under an existing value an fp32 one". "A repeated call whose file has changed its `dtype` meanwhile looks up another identity — the case ADR-0100 decision 5 states for any change of value." A result holds no `kind`, `dtype` or `layout`. Its `outcome` keeps the three values of ADR-0042 decision 2 (**owner**, HQ-0077: "Every other member, presence rule and meaning is ADR-0042's").
- **How the readers find a check's lines today** (observation, `main` `df8d0088`). Each maps the request's or entry's `point` to the one `step` of ADR-0040 decision 3 and reads only `events.ndjson`:
  - `checks.read` (`hilclaw/checks_tool.py`, ADR-0131 decision 3) through its `STEP_OF`; ADR-0131's table says "one element per end line of that attempt at the point's `step` (`correctness_check_sim` or `correctness_check_board`, ADR-0040 decision 3)" (cited).
  - `verdicts.write` (`hilclaw/verdicts_tool.py`) founds a `check` entry on the lines of its `ref` "at the `step` of its `point`" (ADR-0131 decision 5) and holds both attempts of a counted comparison to the standing verified at `board` (ADR-0140 decision 5), both through `checks_tool.STEP_OF`.
  - `hilclaw/spec_feedback.py` (ADR-0151 decision 3 ⑨, ⑩) asks `check_standing.standing` under `correctness_check_sim` and `correctness_check_board` and names the compiler log of the deciding check `build-log-<step>-<EE>.txt` (ADR-0078).
  - `hilclaw/check_standing.py` (ADR-0110) computes the standing "per point, from the checks of that point's `step` alone" (cited: ADR-0110 decision 5); since T-0499 it accepts all four values (observation).
- **So, unchanged, a bf16 run's checks are invisible.** `checks.read` would answer every bf16 attempt with no check and "undetermined", `verdicts.write` would refuse every `check` entry of it with `check_unfounded`, and `feedback.attempts` would hide every such attempt.
- **What the readers' records fix and this record keeps.** The request and result of `checks.read` (ADR-0131 decision 3); the `check` and `comparison` entries of `verdicts.json` and their `point` "(`sim` or `board`: the member and values of ADR-0040 decision 4, with their meaning)" (cited: ADR-0071); the order of every check and the failure kinds of each reader; the rule of ADR-0110 decisions 2 and 3; the entry of ADR-0151 decision 2 (d). Each reader's scope (ADR-0110 decision 1; ADR-0131 decision 1; ADR-0151 decision 1) is unchanged; whether a run with bf16 checks is in it is that scope's (a run whose hw_profile names the mock today; the runs of ADR-0076 decision 1 once ADR-0177 decision 2 admits a bf16 contract at the start).

## Decision

### 1. A point's lines are the lines of both its `step` values, read together

- **The rule.** Wherever a reader of a check result (`checks.read`, `verdicts.write`, `hilclaw.spec_feedback`) reads "the lines of an attempt at a point", it reads the step lines of that attempt whose `step` is either value of the point: `correctness_check_sim` or `correctness_check_bf16_sim` at `sim`, `correctness_check_board` or `correctness_check_bf16_board` at `board`. The lines of the two values are taken together, in the order of the log. Nothing else is read: the readers still read only `events.ndjson`, never `instance.json`.
- **Identities stay apart.** An identity is the envelope numbers with `step` and `execution` (cited: ADR-0011 decision 3), so an identity open under one value is not ended by an end line under the other, and `execution` 1 under each value are two checks.
- **For a log without a bf16 line nothing changes**: every reader answers what it answers today, byte for byte. For a log whose attempt holds lines of one value only, the answer is the one the reader gives today for an fp32 log, with the bf16 value in place of the fp32 one.
- **ADR-0110 under the rule.** "The standing is computed per point" (cited: ADR-0110 decision 5) stays; "from the checks of that point's `step` alone" is read as "from the checks of that point's two `step` values alone". Decisions 2 and 3 apply unchanged to all the counting lines together: every end line of either value counts, a `"fail"` or `"broken"` result under either value gives not verified, an open identity or a class (ii) `failure` under either value keeps the attempt from verified, and a class (i) `failure` counts neither way. The two points stay apart as ADR-0110 decision 5 has it. In code this is `hilclaw.check_standing.point_standing`; `check_standing.standing`, per value, is unchanged.
- **Why both values count.** A result under each value has an `outcome` of the same three values and the same meaning (**owner**, HQ-0077, cited above), and each is an outcome of the attempt's kernel at that point. Setting one value's results aside would let a fail be passed over by a pass under the other value — the hole ADR-0110 decision 3 closes for executions of one value.

### 2. What each reader answers

- **`checks.read`.** The request and the result are unchanged: no member, no value of `point`, no failure kind is added. `checks` lists, in the order of the lines, the elements of ADR-0131 decision 3 for the lines of both values; `standing` is `point_standing`; without `attempt`, an attempt is listed when the log holds a line of it under either value. A `result` without an admitted `outcome` under either value, in the request's scope, is `check_result_invalid`; every other failure is as today.
- **`verdicts.write`.** A `check` entry is founded on the results of its `ref` at its `point` under both values (ADR-0131 decision 4's rule, unchanged, over that set); the scope of `check_result_invalid` is those lines. A counted comparison holds both its attempts to `point_standing` verified at `board` (ADR-0140 decision 5). The `comparison` entries keep their own `step` values (`sim_run_timing`, `board_run_timing`; ADR-0140), so the two scopes of the step pass stay disjoint.
- **`feedback.attempts` (ADR-0151).** The standing at each point is `point_standing`; the deciding result is the first ended check of the point in line order under either value whose `outcome` is `"fail"` or `"broken"`; the compiler log of ⑩ is the file of ADR-0078 named by the `step` of that check's line (`build-log-correctness_check_bf16_sim-<EE>.txt` for a bf16 check at the sim point).

### 3. When one attempt holds lines of both values

- **How it can happen.** Only through an `instance.json` whose `dtype` changed between two checks of one attempt — ADR-0177 decision 3's case — or a log not written by the tool. A file "which is written once (ADR-0004)" (cited: ADR-0006 decision 8, as ADR-0100 quotes it) does not produce it.
- **What the readers answer: all of it, as one point.** `checks.read` lists the elements of both values in line order (two elements may then carry the same `execution`, one per value), and its `standing` is that of decision 1 over both. `verdicts.write` founds a `check` entry on the results of both: a `"pass"` entry needs every result of both values to pass, and another `outcome` needs one result of either value to hold it. `feedback.attempts` takes the first failed check of either value in line order.
- **The floor.** Executions of both values count together (decision 1); an attempt passed under one value and failed under the other is not verified.
- **Nothing is refused for it.** No reader raises a failure because both values hold lines; refusing would leave the run with no reading of its checks, and no reader can tell which `dtype` the attempt "really" had without `instance.json`, which none of them reads.

### 4. Where it lives in code

- `hilclaw.check_standing.STEPS_OF` (the two values of each point, the ADR-0040 value first) and `point_standing(lines, point, round, candidate, attempt)`; `hilclaw.checks_tool.checks_in_order`, the pass of `checks_of` as one list in line order, which `checks.read`, `verdicts.write` and `hilclaw.spec_feedback` use to keep the order across the two values; `checks_tool.STEP_OF` is removed. These names are code choices, not rules of this record.

### 5. A-13 reading

- **No data-plane schema change.** No file, member, value or version of the data plane changes. The `check` entry's `point` keeps the meaning ADR-0071 gives it, an execution point of ADR-0040 decision 4; what changes is which log lines `verdicts.write` founds it on — a rule of the tool layer (ADR-0131 decision 5), not a member's meaning. No line of `events.ndjson` changes: the readers only read the lines ADR-0177 decision 3 has the tool write.
- **The tool results an agent reads** (`checks.read`) keep their form. Their content widens to lines that no landed path writes outside a test until ADR-0177's later slices: the `checks` and `standing` of an attempt whose checks are under a bf16 value. For every log without such a line the content is as today.

## Alternatives considered

| Point | Alternative | Why not |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | (i) **Read the lines of both values of the point** | **Adopted** (decision 1): one file, as today; no new read, failure kind or member; a log without bf16 lines answers as today. |
| 1 | (ii) Read the `op_signature` of `instance.json`, as the tool does in ⓢ, and read the lines of the one value it gives | A second file for each reader, with its own failure kinds (`instance.json` missing, damaged, of another version) added to `checks.read`, `verdicts.write` and `spec.write`; ADR-0131 decision 3 and ADR-0151 make `events.ndjson` the only run file read. It hides the lines of the other value when the file changed (decision 3), so a fail under the earlier `dtype` would vanish from the standing. |
| 1 | A `point` value per `step` (`bf16_sim`, `bf16_board`) in the request and in `verdicts.json` | Changes the request of `checks.read` and the values of a member of `verdicts.json` (ADR-0071), a data-plane schema change (CLAUDE.md A-13); the evaluator would have to know the `dtype` to ask. |
| 3 | Refuse a read whose attempt holds lines of both values (for example `run_file_invalid`) | The run could then never be judged; the case is a changed `instance.json`, not a damaged log, and ADR-0019's meaning of `run_file_invalid` does not cover it. |
| 3 | Take only the value of the last line, or of the latest check | A fail under the earlier value would be passed over — the hole ADR-0110 decision 3 closes; "latest" needs an order of checks that ADR-0110 does not use ("the order of the lines does not matter", cited: ADR-0110 decision 2). |
| 3 | Count each value's standing apart and combine the standings | Not equal to the rule over the lines: a pass under one value beside only a class (i) failure under the other would be undetermined, though no verdict is hidden. |

## Consequences

- **Code**: `hilclaw/check_standing.py` (`STEPS_OF`, `point_standing`), `hilclaw/checks_tool.py` (`checks_in_order`, the read over both values, `STEP_OF` removed), `hilclaw/verdicts_tool.py` (founding and the counted comparison over both values), `hilclaw/spec_feedback.py` (standing, deciding check and compiler log over both values), with their tests. `hilclaw/timings_tool.py`, `hilclaw/check_identity.py` and the tool `correctness.check` are not changed.
- **Runtime acceptance items**: none.
- **The cost, stated.** With lines of both values for one attempt, `checks.read` may list two elements with the same `execution` and does not say which value each is under; the `result`'s `shape` usually tells them apart (three members for `gemm`, two for `rmsnorm`, `elementwise_add`, `swiglu`), but nothing in the result names the value (ADR-0177 decision 3, "What a result does not say"). The evaluator cannot ask for one value alone.
- **Less exact, not edited**: ADR-0110 decision 2 ("whose `step` is the point's"), decision 5 ("from the checks of that point's `step` alone") and its walk-through; ADR-0131 decision 3's table ("at the point's `step` (`correctness_check_sim` or `correctness_check_board`, ADR-0040 decision 3)") and decision 5 ("at the `step` of its `point`"); ADR-0151 Context ("its checks as step lines of `events.ndjson` (`correctness_check_sim`, `correctness_check_board`)"). Each is read with decision 1. Found by a search of `ARCHITECTURE/decisions/` for "point's `step`" and "`correctness_check_sim`"; not claimed complete.
- **The evaluator's definition** (`orchestration/agents/evaluator.md`) names no `step` value (searched) and is not changed.
- **UsageCases and StandardScenarios** (S0 and S1 re-checked): no use case step changes. S0 step 6 (the evaluator's reading of the checks) and S1's round ≥ 1 feedback keep their verdicts; for the per-operation instances of S0 step 4 the readers now read the checks those instances will write. Verdicts unchanged, no gap.
