## Context

- **The question as it was left** (cited: ADR-0071 Boundaries): among what is not decided, "whether a 'not converged' measurement may serve as `against` (OD-13 sub-question ②)". ADR-0113 answered ② for a best and the baseline of round 0 in the same scope and left this part open, because `against` "is the base of a hypothesis verdict (OD-30), not a best or a baseline" (cited: ADR-0113 Consequences).
- **The entry** (cited: ADR-0071 decision 3): a `comparison` entry is "one application of ADR-0007 decision 7", with `measured` "the B of the pair" and `against` "the A of the pair"; `difference` is `lower` or `higher` when the pair is different and `not_different` when it is not. A *ref* names an attempt and `point` the place; which execution an entry stands for when an attempt has several at one point is OD-18 sub-question ⑳ (cited: ADR-0071 decision 3).
- **What ADR-0071 decision 4 already says** (cited): a counted comparison is one at `board` whose `measured` and `against` both have a `check` at `board` with `outcome` `pass`; "A broken timing run has no measurement (ADR-0063 decision 3), so no comparison entry can name it; a 'not converged' measurement is 'usable and marked' (cited: ADR-0007 decision 4) and is not excluded here." `supported`: every `expected` entry has at least one counted comparison of its metric, and every counted comparison of an expected metric is as expected. `refuted`: at least one is contrary and none is as expected. `inconclusive`: every other case. "'Not different' never refutes here." And, open: "which attempt `against` must be … and which comparisons a verdict must include stays open with the base".
- **The comparison rule** (cited: ADR-0007 decision 7): for A with samples x and B with samples y, each bootstrap replicate draws from x and from y "independently and with replacement" and computes r = T(y\*) / T(x\*); the pair is different only if the whole 95 % interval lies above 1 + `delta` or below 1 / (1 + `delta`). A not-converged measurement "enters decision 7 like any other: its spread widens the interval there, so it is rarely called different from anything" (cited: ADR-0007 decision 4). With 5 to 10 samples the interval is approximate (cited: ADR-0007 decision 10).
- **How the mark reaches a reader of the scope** (cited): a result of `sim.run` or `board.run` holds `outcome` `"converged"`, `"not_converged"` or `"broken"`, and its intervals exactly when it is completed (ADR-0063 decisions 2 and 4).
- **Requirements** (cited): FR-17, a verdict judges whether the experiment supports or refutes the hypothesis or cannot judge, "지지 판정은 검증 하네스를 통과한 결과에만 기댄다" (markup simplified), and "evaluate에 이른 라운드는 판정 없이 끝나지 않는다". NFR-3, variance is taken into account "반복 측정/신뢰구간 등으로" and a run in an imprecise environment continues "상대 수치로", with "개선 판정은 ADR-0007 그대로".

## Decision

### 1. Scope and terms

- **Scope**: the scope of ADR-0113 decision 1, unchanged (cited): a measurement held by a completed result of `sim.run` or `board.run` about one attempt of a run that ADR-0061 decision 1 covers. Both execution points are in the scope; whether a comparison at a point counts is ADR-0071 decision 4's, unchanged.
- **To serve as `against`**: the attempt named by `against` of a `comparison` entry at a `point` is one whose result at that point holds the measurement that stands for the A of the pair. "Serve" is this record's word, not the name of any member.
- **Eligible to serve**: nothing about the measurement's timing outcome bars it from serving. As in ADR-0113 decision 1, eligibility is not the choice: **which attempt `against` must be is not part of it** (decision 4).

### 2. What a wide A-side interval does to each value

This is derived from the cited rules; it is not a further rule.

- **The interval.** The ratio's replicates draw A and B independently (ADR-0007 decision 7), so the spread of either side widens the interval, and a wider interval is less often wholly outside the two bounds. **A noisy A therefore makes `not_different` more likely and each of `lower` and `higher` less likely, as a noisy B does.** The form is symmetric (ADR-0007 decision 7); nothing in the rule weighs one side's spread more than the other's.
- **`supported`** can only become less likely. It needs every counted comparison of an expected metric to be as expected; a counted comparison that is `not_different` is not, so it turns a verdict that would be `supported` into `inconclusive` ("as-expected comparisons mixed with others", or "only no-difference comparisons").
- **`refuted`** becomes less likely in the ordinary case: a contrary comparison that becomes `not_different` no longer refutes, since "Not different never refutes". **There is one path the other way.** If a verdict counts a contrary comparison and a second comparison that would be as expected, the value is `inconclusive`; if noise turns the second into `not_different`, the value is `refuted` (one contrary, none as expected). The refutation then rests on the contrary comparison, which is a counted comparison called different; what the noise does is hide a comparison that would have stood against it.
- **`inconclusive`** gains from both, except on the one path above, where it loses to `refuted`.
- **So the cost of a noisy A falls mostly on how often a verdict other than `inconclusive` is reached, and not wholly.** The one path to `refuted` exists for every `not_different` comparison in a mixed set, whatever made it `not_different`: a noisy B, a noisy A, or a true difference inside the interval. A chance "different" call of a noisy pair is possible with 5 to 10 samples (ADR-0007 decision 10), as for any pair; a wider spread makes it less likely, not more (as ADR-0113 Limit 2 found).
- **The `measured` side is already admitted.** ADR-0071 decision 4 does not exclude a not-converged measurement from a comparison entry ("is not excluded here", quoted in Context), and its Boundaries leave only the `against` side open. This record reads that as admitting a not-converged `measured`. By the first bullet, a noisy `measured` has the same effect on the interval and on every value as a noisy `against`.
- **How this differs from ADR-0113.** For a best or a baseline, ADR-0113 found that a noisy base makes it harder to show any change in either direction, with a sticky best as the cost (ADR-0113 decision 2 and Limit 1). The effect on the interval is the same here. What differs is what `not_different` leads to. For a best, a pair that is not different is "never an improvement" (cited: ADR-0007 decision 7): the incumbent stays. For a verdict, a `not_different` comparison is one input to a rule over all the verdict's counted comparisons, and it can move the value in both directions: from `supported` to `inconclusive`, and, on the one path above, from `inconclusive` to `refuted`.

### 3. The answer: the mark does not bar a measurement from `against`

- **A measurement whose result has `outcome` `"not_converged"` is eligible to serve as `against`, as a measurement whose result has `outcome` `"converged"` is. A comparison entry whose `against` is such a measurement counts under ADR-0071 decision 4 exactly when that decision says it counts** — at `board`, with a `check` at `board` with `outcome` `pass` for both sides — and is read against `expected` as any other. The mark adds no condition and needs nothing first: no re-measurement and no flag that must be cleared.
- **Why** (this record's own):
  - *Barring `against` alone would not remove the effect.* Decision 2 shows that a noisy A does to the interval and to every value what a noisy B does, and ADR-0071 decision 4 already admits a noisy B. A bar on one side only would leave the same effect in place through the other.
  - *The rule already turns noise into `inconclusive`.* ADR-0071 decision 4 leads a verdict whose counted comparisons are only `not_different` to `inconclusive`, and never lets `not_different` refute, and a `supported` needs every counted comparison to agree. The one path to `refuted` in decision 2 needs a counted comparison that was called different against the expectation, and it exists for every `not_different` comparison, not only for those with a noisy A. NFR-3 asks that variance be taken into account "반복 측정/신뢰구간 등으로" (cited), which this record reads, as ADR-0113 did, as an interval and not a cut-off.
  - *A bar would be inconsistent with ADR-0113 if the base is fixed as a best or the baseline.* ADR-0071 decision 4 names the basis attempt, the incumbent of `best_ref`, the baseline of OD-28 and another candidate of the round as possible bases (cited). ADR-0113 lets a not-converged measurement stand as a best and as the baseline of round 0. If the base is later fixed as one of those two and such a measurement could not serve as `against`, a hypothesis tested against that base would have no counted comparison and would be `inconclusive` whatever its candidates did, or would need another base or a re-measurement.
  - *In a noisy environment a bar would leave most verdicts without a base.* The run goes on in such an environment (owner, O1, quoted in ADR-0113 Context). Where most measurements end not converged, most comparisons made under this answer are `not_different` (Limit 1), but a large enough effect can still be called different; under a bar, most comparisons would have no eligible A side at all and could show no effect, however large (this record's own weighing). FR-17 is met either way, since `inconclusive` is a verdict (ADR-0071 decision 3); what differs is what the verdicts can say.
  - *It needs nothing that is still open* (Alternatives), and it holds under every choice of base, because it is a property of the measurement and not of which attempt is named.

### 4. What the answer does not touch

- **Which attempt `against` must be** (the base of the comparison), **which comparisons a verdict must include**, a base that is not an attempt of the run, `best_ref` and promotion: open (cited: ADR-0071 decision 4 and Boundaries; OD-20, OD-28, OD-29, OD-30). The answer is not an input to them beyond saying that the mark does not bar a measurement from `against`. **Whether a verdict may leave out a comparison** — and so turn an `inconclusive` that rests on a noisy `not_different` into a `supported` — goes with which comparisons a verdict must include, and is not decided here.
- **Other bars stay as they are** (cited, nothing added): a broken result has no measurement and cannot be named (ADR-0071 decision 4; ADR-0063 decision 3); a comparison whose side has no `check` at `board` with `outcome` `pass` is not counted, which covers a measurement isolated under FR-1 ⑶ (ADR-0071 decision 4); a sim comparison counts for nothing (ADR-0071 decision 4).
- **The three values and their rule**, "Not different never refutes", and an equivalence rule that would let `not_different` refute (OD-13; ADR-0071 Boundaries): unchanged and not decided.
- **The paired re-measurement** (OD-13 sub-question ①) is not decided. If it is adopted, a re-measured base is another measurement, and this record applies to it as to any other.
- **A run found imprecise.** A not-converged measurement makes its run one found imprecise from its end line on (cited: ADR-0113 decision 4, which reads ADR-0103 decisions 3 and 5 and ADR-0104 decision 4). Whether a verdict, its cause or a case built from it (FR-18) carries a warning or may be reused is OD-13 sub-question ⑪ (다) and (라), not decided here.

### 5. Limits

1. **Fewer verdicts reached.** In a noisy environment most comparisons may be `not_different`, and most verdicts `inconclusive`. This is the cost decision 2 finds. O1's warning that improvements "may be hard to establish" is about improvements, not verdicts, and this record does not extend it; whether a verdict carries a warning is OD-13 sub-question ⑪ (다) and (라) (decision 4). The registered remedy, the paired re-measurement of ①, is open.
2. **A hidden counter-comparison.** On the path of decision 2, a verdict can be `refuted` where a less noisy pair would have given `inconclusive`. The path is the rule's for every `not_different` comparison, and this record neither adds nor removes it.
3. **Outside the scope** — a simulator, a board, another configuration — ② stays open (OD-13 sub-question ③).
