## Context

- `correctness.check` is journaled, and the two checks of an attempt are the steps `correctness_check_sim` and `correctness_check_board` (cited: ADR-0040 decisions 2 and 3). `execution` "counts the executions of that step for that target", and "Running the step again is a new identity with a higher `execution`" (cited: ADR-0011 decision 3). A further `execution` is a new check whose "outcome can differ from that of the earlier check by its inputs alone" (cited: ADR-0040 decision 3).
- ADR-0040 Limits: "A caller can re-check until a pass is stored. … A kernel that is right for some inputs and wrong for others (ADR-0021 decision 6) then passes at some `execution`. This record does not stop it and does not say which of several ended checks of one attempt at one point counts. **Said plainly: this is a hole in the hard floor of FR-9.**" (cited).
- FR-9: "정확도는 hard floor(게이트)이며 점수의 한 축이 아니다 — 실패는 감점이 아니라 복귀·탈락이다"; "검증을 통과하지 못한 결과는 best·통계에 반영하지 않는다"; "board가 최종 판정" (cited; markup simplified). FR-1 has the caller act on the outcome at each point: ⑴ sim failure → repair, ⑵ board failure → elimination, ⑶ sim pass and board failure → quarantine and triage (cited).
- A check that ends holds exactly one of `result` and `failure` (cited: ADR-0011 decision 3). A result reports `"pass"`, `"fail"` or `"broken"`, and "only a pass counts (FR-9)" (cited: ADR-0042 decision 2, quoting ADR-0021 decision 5). A broken run "is neither pass nor fail", "nothing counts as verified by it (FR-9)", and each of its conditions "can be caused by the kernel" (cited: ADR-0021 decision 5). An end line with a `failure` means "the step is over without an outcome" (cited: ADR-0047 decision 3, from ADR-0011 decision 4). The kinds it can hold today are the tool's own `operation_answer_invalid` (ADR-0045 decision 4), `operation_failure_invalid` (ADR-0047 decision 4) and `build_log_unwritable` (ADR-0078 decision 7), and the backend kinds `no_kernel_source` and `kernel_source_unreadable` (ADR-0056 decision 6) and `build_directory_unusable`, `settings_refused`, `build_input_missing`, `compiler_unavailable`, `build_output_missing` and `executable_unstartable` (ADR-0060 decision 4) (all cited). ADR-0101 decision 2 names the same eleven as the stored kinds of the two `step` values, and its decision 3 says "The meaning of a stored kind is fixed and never changes" and that a stored `failure` is told apart "by its `kind` alone" (cited). Of `build_log_unwritable` ADR-0078 decision 7 says that the step ends with it "in place of whatever the operation returned or raised" (cited).
- Beside the four conditions of ADR-0021 decision 5, a broken result can report `build_exit_status` (a compile or link failure) or `build_time_limit` (a build past its time limit): "A result: `outcome` `broken` with a new value of `condition`" (cited: **owner**, HQ-0020 point 1, as ADR-0060 renders it).
- "Such a kernel breaks the contract (ADR-0003 decision 2) and is a defect of the candidate" — said of a kernel that passes one check and fails the next (cited: ADR-0021 decision 6).

## Decision

### 1. Scope and readers

- **The checks**: the calls of `correctness.check` about one attempt — one triple `round`, `candidate`, `attempt` — of a run the contract serves: a run of the first case and a run whose hw_profile names the mock (cited: ADR-0021 decision 1; ADR-0038 decision 6). **One execution point**: one of the two `step` values. A check of a repaired attempt is a check of another attempt (cited: ADR-0040 decision 3) and has a standing of its own.
- **Who reads the rule**: the consumer of check outcomes that FR-1 names — what returns an attempt to code generation (⑴), eliminates a candidate (⑵), quarantines and triages (⑶) and promotes — and what keeps an unverified result out of the best and the statistics (FR-9). **This record names no component**: the Round-Manager and the evaluator are PLAN P2-1 and P2-3, and the roster is OD-23.
- The terms of ADR-0011 (identity, start line, end line, open, ended), ADR-0021 (a check) and ADR-0038 (execution point) are used unchanged.

### 2. The checks that count

- **Every ended check of the attempt at that point whose end line holds a result counts, whatever its `execution` and wherever its lines stand in the log.** None is set aside because a later or an earlier one exists.
- **An end line with a `failure` never counts for the attempt**: it establishes no outcome (Context). What it does beside that depends on whether a verdict on drawn inputs can have existed in its check. This is told by its kind and by the records that name the kind:

| Class | Kinds today | Why | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| **(i) no executable of the check was started** | `no_kernel_source`, `kernel_source_unreadable` (S1, S2), `build_directory_unusable` (S3), `build_input_missing` (S5), `compiler_unavailable` (S6), `build_output_missing` (S9), `executable_unstartable` (S10) | Each situation lies before the verification executable is started, or is the failure to start it (cited: ADR-0056 decision 5; ADR-0060 decision 4). No kernel ran on drawn inputs, so no verdict is hidden | counts neither for nor against |
| **(ii) a verdict may have existed** | `build_log_unwritable`, `operation_answer_invalid`, `operation_failure_invalid`, `settings_refused`, and any kind a later record adds unless that record places its situations in (i) | `build_log_unwritable` stands "in place of whatever the operation returned or raised" (cited: ADR-0078 decision 7). The two tool kinds do not say how far the operation came. `settings_refused` includes a setting of the process refused after the process was started (cited: ADR-0060 decision 4, S4) | counts against nothing, and keeps the attempt from being verified at that point, as an open identity does (decision 3) |

- **An open identity is a check whose outcome is not known.** It counts against nothing, and it keeps the attempt from being verified at that point (decision 3).
- **An end line without a start line** — which the fold of ADR-0011 decision 6 also "counted and reported" (cited) — counts like any other end line.
- **Two end lines for one identity** — two turns at once, which ADR-0011 decision 9 does not prevent, and of which the spend fold of its decision 6 says that the second end line "is counted and reported" (cited) — both count here as well.
- The checks are found **in the complete lines of the run's `events.ndjson` alone**: the `step_started` and `step_ended` lines whose envelope numbers are the attempt's and whose `step` is the point's. Nothing of `STATE.json`, a result file or a request is needed, and the order of the lines does not matter. A torn tail is no line (cited: ADR-0006 decision 6). What a reader does with a damaged line stays as that decision says.

### 3. The standing of the attempt at that point

The standing is one of three. Its names are words of this record, **not values of any file or member**.

| Standing | When | What it means for the hard floor |
|---|---|---|
| **not verified** | at least one counting check reports `"fail"` or `"broken"` | The attempt does not pass the floor at that point, and no further check changes that (for a fail whose cause FR-1 ⑶ treats as not established, see below) |
| **verified** | no counting check reports `"fail"` or `"broken"`, at least one reports `"pass"`, no identity of that point is open, and no end line holds a `failure` of class (ii) | The attempt passes the floor at that point — the only standing that does |
| **undetermined** | every other case: no line; only end lines with a `failure`; or passes beside an open identity or a `failure` of class (ii) | The attempt does not pass the floor at that point, for lack of an outcome |

- **Why a fail stands.** A kernel that passes one check and fails another "breaks the contract" and "is a defect of the candidate" (cited: ADR-0021 decision 6). A later pass on other inputs does not take a fail back. With this rule a further check can keep or lose verification and never gain it after a fail, so **the counting part of the hole that ADR-0040 Limits names is closed within the attempt**: no `execution` turns a recorded fail into a pass (a new attempt: Limits). What keeps a caller from asking stays open (decision 6).
- **A fail whose cause is not established.** FR-1 ⑶ treats a sim pass followed by a board failure as "원인 미확정": "해당 board 측정은 격리하고(기록은 남기되 record로 인정하지 않음)", and the triage sorts the cause into "후보 결함 / board 결함 / 시뮬레이터 결함" (cited). A sim-pass/board-fail of this kind "can then come from the drawn inputs" (cited: ADR-0021 decision 6). **The standing at the board point is then not verified, and no further check at that point changes it.** A further pass does not settle the triage, and the board measurement is not recognised either way. **Whether a fail or broken result that the triage attributes to the board or to the simulator still counts once that cause is established is not decided here** (FR-1 ⑶; OD-10). This record does not anticipate the triage or its recovery.
- **Why a broken result stands with a fail.** Each of the four conditions of a completed run can be caused by the kernel (Context). If a broken result were set aside, a kernel that crashes or hangs on some inputs could be re-checked until it passes — the same hole. The two build conditions involve no drawn input. A compile or link failure is a fact about the attempt's sources under its settings, and the owner made both build conditions results — outcomes about the attempt — and not tool failures (Context). This record does not sort results again by cause, which the harness cannot establish (ADR-0021 decision 5), so all six conditions stand. The cost: a defect of the harness, or an environment that slows a build past its limit, also leaves a right kernel not verified at that point (Limits). That cost is weighed as the lesser one: it shows on every attempt it touches and leaves nothing unverified in the best.
- **Why an open identity keeps the attempt from being verified.** Whether its check had an outcome is not known (cited: ADR-0011 decision 4), and a death during the judging loses an outcome that existed (cited: ADR-0040 Limits). "Until a recovery rule exists (OD-10, the retry part of OD-17), an open step is a point the run does not pass by itself" (cited: ADR-0011 Consequences); this standing follows that sentence and adds no recovery rule. A fail beside an open identity still gives not verified, because the open check cannot take a fail back. **A `failure` of class (ii) is treated alike for the same reason**: a verdict may have existed in its check and is not known. **Unlike an open identity, it is permanent.** Its line is ended and is never edited (cited: ADR-0006 decision 5), and no recovery rule turns an ended identity back into an outcome. An attempt with such a line can never again be verified at that point. The ways out are a new attempt (for example a repair) or a later record that amends this decision.
- **A call that ended with `end_line_failed`** leaves its identity open or ended (cited: ADR-0026 decision 4). The standing reads the log as it is.
- **What follows a standing is not decided here.** Verified is the standing on which FR-9 lets the attempt be compared, promoted and kept at that point. Where not verified rests on a fail, FR-1 ⑴ to ⑶ apply as written, the quarantine and triage of ⑶ included. Where it rests on broken only, which of them applies is not decided ("What anybody does after a fail or a broken run is not decided", cited: ADR-0021 decision 5). What follows undetermined — waiting, recovering an open step, asking for a check — is OD-10, the retry part of OD-17 and the second part of ⑭.

### 4. The alternatives, weighed

| Alternative | The hole of ADR-0040 Limits; FR-9's floor | A `failure`; a `broken` result; an open identity | Found from the log alone (ADR-0011) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **(a) The first ended check counts** | Closes the counting part: a later pass does not override a first fail. But a later fail does not count either, so a recorded proof of a wrong kernel is ignored. That is no floor for a kernel that was lucky once | "First" must say whether a `failure` is a first check; if it is, the attempt has no outcome for good, and if it is not, re-checking after a `failure` chooses which check counts. A first broken result decides alone. An open first identity leaves "first" unknown until it is recovered | Only with an order: by `execution` (caller-given and possibly with gaps) or by line position (which differs from it when two turns run at once). The two orders can name different checks |
| **(b) The last ended check counts** | Leaves the hole open: a caller re-checks until a pass and stops | A trailing `failure` either erases an outcome or must be skipped by a rule. A broken result is overridden by the next pass. A check that is still open makes "last" unknown | Needs an order, as (a), and a further check changes which one counts |
| **(c) Every ended check counts** (chosen) | Closes the counting part; any fail or broken stands. Matches FR-9 as a floor and #5 A-6 ("must pass verification") | A `failure` never counts for; one of class (ii) blocks verified like an open identity; broken stands with fail; an open identity blocks verified and does not cancel a fail | Yes: one pass over the matching complete lines, in any order |
| **(d) Any pass counts** | The hole itself: one pass among any number of fails verifies the attempt | A `failure` and broken are ignored; an open identity does not matter | Yes, but it reads the log in a way that loses the floor |

- **Four variants of (c) were weighed and not taken.** *(c′) An open identity does not block verified*: this drops the stop of ADR-0011 Consequences for one tool and verifies an attempt whose lost outcome may have been a fail. *(c″) A broken result counts neither way, like a `failure`*: this reopens the hole for a kernel that crashes or hangs on some inputs (decision 3). *(c‴) Every `failure` counts neither way*: a `failure` of class (ii) may stand in place of a fail, and a later pass would then verify the attempt, which is the reason (c′) is not taken. What (c‴) saves is the permanent block of decision 3 after a class (ii) failure. That block is weighed against the floor: the block costs a new attempt after an event that neither the candidate nor the checks can choose (an unwritable log file, a backend that does not answer the contract), and (c‴) would let a lost verdict, perhaps a fail, be replaced by a later pass. FR-9 makes accuracy a floor and #5 A-6 says a candidate "must pass verification" (Context), so the cost is taken. *(c⁗) Every `failure` blocks verified*: this pays the same permanent block also after a class (i) failure, where no executable was started and no verdict can be hidden — one missing compiler would end every attempt it touched for nothing.
- **Not weighed as a rule of this record**: a majority or a share of passes, or a required number of passes. Each would put a number on the strength of the floor, and no fixed input gives one (the number of draws is not fixed either: ADR-0046 decision 7).

### 5. The two points stay apart

- **The standing is computed per point, from the checks of that point's `step` alone.** A board check never changes the standing at the sim point, and a sim check never changes it at the board point. This follows ADR-0038 decision 4, under which the two checks of an attempt are two identities that differ in `step`, and FR-1 ⑴ to ⑶, which give each point its own consequence (both cited).
- **This record does not merge the two.** "board가 최종 판정" (cited: FR-9) and FR-1 ⑶ say what a sim standing beside a board standing means: verified at the sim point and not verified at the board point is FR-1 ⑶ as written, with its quarantine and triage (decision 3). How the consumer reads the two together beyond that is FR-1's and PLAN P2-1's.

### 6. The second part of ⑭ — a finding, not an answer

- The first part could be answered without a hook, a gate, a retry rule, a number of draws or a re-verification rule: the rule above only reads the log. **Nothing in it stops a caller from asking for a further `execution`**, and this record adds nothing that does. What is reduced is the reason to ask: a further check can no longer turn a fail into a pass. A caller can still use further checks at an attempt that has passed and has no fail. That is no path past the floor, but it costs runs and may leave an open identity or a `failure` of class (ii), and the latter makes a verified attempt undetermined for good (Limits).
- **Whether the re-verification that FR-9 asks for before a target of FR-3 ④ is counted as reached is "a further `execution` of one of the two steps, or something a later record defines", is not decided** (cited: ADR-0040 Limits; OD-16 sub-question ⑤). If a later record makes it one, the rule here counts its outcome like any other. If a later record makes it something else, that record says how it combines with the standing.

### 7. Limits

- **A caller can still ask for further checks**, and each draws inputs and costs a run. Nothing here stops it (decision 6).
- **The rule holds within one attempt.** A repair call raises `attempt` (cited: ADR-0040 decision 3, from ADR-0002 decision 5), and the new attempt starts with a standing of its own, also where its kernel source did not change. So a kernel that is right for some inputs and wrong for others can still reach a pass through a new attempt. What makes a new attempt and how many are allowed is not decided here (FR-1 ⑴; OD-19; the second part of ⑭). The counting part of the hole is closed in this sense only.
- **A further check of a verified attempt makes it undetermined while it runs, and keeps it undetermined if the process dies.** That is the price of decision 3's open-identity rule, until a recovery rule exists.
- **A `start_line_failed` after the line feed** leaves "an open identity under which nothing ran and which is not started again" (cited: ADR-0026 decision 7). Under decision 3 it keeps the attempt undetermined at that point until a recovery rule exists, although no check took place.
- **A defect of the harness that breaks a run, or a build past its time limit for reasons of the machine, marks a right kernel not verified** at that point. Nothing here tells it from a defect of the kernel (cited: ADR-0021 decision 5).
- **A `failure` of class (ii) keeps an attempt from being verified at that point for good**, also where nothing was wrong with the kernel — for example a log file that could not be written, or a backend that raised a kind outside the contract. A further check of a verified attempt can end that way as well. What remains is a new attempt or a later record that amends decision 3.
- **A class (i) `failure` counts neither way even where it reflects the attempt.** ADR-0060 decision 4 says that S9 and S10 "may follow from what the attempt's source made" (cited). No verdict on drawn inputs is hidden by them, so the floor is not passed by them; what follows such a failure is not decided here.
- **A backend that turns a fail or a broken run into a tool failure of a class (i) kind** hides that check from the standing, and a later pass then verifies the attempt. Turned into a class (ii) kind, the check leaves the attempt undetermined at that point for good instead. ADR-0016 decision 6 already says that a backend that does not answer the contract is not detected by the frame (cited), and ADR-0096 keeps one known path from doing it.
- **Each result is taken as it stands**, judged under the tolerance, the number of draws and the shape its call read. A file changed between two checks gives them different ones (cited: ADR-0021 decision 6; ADR-0046 Limits; ADR-0041 Walk-through: "Two ended checks of one attempt at one point are then about two shapes (decision 9)"), and the standing does not judge them again.
- **For an attempt of an earlier round**, a reader needs lines before the stored position. A fold from the first line "is always allowed" (cited: ADR-0011 decision 6); what is read at the entry of a turn is OD-17 sub-question ①.
- **The point a call states is believed** (cited: ADR-0038 decision 8), and a caller that states the numbers of another attempt "records the check under them" (cited: ADR-0040 Limits). By this record's rule, such a check counts for the point and attempt under which it was recorded.

## Alternatives considered

| Alternative | Why not |
|---|---|
| The first ended check counts | Decision 4 (a): ignores a later fail; "first" needs an order the log does not fix uniquely, and a `failure` or an open check as the first leaves it unresolved. |
| The last ended check counts | Decision 4 (b): leaves the hole of ADR-0040 Limits open. |
| Any pass counts | Decision 4 (d): is the hole. |
| Every ended check counts, but an open identity does not block verified | Decision 4 (c′). |
| Every ended check counts, but a broken result counts neither way | Decision 4 (c″). |
| Every `failure` counts neither way, or every `failure` blocks verified | Decision 4 (c‴) and (c⁗). |
| A majority or a required number of passes | Puts a number on the strength of the floor that no fixed input gives (decision 4). |
| Leave the first part open until the second part is answered | The first part needs none of the hooks, gates or retry rules of the second (decision 6), and a code slice and the consumer of PLAN P2-1 need the rule. |
| Store the standing in a member of a file or a result | A data-plane change (CLAUDE.md A-13); the standing follows from the log and need not be stored. |

## Consequences

- **A code slice this makes possible** (code mode, a later task): a pure function over the complete lines of a run's log, or over the step lines of one attempt, that returns the standing of the attempt at a point. It needs nothing that does not exist yet: the log and its line kinds are there (observation: `hilclaw/journal.py` names `step_ended`). It waits only for a caller, the consumer of PLAN P2-1 and P2-3. **No module or function is named as binding.**
