# ADR-0075: Hypotheses injected at instance start — what is injected and its form, the member of `instance.json` that records it, and the admission of `origin` `injected` with the member that points to it (one more part of OD-30)

## Status

## Decision

A value is **cited** when a source is named with it. Everything else is **this record's own** design: the member names `injections` and `injection`, the kinds `hypothesis` and `material` with their members, types and rules, the choice of `instance.json` as the place, that a written hypothesis keeps its statement verbatim and states no basis or expected effect, that material is given without a version, that the entries are closed, the in-file and cross-file rules, the two readings of ADR-0059 decision 3's "derived from material the user gave" (decisions 1 and 3), and the reading of FR-11 in decision 4.

### 1. What is injected at start, and its form

- **An injected input is one entry of `injections`** (decision 2), an object with exactly the members of its `kind` (*name*, *string* of ADR-0001 decision 1; *text* of ADR-0002 decision 2):

| `kind` | Members | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `hypothesis` | `id` (*name*), `kind`, `statement` (*text*) | A hypothesis the user wrote: FR-17 ①, what is changed and what it is expected to improve, as the user states it. |
| `material` | `id` (*name*), `kind`, `source` (*string*) | Material from which a hypothesis is to be derived (FR-15: "외부 연구 논문 링크 등"): the identifier of the material as the user gives it. |

- **`id`** is unique within `injections` and names the input for the run: an input is identified by (`run_id`, `id`). The entries' order carries no meaning.
- **A written hypothesis states only its statement.** Its `basis` and `expected` are written by the selection role when a round tests it (decision 3), by the rules of ADR-0059 decision 3 that bind every hypothesis — so the injected hypothesis passes through the same record, verification and verdict as a system one (owner, point 4). Why not more: an `attempt` basis names a coder call, and none exists at start; a `case` basis must name a case of the round's `cases_read` (ADR-0073 decision 2), which the lookup gives a round's selection, not the start (ADR-0073 decision 1); material the user rests a hypothesis on is a `material` entry of its own, whose version is recorded where it is read (next bullet); an `expected` beside the `statement` would be a second statement of the same expectation, which could disagree with it. The cost: the structured expected effect of an injected hypothesis is the selection's reading of the user's statement. It is still written before the measurement (ADR-0059 decision 1), so it cannot be fitted to the result. Likewise its `basis` states what the selection tests it against, not the user's reason; and the round-1 gap of ADR-0059 decision 3 applies to it too — with no attempt of round 0 (OD-28), no case and no material, no basis can be recorded.
- **Material states only its identifier, not its version.** FR-14 ② asks for the identifier and version of material "실제 참조한"; the version is known when the material is read, and requiring it at start would fix that the material is read at start, which is part of who derives a hypothesis and when (Boundaries). The version is recorded where the material is used: in the `external` basis entry of each hypothesis derived from it, whose `version` ADR-0059 decision 3 already requires (decision 3 below). How `source` relates to the source registry of FR-14 ① is OD-12; the cost of *string* is ADR-0059's (an identifier over 255 characters or outside printable ASCII does not fit).
- **Injected material and reference material.** FR-15 also lets an instance start with reference cases and material ("참고 사례·자료", UC-1 step 1), a hint like the cases, and gives a paper link as an example of both. This record's reading: the "material the user gave" of ADR-0059 decision 3 is material given **as an injection**, an entry of `injections`. A hypothesis that the runtime draws from reference material given otherwise is `origin` `system`, resting on an `external` basis entry, as one drawn from material it found itself (ADR-0059 decision 3). The form and place of that reference input is not decided here (Boundaries).
- **Nothing here says who derives a hypothesis from material, when, or whether it can be derived**: a material entry is an input; that no round ever derives a hypothesis from it is a valid state (decision 4).

### 2. Where it is recorded

- **An optional top-level member `injections` of `runs/<run_id>/instance.json`**: an array of at least one entry of decision 1. **Absent**: the instance records no injected input — the start of the instance had none to record; nothing more is inferred. An empty array is invalid, so absence is the one form of "none".
- **Why `instance.json`**: injection is an input of the instance's start (FR-15; UC-1 step 1 lists "(선택) 주입할 가설" among what the user starts an instance with), and `instance.json` records "What the instance was started with" and is written once at the start (cited: ADR-0004 path table; ADR-0025) — so the input cannot be added to or altered after the run began, which matches "at start". FR-14 ② names the "instance 기록" for the identifiers of material.
- **Versions**: a top-level member that may be absent and may be passed over does not raise the `schema_version` of `instance.json` (cited: ADR-0018 decision 2). Its entries are closed: adding a member to an entry or a value of `kind` changes the member's form and raises that version (ADR-0018 decision 2: a defined member's "type, form or meaning changes"). Why closed: a member a reader passes over could change what an input is (a version of material), and a reader that passed over it would act wrongly. **The cost**: every later extension of an input given at start — a `version` of material, a kind that names a case, any further kind — raises the `schema_version` of `instance.json`, which is coupled to the layout version (ADR-0024 decision 3), and reading a file of another version is OD-11. A reader that passes over it misreads no member it knows: the HW tools that read the file need none of its content, and the reader that needs it — the selection role — is built after this record. No path, file name or number form changes, so the layout version stays 1 and so does the `schema_version` tied to it (cited: ADR-0004 decision 9; ADR-0024 decision 3).
- **What writes it is not decided here**: the start of the instance writes the file, once (cited: ADR-0004); what component that is, how the user's input reaches it, and when, stay sub-question ⑰ of OD-8 and OD-23. By ADR-0025 decision 2 the content is valid by the records of the file before it is written; with this record a content whose `injections` breaks decision 1 or 2 is not valid, so it is not written.
- **A member that is present must be valid** (as ADR-0020 decision 3 has it for `tolerance`). A file whose `injections` breaks decision 1 or 2 is **invalid** in the sense of ADR-0018 decision 2: a reader of this record reports it, uses no member of it — not its `hw_profile` either — and repairs nothing. For a call of a HW tool such a file is the situation "invalid by decision 2" of ADR-0018 decision 4 ([ADR-0019](ADR-0019-od18-unusable-run-kind.md) decision 3, `run_file_invalid`), though the tool needs nothing of the member. Why: an injection read in part would change what the user asked to be tested. The writer does not write such a content (above), so this arises only for a file changed after the start or written by a writer that does not follow ADR-0025.
- **The costs.** A misspelt member name (`injectoins`, `injected_inputs`) is passed over by a version 1 validator and, since the file is never rewritten, the injection is lost for the life of the run (cited: ADR-0018 decision 2, "The cost"); the start of the instance is the place to show the user what was recorded (Boundaries). A call of a HW tool that reads the file reads it whole, now with the users' statements in it, and keeps nothing between calls (cited: ADR-0024 decision 5; ADR-0018 decision 4): a valid member costs it bytes, and an invalid one stops it (above). **The set of valid version 1 files becomes narrower without a new version**: a file holding an `injections` that breaks this record was valid by ADR-0018 alone and is invalid now; every other file valid before this record stays valid and means the same, and nothing writes a member of that name today (name check under Consequences). Every persistent artefact keeps a `schema_version` (NFR-7 ②): this member is under that of `instance.json`.

### 3. The admission of `origin` `injected` in `hypotheses.json`

- **`origin` `injected` is admitted** (ADR-0059 decision 3) together with one member that only entries of that value carry: **`injection`** (*name*), the `id` of the entry of `injections` in the run's `instance.json` that the hypothesis comes from. An entry with `origin` `injected` has exactly the members of ADR-0059 decision 3 and `injection`; an entry with `origin` `system` has no `injection`. Every other rule of ADR-0059 decisions 2 to 5 binds an injected entry as it binds a system one: at least one `basis` entry, at least one `expected` entry, `tested_by` within `candidates`.
- **In-file rules** (one `hypotheses.json` shows them): the member's presence follows `origin`, and its value is a *name*. Nothing more: whether that name exists, and what kind of input it names, are in another file.
- **Rules between files**, against the `instance.json` of the same run:
  - `injection` names the `id` of an entry of that file's `injections`;
  - if that entry is of kind `hypothesis`, the hypothesis entry's `statement` equals its `statement` (the same JSON string), and no other entry of the same `hypotheses.json` names it — one hypothesis tested by several candidates is one entry with several `tested_by` values (ADR-0059 decision 4);
  - if that entry is of kind `material`, the hypothesis entry has at least one `basis` entry of kind `external` whose `source` equals the material's `source`, and that entry's `version` is the version of the material actually read (FR-14 ②; FR-15 "식별자+버전으로 기록한다"). Several hypotheses of a file may come from one material.
  - Checked by no write: the write of `hypotheses.json` reads no other file (cited: ADR-0059 decision 1). A reader that holds both files can check them, as `hilclaw/hypotheses_cases.py` does for the rules of ADR-0073; a pair that breaks them is reported. What checks them and when is OD-18 and OD-23. That the `version` is that of the material read cannot be checked from the two files.
- **Why verbatim for a written hypothesis**: the verdict is then of the user's hypothesis, not of a paraphrase (owner: "the same experiment, verification and verdict"). A narrower or altered variant that the selection forms from it is, by this record's reading, the selection's own hypothesis: `origin` `system`, resting on what its `basis` names. FR-15 tells a written hypothesis apart from material "가설을 도출할", so ADR-0059's "derived from material the user gave" is not read to cover a variant of a written one. The cost: no member links such a variant to the input it came from; a `basis` kind that cites an earlier hypothesis is open (ADR-0059 Boundaries). **Why a pointer and not a copy**: the input is in `instance.json` already; the pointer identifies it and the statement shows it.
- **A later round may test an injected hypothesis again**: each test is a new entry with its own (`run_id`, `round`, `id`) and the same `injection`. Nothing here requires or forbids it, or says which round tests what (Boundaries).
- **Version**: admitting a value ADR-0059 defines, with the member only its entries carry, does not raise the version of `hypotheses.json` (cited: ADR-0059 decision 2), as ADR-0073 decision 2 did for `case`. The cost is that decision's: a reader built before this record refuses a file holding `injected`.
- **What reaches the case file**: a case copies `hypotheses.json` whole (cited: ADR-0072 decision 3), so a case may hold `injected` entries with `injection`. The copy keeps `origin` (S11 step 4: "출처와 함께 사례로 저장"); a written hypothesis's statement and a material's `source` and `version` are in the copy itself. The `injection` value names an input of the source run's `instance.json`, which a case does not copy and the lookup does not open (ADR-0073 decision 1; whether another reader opens the source run's files is open, ADR-0072 decision 3 and Boundaries), so in a case it identifies the input and is not resolved by the lookup. The in-file rules of this decision bind the copy (a copy is valid by its own record, ADR-0072 decision 3); the rules between files do not, since a case holds no `instance.json`. The case's schema and version do not change; a case reader built before this record refuses such a case, the cost of ADR-0059 decision 2 carried to the case, as ADR-0073 noted for `case`. A later run that rests a hypothesis on such a case writes `origin` `system` (cited: ADR-0073 decision 2).

### 4. FR-11: the identification of injected hypotheses

- **Each injected hypothesis a round tests is identified in that round's decision log** by its `origin` `injected` and its `injection`, which lead to the input in `instance.json`; the record of the round is part of the decision log (cited: FR-11; ADR-0059 Context). Within the run, whether it was written by the user or derived from material is read from the input's `kind`.
- **An injected input that no round tests is recorded by nothing beyond `injections`**: it is the entry of `injections` that no `injection` of the run names. No round records that it considered and did not test an input. Every selection of the run has the same `injections`, so a per-round member listing them would repeat the file, and a reason for not testing one would be a rule of distribution, which is not decided (Boundaries). Whether a run must test every input, and by when, is that rule too.
- **No rule of share or order follows** from this record: a round may hold only system entries, only injected ones or both, so far as the file's validity goes. FR-17's "시스템은 … 스스로 추가 가설을 세우는 일을 멈추지 않으며" binds the runtime; how it is enforced, and whether each round must hold a system entry, is the distribution question (Boundaries; OD-18).

### 5. Round 0

**No injected hypothesis is tested in round 0**: round `0000` has no hypothesis record and a file whose `round` is 0 is invalid (cited: ADR-0059 decision 5), and it has no verdicts (cited: ADR-0071 decision 6). The earliest round that can test an injected hypothesis is round 1. Whether the bring-up of round 0 uses an injected input in any other way is not decided (Boundaries).

### Example

A run started with one written hypothesis and one material. The `run_id` is a placeholder of the form of ADR-0027 decision 2, given by no source; statements and identifiers are synthetic. The `hw_profile` is the placeholder of ADR-0018's Example, so **the `instance.json` below is the shape of the member and not a valid file** (as the Example of ADR-0053 says of its own); the `injections` member in it is valid by decisions 1 and 2. Path: `runs/20261021t080000z-3c5e7a9b1d2f4608/instance.json`.

```json
{
  "schema_version": 1,
  "hw_profile": {
    "key": "cpu_standin-b0d8610d8f470856",
    "record": {"schema_version": 1, "accelerator": "cpu_standin", "revision": "NOT-DECIDED-HERE",
               "toolchain": {"clang": "NOT-DECIDED-HERE"}, "host": {"cpu": "NOT-DECIDED-HERE", "os": "NOT-DECIDED-HERE"},
               "hw_source": "NOT-DECIDED-HERE"}
  },
  "injections": [
    {"id": "u1", "kind": "hypothesis", "statement": "SYNTHETIC: blocking the inner loop lowers the kernel time"},
    {"id": "u2", "kind": "material", "source": "https://example.org/synthetic-paper"}
  ]
}
```

Round 1 of that run, N = 2: candidate 1 tests the written hypothesis, candidate 2 a system one; no hypothesis of the round comes from `u2`, which is valid (decision 4). It is valid by ADR-0059 decisions 2 to 5 with this record, and the pair is valid by the rules between files of decision 3. The `attempt` entries assume a round-0 attempt `0000/01/01`, which no record yet says exists (OD-28), as ADR-0059's Example does. Path: `runs/20261021t080000z-3c5e7a9b1d2f4608/rounds/0001/decision/hypotheses.json`.

```json
{
  "schema_version": 1, "run_id": "20261021t080000z-3c5e7a9b1d2f4608", "round": 1, "candidates": 2,
  "hypotheses": [
    {"id": "h1", "statement": "SYNTHETIC: blocking the inner loop lowers the kernel time",
     "basis": [{"kind": "attempt", "ref": {"round": 0, "candidate": 1, "attempt": 1}, "text": "NOT-DECIDED-HERE"}],
     "expected": [{"metric": "latency_minimisation", "direction": "lower"}],
     "origin": "injected", "injection": "u1", "tested_by": [1]},
    {"id": "h2", "statement": "NOT-DECIDED-HERE",
     "basis": [{"kind": "attempt", "ref": {"round": 0, "candidate": 1, "attempt": 1}, "text": "NOT-DECIDED-HERE"}],
     "expected": [{"metric": "latency_minimisation", "direction": "lower"}],
     "origin": "system", "tested_by": [2]}
  ]
}
```

Had a later round derived `h3` from `u2`, its entry would carry `"origin": "injected", "injection": "u2"` and a `basis` entry `{"kind": "external", "source": "https://example.org/synthetic-paper", "version": …, "text": …}` with the version of what was read.

## Alternatives considered

| Point | Alternative | Why not |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The user states `statement`, `basis` and `expected` | No attempt exists at start; a `case` basis must be in the round's `cases_read` (ADR-0073 decision 2); material is a `material` entry of its own; an `expected` beside the statement could disagree with it. |
| 1 | A written hypothesis that the selection may reword | The verdict would be of a paraphrase, not of the user's hypothesis; a variant is the selection's own (`system`). |
| 1 | Material with a required `version` at start | It would fix that the material is read at start — part of who derives and when (Boundaries); the version is recorded where the material is used (FR-14 ②). |
| 1 | Material left wholly to a later record | FR-15 counts material as injection and FR-14 ② names the instance record; a pointer form decides neither who derives nor when. |
| 2 | (b) A fifth run-level file beside `instance.json` | ADR-0004 decision 2 names "the four run-level files"; a fifth would change the tree it fixes and so, by this record's reading of ADR-0004 decisions 2 and 9, raise the layout version and with it the `schema_version` of `instance.json` (ADR-0024 decision 3) — a version raise this route does not need. Its writer and its write would also need records of their own. |
| 2 | (c) A file in `rounds/0000/decision/` or in `decision/` of round 1 | Injection is the user's input at start, not a selection's decision; round 0 has no hypothesis record (ADR-0059 decision 5), and a round-1 file would bind injection to round 1. |
| 2 | An empty `injections` allowed | It would say what absence says. |
| 2 | A present but invalid `injections` passed over, the rest of the file used | A run would go on without, or with part of, what the user injected, and nothing would show it; ADR-0020 decision 3 chose the same for `tolerance`. |
| 2 | Entries open, or further kinds defined and not admitted now (the route of ADR-0059 decision 2) | A passed-over member could change what an input is; a kind defined now for injection at a gate or during a run would define what the owner has not put in scope (②, ③), and such inputs arrive after this file is written. |
| 3 | `injected` admitted without a pointer, or with a copy of the input | Without a pointer FR-11 cannot say which input a hypothesis came from (ADR-0059 Alternatives); a copy repeats the input and can disagree with it. |
| 3 | A pointer by index into `injections` | An index is meaningless once the order carries none; an `id` survives reading in any order. |
| 4 | A per-round member listing the inputs considered and not tested, with reasons | It repeats `injections` every round, and a reason is a rule of distribution (Boundaries). |

## Consequences

- **A code slice can be cut** (code mode, P2-6 and P2-7): a validator of `injections` by decisions 1 and 2 beside the other members of `instance.json`; the admission of `injected` with `injection` by decision 3 in `hilclaw/hypotheses.py`; a check of the rules between `hypotheses.json` and `instance.json` of decision 3 for a reader that holds both, after the model of `hilclaw/hypotheses_cases.py`. Names and call forms are that task's. Binding them to an agent, a prompt, a hook or a user interface is not part of it.
- **A-13 reading**: document mode. Added: an optional top-level member at the open top level of `instance.json`, which raises no version (ADR-0018 decision 2), and the admission of a value ADR-0059 defines with the member only its entries carry, which raises none (ADR-0059 decision 2). Unchanged: every member, value and version of `instance.json`, `hypotheses.json`, `verdicts.json`, `STATE.json`, `spec.json`, `eval.json`, `artifacts.json`, `events.ndjson` and the key record files; no file or path is added and the layout version stays 1. The set of valid version 1 `instance.json` files becomes narrower without a new version, as with ADR-0020 (decision 2); no such file exists in a data plane, since nothing writes the file yet. **Reached indirectly**: the case file, which copies `hypotheses.json` whole (ADR-0072 decision 3), may now hold `injected` entries; its schema, its version and the rule of the copy are unchanged, and a case reader built before this record refuses such a case (decision 3). Nothing was built or run, and no `claude` process was started.

## Boundaries (not decided here)

- **Injection at a checkpoint gate** (UC-2 "방향 수정") **and during an autonomous run** — open in OD-30; **whether either is in the MVP** is a scope decision for the owner (HQ, per the OD-30 section; C-6).
- **The order and share of injected and system hypotheses** across rounds and candidates: whether injected ones are tested first, mixed into one round's N candidates, left to the selection's judgement; whether every input must be tested, by when, and whether every round must hold a system hypothesis (FR-17's addendum is a requirement, not a rule of distribution).
- **Who derives a hypothesis from material, and when** — the orchestrator side or the curator flow (UC-5) — and **the coder's own search** (OD-12); the relation of `source` to the source registry of FR-14 ① and whether an excerpt is snapshotted (OD-12); and how material that a selection read but derived no hypothesis from is recorded (FR-14 ②; the reference-evidence record format of OD-12) — decision 1 records the version only in the `external` basis of a hypothesis derived from it.
- **Material that is unavailable** (FR-14 ③): what is recorded when it cannot be read, and whether the run goes on.
- **The catalog boundary**: whether and how a supported injected hypothesis becomes a catalog technique (UC-5, OD-24, OD-7).
- **The reference input at start** (FR-15 "참고 사례·자료", UC-1 step 1) that is not an injection: its form and place, and what a selection does with it. No register row names its form and place in the run's records; that is a follow-up for the integrator.
- **What writes `instance.json`**, how the user's input reaches that writer, and when (OD-8 sub-question ⑰, OD-23); how the start shows the user what was recorded; any input form of a user interface (UC-1).
- **Round 0's use of an injected input** in bring-up (FR-1, OD-28).
- **Other OD-30 parts**: the verdict's comparison base and magnitude, rounds that never reach evaluate, failed-attempt records, promotion, the selection rule for many cases, and the S6 metric (P2-8; HQ-0023).
- **Owner-only (DRIVER §9)**: #5 A and B items, C-6, OD-25, OD-3. Nothing of them is touched.
- Code, tests, schema files and prompts (P2-6 and P2-7's later slices); any change to an accepted record or to the `HIL-Claw-data` repository; any new external dependency.
