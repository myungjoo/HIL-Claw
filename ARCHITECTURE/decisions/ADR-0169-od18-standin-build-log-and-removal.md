## Decision

A sentence is **cited** when a record, a requirement, a fact E1 to E12 or a file is named with it; it then says what that source says and adds nothing. Every other sentence is **this record's own**.

### 1. Scope and terms

- **The calls**: those of ADR-0168 decision 1 (cited): a call of `correctness.check`, `sim.run` or `board.run` at a run of ADR-0144 decision 1. **A build container**: that of the verification build or of the timing build; the waiter's build of ADR-0145 decision 3 is named apart (decision 4). **The client's streams**: the two `bytes` the client hands back of a build container, its standard output and its standard error. Rows C1 to C19 are ADR-0168's.

### 2. What the log of a build container is

- **The log the operation hands the keeper for a build container is the client's standard output followed by its standard error, each decoded on its own as UTF-8 with every invalid byte sequence replaced by U+FFFD, joined with nothing between them.** Nothing is added — no separator, heading, status, command or name — and nothing is removed.
- **The client's own text in those streams is part of the log and is not filtered out** — the answer to ADR-0144 decision 3's "Whether the compiler's log of ADR-0078 must exclude it".
- **How this reads ADR-0078.** Decision 1 defines the log as what the compiler "wrote on its standard output and its standard error" (cited); inside a container those channels reach the operation only through the client (cited: ADR-0144 decision 3, "passes on the program's standard output, standard error and exit status"), and ADR-0078 decision 5 gave the route to the container (cited). **This record reads "the log" of a build container as everything that reaches the operation on those two channels** — the compiler's bytes and whatever the route writes on the same channels — and reads decision 4's "Nothing is added, removed or changed" as binding the operation, which adds and removes nothing. Under that reading decision 4 is applied as written: UTF-8 without a byte order mark, invalid bytes replaced, no header, command, setting, time, status, line-end conversion or final newline the streams did not hold. **That the client's lines may stand in a file ADR-0078 calls "the compiler's log" is the cost of the reading**; if the owner reads decision 4 as forbidding them, that is an objection to this decision.
- **Why the two streams in that order, and not as one interleaved text.** The client hands back two byte strings (observation), so the order in which the compiler wrote lines across the two is not had; one stream after the other keeps each stream's order whole. Standard output comes first, in the order of the descriptors (1, 2). For `clang` the diagnostics are on standard error and standard output is usually empty (this record's expectation; runtime acceptance item (lxxviii)), so the order rarely shows. Each stream is decoded on its own, so that a byte sequence cut at the end of one stream is not joined to the start of the other.
- **Weighed for the hardest choice — the client's own text.**
  - **(a) Kept, not filtered** (taken). It holds nothing the operation added, and the client writes of its own mainly when it fails with 125 to 127 (cited: ADR-0144 decision 3, E7), for which no log is handed over (decision 3); what it may write beside a program's status — a warning, for example — is evidence of the environment of that build, the kind of text ADR-0078 decision 2 keeps for S9 ("for S9 the only evidence of what happened", cited).
  - **(b) Filtered out by its words** (for example lines that begin with the client's name). The full set of the client's lines was not read (cited: ADR-0144 decision 3, "the full set was not read"), a compiler diagnostic can hold the same words, and every line removed is a change of what the compiler wrote, against ADR-0078 decision 4 and its Alternatives row "Replace known path prefixes … The file would no longer be what the compiler wrote" (cited). Not taken.
  - **(c) Standard error only.** It drops what the compiler wrote on standard output, against ADR-0078 decision 1's "standard output and its standard error" (cited). Not taken.
  - **(d) A separator or a heading between the streams.** It adds bytes the compiler did not write, against ADR-0078 decision 4 (cited). Not taken.
- **The verification process's streams** are no compiler's log: its standard output is the report (cited: ADR-0017 decision 7, "the standard output the process was started with"; ADR-0144 Q3), and its standard error is read by nothing (cited: OD-16 sub-question ⑤, as ADR-0078 Boundaries names it).

### 3. Which build containers have a log, and when it is handed over

- **A log is handed over exactly when the build container's answer is one for which ADR-0078 decision 2 keeps a log — S7, S8, S9 or a build that ended ok — and the client handed its streams back.** By the rows of ADR-0168:

| | The build container (ADR-0168 row) | Answer (cited: ADR-0168 decision 2) | Log handed over | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| L1 | Ended with 0 (C7), whatever follows: the executable found or not (S9), a process with any answer | go on; `build_output_missing` if no executable | **yes**, before the executable is looked for | an ok build or S9: kept (cited: ADR-0078 decision 2) |
| L2 | Ended with any status but 0 and 125 to 127, a negative one included (C6) | `build_exit_status` (S7) | **yes** | S7: kept (cited: ADR-0078 decision 2, O2 as ADR-0060 applied it) |
| L3 | Ended with 125, 126 or 127 (C5) | `compiler_unavailable` (S6) | **no** | read as the container runtime's (cited: ADR-0168 decision 3); S6 "found … no compiler or none that ran, so … no log" (cited: ADR-0078 decision 2) |
| L4 | Passed its time limit and was handed back (C10), whatever its forced removal gave | `build_time_limit` (S8) | **yes**: the streams read before the limit | S8: kept (cited: ADR-0078 decision 2, "killed for its time limit") |
| L5 | Passed its time limit and its forced removal raised (C10; the exception carries no streams) | `build_time_limit` (S8) | **no**: nothing reached the operation | the log "did not reach the tool function" (cited: ADR-0078 decision 2, a reading of a missing file it names) |
| L6 | Raised before or at the start: C2, C3, C4, C13, C17 | S6, S3 or S4 | **no** | nothing started |
| L7 | Raised after the start: C11 (`OverflowError`), C14 (an `OSError`), C18 (a request to end) | S4; not placed; passes | **no**: no streams | the exception carries none (observation); after a request to end nothing is written anyway (cited: ADR-0078 decision 7) |
| L8 | A status that is not exactly an `int` | not placed (cited: ADR-0168 decision 3) | **no** | no row reads it |

- **The presence rule of ADR-0078 decision 2 holds unchanged** (cited): "the file exists exactly when the operation handed over a log", and a missing file says that no compiler of that check ran or that its log did not reach the tool function. L5 is the second case.
- **Weighed for L3.** **(a) No log** (taken): the answer is S6, for which ADR-0078 decision 2 keeps none, so the rule "a log exactly for the rows it keeps" stays one rule; and the compiler may never have started (cited: ADR-0168 decision 3, the daemon not reachable, the image not in the store, `clang` not found). **(b) The streams handed over**, as the only text that says what failed. The file would then exist for an S6 identity, which ADR-0078 decision 2's presence rule says does not happen, and it would hold the client's words rather than any compiler's. Not taken. **The cost**: should a source make `clang` end with 125 to 127 (cited: ADR-0168 decision 3 knows of no way; its item (lxxvi)), the log of that compile failure is not kept.
- **Weighed for L4.** On the host a build killed at its limit had a compiler that was started (cited: ADR-0078 decision 2). Through the client the client was ended, and whether the container had started `clang` before the limit is not known (cited: ADR-0144 decision 3, "If the program is ended before the container runtime has created the container …"). The log is handed over because the answer is S8, which O2 keeps (cited, as ADR-0078 decision 2 applies it); a build whose container never started gives a log of what the client wrote, often empty. **Not taken**: no log for a timed-out build, which would drop the log of S8 that O2 names.
- **When**: the log is handed over after the build container is read and before the executable is looked for or the process is started, so that it is handed over "before it returns or raises" (cited: ADR-0078 decision 6) whatever follows.

### 4. The timing build, and the waiter's build

- **The timing build of `sim.run` and `board.run` follows decisions 2 and 3** (cited for its log: ADR-0108 decisions 2 and 4, "the form of ADR-0078 decision 4", "as ADR-0078 decision 5 says of the check's"): its log is the client's standard output then standard error, handed over by the rows L1 to L8 read for the timing build (S9 there as ADR-0068 decision 2 reads it, cited by ADR-0108 decision 2).
- **The waiter's build (ADR-0145 decision 3) is not answered here.** ADR-0108 keeps "the one call … that the operation of a call makes" (cited: decision 1, "a call makes at most one") as the timing build, and the keeper takes one log (cited: ADR-0108 decision 5). Keeping the waiter's log too would need either a second file of a timing identity — a new file name of the data plane (CLAUDE.md A-13; ADR-0101 Consequences) — or the logs of two compiler processes in one file, which ADR-0108 decision 1's "The log: everything the compiler process of that build wrote" (cited) does not read as covering. Not keeping it is a reading of A-2's "에러 로그" that ADR-0078 decision 2 did not make for a build of an attempt (cited: ADR-0078 decision 2 kept every build whose compiler ended). **Neither is chosen**; it is left to the record that the operations of the run tools need (Boundaries, Consequences).

### 5. The time limit of a forced removal: 60 s, after the build's or process's limit

- **Every forced removal of a build or process container of the three tools — after its time limit, and before an exception of the client run is passed on (cited: `run_in_container`) — is given 60 s.** The value is a constant of the package source, applied by the operations of `cpu_standin` of `correctness.check`, `sim.run` and `board.run` alike, under the rule of ADR-0117 decision 2 that "They change only by a commit of the source repository" (cited); which module holds it is the code's.
- **It lies after the build's or process's limit and is part of neither** (cited: ADR-0144 decision 3, "the forced removal at the limit lies after it"). It changes no answer: the answer of a container that passed its limit is `build_time_limit` or `time_limit` "whatever its forced removal gave" (cited: ADR-0168 C10), and a removal that is not shown to have succeeded within its limit leaves the build directory (cited: ADR-0168 decision 5).
- **What it adds to the longest call of ADR-0117 decision 5.** A forced removal follows only a container that did not end by itself, and after it the call ends with a broken result or with what the exception gives (cited: ADR-0168 C10, C11, C18), so a call holds at most one. The longest call is then one build and one process, the process past its limit: **60 + 1500 + 60 = 1620 s** against ADR-0117's 1560 s, plus the bounded retry of the build directory's removal (about 2 s in the code; ADR-0096 decision 3) and the tool function's own work. That call's result is a broken `time_limit`, which "holds no counts" (cited: ADR-0117 decision 5, from ADR-0042 decision 7), so no draw is judged and the tool function's own work after it is small (this record's estimate); a process that completes is never force-removed. **So the MCP idle window of 1800 s still holds every call** (180 s left beside the forced removal; 240 s, as ADR-0117 decision 5 has it, for a call whose process completes), and ADR-0117 decision 5's bound for a check of many draws is unchanged. For `sim.run` and `board.run` the two builds share B_t (cited: ADR-0145 decision 3), so the same 1620 s holds. The Bash default of 600 s already does not fit every call (cited: ADR-0117 decision 5); this changes nothing there.
- **Weighed.**
  - **(a) 60 s** (taken). `docker rm --force` ends the container's main process and removes the container (cited: ADR-0144 decision 3, E5); this record's estimate is that it takes seconds, more on Docker Desktop, whose daemon runs in a VM (cited: ADR-0049 D3, as ADR-0144 Q3 reads it). 60 s is ten times and more of that, so that the limit ends a removal that hangs, not one that is slow; and it is the value the code of the coder's container took for the same command (observation; it binds nothing).
  - **(b) A shorter value such as 10 s.** It saves at most 50 s of a call that is already broken, and a removal cut off before it ended leaves the build directory and maybe a running container (cited: ADR-0168 decision 5) on a slow but working daemon. Not taken.
  - **(c) A value derived from the build's or process's limit.** Nothing a removal does grows with the work of the kernel; the rule of ADR-0117 decision 4 bounds the kernel's calls (cited). Not taken.
  - **(d) No limit**, waiting for the removal to end. `run_in_container` refuses it (observation: "neither has a default", a "positive and finite" number), and a removal that hangs would hold the call past the MCP window. Not taken.
- **The removal of the build directory** after the last container, with its bounded retry, is the code's (cited: ADR-0096 decision 3, "The bound is the code's"); this record adds no limit to it.

## Consequences
