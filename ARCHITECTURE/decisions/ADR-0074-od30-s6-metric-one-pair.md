## Decision

A value is **cited** when a source is named with it. Everything else is **this record's own** design: the pair conditions, the eligibility of an attempt, the choice of the first run's best and of its round, the treatment of drift, the verdicts and their conditions, the case condition, and what is reported beside a verdict. The names below (**first run**, **second run**, **eligible attempt**, **first-run best**, **first-run round**, **second-run round**, **met**, **not met**, **not applicable**) are names of this record, not members or values of any file.

### 1. The pair, and the first run's best

- **The pair** is an ordered pair (**first run**, **second run**) of two runs, named by whoever applies the metric. The order is not derived from the `run_id`s: no component may depend on their order (cited: ADR-0027 decisions 3 and 4). The two runs form a pair when their `instance.json` state the same key — equal `key` members of `hw_profile` and `op_signature` (ADR-0018 decision 3, ADR-0031 decision 3); the attributes in their `record`s (`label`, and a hw_profile's `hw_source`) never change a key (ADR-0001 decisions 1 and 5) and are no condition — and equal values of `objective` (the target; ADR-0053), `tolerance` (ADR-0020), `candidates_per_round` (N; ADR-0028), `round_budget` (ADR-0032), `wall_clock_budget_s` (ADR-0033) and `n_max` (ADR-0023); a member is equal when both files state the same value or both leave it out, except that a run whose `instance.json` states no `op_signature` has no key (ADR-0031 decision 5) and forms no pair. The key and the next five are the S6 proposal's "같은 키·target·tolerance·N·budget" (both budgets the record states); `n_max` is added because it bounds every measurement compared (ADR-0007 decision 4). Two runs that differ in any of them are **not a pair**: the metric gives no verdict and reports which member differs. Members not listed (`schema_version`, `source_commit`, `owner`, `project`, `draws`) are no condition; the source commits are reported (decision 5).
- **Only latency minimisation.** The metric is defined for a pair whose `objective` states the one objective `latency_minimisation` and no constraint — the only objective an instance can state (owner, O1) and the only case a counted comparison covers today (cited: ADR-0071 decision 4 "Only comparisons without a memory ceiling are covered"). Any other objective or a stated constraint is outside it (Boundaries).
- **An eligible attempt** of a run is an attempt with exactly one board timing run, whose `outcome` is `converged` (ADR-0063 decision 2), and exactly one correctness check at `point` `board`, whose `outcome` is `pass` (ADR-0042 decision 2). Its **samples** are the `intervals_ns` of that timing run, which are the samples of `latency_minimisation` (cited: ADR-0050 decision 3; ADR-0063 decisions 4 and 5). Reasons:
  - *Board only, check passed*: the condition of ADR-0071 decision 4 for a counted comparison, read for one side. A sim measurement counts for nothing (FR-1: board is the final measurement); a measurement isolated under FR-1 ⑶ has a board check that is not `pass`; a broken timing run has no measurement (ADR-0063 decision 3). Whether sim counts on sim-only hardware is OD-26.
  - *Converged only*: a `not_converged` measurement is "usable and marked" (ADR-0007 decision 4) and enters decision 7 "like any other", where its spread widens the interval and makes `not different` more likely. Admitted as the bar it would lower the bar; admitted on the second run's side it would make "reached" easier. For an acceptance test, an error that withholds `met` is preferred to one that grants it. **This is the metric's rule only**: what a run does with a `not_converged` measurement — whether it may stand as a best or a baseline — stays OD-13 sub-question ②.
  - *Exactly one each*: which of several timing runs or checks of one attempt at one point counts is OD-18 sub-question ⑳ (as ADR-0071 decision 3 leaves it). A pair in which an attempt of either run holds more than one is **outside the metric**: no verdict, reported.
- **The first-run best**, the first run's best for the metric: among the eligible attempts of the first run, the one whose samples have the **lowest trimmed mean** (ADR-0007 decision 5), lower being better for this type (owner, O1). **This order of more than two measurements is the metric's own rule**: ADR-0050 decision 3 gives the direction of a pair and leaves what holds "for more than two candidates" to OD-13 and OD-1; how a run orders its own attempts, and its best, is not decided here. **Ties** (equal trimmed means) go to the lowest round, then the lowest candidate, then the lowest attempt, so that the first-run best is unique. A first run with no eligible attempt has **no first-run best**.
- **The first-run round**, the round in which the first run reached the first-run best: the lowest round of the first run that holds the first-run best or an eligible attempt that **reaches** the first-run best by decision 2. It is at most the round of the first-run best.
- **Why the lowest trimmed mean, and why the first-run round is not simply the first-run best's round.** The pairwise rule of ADR-0007 decision 7 need not be transitive and names no best of many; the trimmed mean is the policy's one number for a measurement, and it orders any set. But the lowest of several measurements that are not different from each other is partly chance: taking the first-run best's own round as the count would let noise late in the first run lengthen its count and favour `met`. Measuring both runs' counts by the same "reached" rule against the same the first-run best removes that bias.
- **Nothing of `best.json` or `best_ref` is defined** (OD-20). The first-run best is computed from the recorded facts each time the metric is applied; a run's own best, the incumbent and the baseline (OD-28) are other things and are not read.

### 2. "Reached", and drift

- **An eligible attempt X reaches the first-run best** when the rule of ADR-0007 decision 7, applied to the ordered pair (A = the first-run best's samples, B = X's samples), gives **not different**, or **different** with the whole interval below 1 / (1 + `delta`) — X lower, an improvement for latency minimisation (cited: ADR-0050 decision 3). Different with the whole interval above 1 + `delta` is not reaching. `delta`, `confidence`, `resamples`, the trim and the fixed seed are ADR-0007's, unchanged (decisions 5, 7, 8 and 9); this record adds no constant.
- **Why "not different" counts as reached**: the S6 proposal says so, and a second run that lands within the noise of the first run's best has done what the first run did. ADR-0007 decision 7's "never an improvement" speaks of improvement; reaching is not an improvement over the first-run best.
- **The second-run round**, the round in which the second run reached the first-run best: the lowest round of the second run that holds an eligible attempt reaching the first-run best. A second run with no such attempt has **not reached**.
- **Drift: the recorded sample sets are compared as they are** (the OD-30 section asks for sub-question ② whether the first run's best must be measured again; the answer here is no). The two sets come from different sessions; "A drift between the two measurements is not cancelled by it" (cited: ADR-0007 decision 7; decision 10), and ADR-0050 decision 3 leaves what holds "for two measurements of different sessions" to OD-13 and OD-1. **Comparing them is the metric's own rule** and says nothing of how a run compares measurements across sessions. A slower second session makes reaching harder (an unearned `not met`), a faster one makes it easier (an unearned `met`); the verdict does not show that the kernels rather than the conditions differ. The exact key bounds the conditions only through its fields: two machines with equal values share a key (cited: ADR-0001 decision 5, as [ADR-0035](ADR-0035-od4-cpu-standin-hw-key-values.md) decision 7 reads it for `cpu_standin`), and nothing bounds the time between the runs.
- **Not adopted: a re-measurement of the first-run best in the second run's session** (OD-11's incumbent re-measurement, OD-13's paired re-measurement). It would need the first-run best's binary to be runnable again (what is archived of an attempt: OD-20; CAS), a run behaviour that no landed record gives, hardware time (C-4), and a place for the re-measurement's samples, which would be a new data-plane record. Boundaries.

### 3. The verdict of one pair

- **The round counts compared** are the first-run round and the second-run round, rounds counted from round 0 as the runs number them (ADR-0059 decision 5; ADR-0071 decision 6): reaching in round r means r rounds after bring-up.
- **The verdict of a pair inside the metric (decision 1) is one of three values, a closed list**, decided in this order; two runs that are not a pair, or a pair outside the metric, get none:
  1. **not applicable** — (i) the first run has no first-run best; (ii) the first-run round is 0 or 1; or (iii) the second run reached the first-run best in round 0 (a second-run round of 0).
  2. **met** — the second run reached the first-run best with the second-run round < the first-run round, and the case condition of decision 4 holds.
  3. **not met** — every other case: the second run did not reach the first-run best, or reached it with the second-run round ≥ the first-run round, or reached it with the second-run round < the first-run round while the case condition fails.
- **Why (ii) is not applicable**: with a first-run round of 0, no second-run round is lower; with 1, only a second-run round of 0 is, and round 0 has no hypothesis record (ADR-0059 decision 5), so no `case` entry can be in it and the case condition could never hold. Such a pair cannot show what S6 asks. **Why (iii)**: a second run whose round 0 already reaches the first-run best has no round with a hypothesis record up to the second-run round, so no hypothesis can be shown to rest on a case; whether bring-up uses cases at all is open (ADR-0073 Boundaries). Not applicable is neither `met` nor `not met`; how it enters a judgement of several pairs is sub-question ③ (owner).
- **Not adopted for (ii): another metric for that case** (for example the best at an equal round count, from the OD-30 section). It would need a second comparison rule — "better at round k" between two runs — and a second verdict for a pair that the S6 proposal's metric cannot judge; when a first run's best comes that early, the case for faster convergence is not testable by that pair.
- **A second run that ended before reaching** is `not met` whatever ended it (budget, early target, stop, process death); the metric reads the rounds recorded. Whether such a pair, or a first run that ended abnormally, is used at all is the choice of pairs (③, the owner).
- **What one verdict shows**: `met` shows one pair in which the second run, resting on a case of the first, reached the first run's best in fewer rounds. It does not show that cases cause faster convergence: model output is not deterministic, and one pair can be chance (OD-30 sub-question ③). How many pairs, how their verdicts are combined, and a control are the owner's.

### 4. The case condition

- **The case condition holds** when at least one hypothesis in the `hypotheses.json` of a round k of the second run, with 1 ≤ k ≤ the second-run round, has a `basis` entry of kind `case` whose `case.run_id` is the first run's `run_id` (ADR-0073 decision 2). A case of the first run is recognised by that `run_id` alone — the `run_id` its file name carries (ADR-0072 decision 1) and the reference repeats; its `round` and `hypothesis` are any.
- **Why `case` entries and not `cases_read`**: the selection of every round ≥ 1 is given every valid case of its key (ADR-0073 decision 1), so once the first run's rounds are promoted `cases_read` names its cases in every round whatever the selection did with them — it would test the lookup, not the use. A `case` entry says a hypothesis rests on that case, which is what the S6 proposal's reason asks ("없으면 빨라진 것이 사례 덕인지 말할 수 없다"). Requiring both adds nothing: every `case` entry's case is in the same file's `cases_read` (ADR-0073 decision 2).
- **Why rounds 1 to the second-run round**: a reference after the round that reached cannot have helped it reach; round 0 has no hypothesis record.
- **Not checked by the metric**: that the named case exists in the key directory and holds the named hypothesis — a rule between files of ADR-0073 decision 2, checked elsewhere; and how the hypothesis fared.

### 5. What is reported beside the verdict

None of this changes the verdict, and none of it is stored by this record (Boundaries):

- the first-run best (round, candidate, attempt) and the first-run round; the second-run round or "not reached"; the attempt that reached; the rounds and hypotheses of the `case` entries found, or which value of decision 3 applied and why.
- **The board measurement count** (NFR-5) of each run up to its count — rounds 0 to the first-run round for the first run, 0 to the second-run round (or to its last recorded round, if it did not reach) for the second: the number of board timing runs of those rounds, each a result of ADR-0063 whatever its `outcome`, as `board.run` of [ADR-0061](ADR-0061-od18-run-tools-split.md) returns it. The fact is defined by ADR-0063; the file it is read from is PLAN P1-8.
- **The token spend** up to the same rounds: **no landed record gives it** (OD-19, NFR-5, #5 A-8). The metric reports it as not available until a record defines it; it invents no figure.
- Whether the two `instance.json` state the same `source_commit` (ADR-0022) and the same `draws`: a harness change between the runs is a further limit of the comparison (ADR-0007 decision 10), and a different number of verification draws changes how strong a board `pass` is; neither is a condition.

### Worked example (synthetic values; samples in nanoseconds, ADR-0063 decision 4)

Both runs: the same key, N = 1, one attempt per candidate, every attempt with one board timing run and one board check `pass`; no further round. Round 0's attempts are assumed, as in ADR-0071's and ADR-0073's Examples, although no record yet says a round-0 attempt exists (OD-28). First run `20261009t012331z-9f3a6c1e0b7d4a52` (the example of [ADR-0027](ADR-0027-od8-run-id-form.md)), second run `20261020t093000z-0f1e2d3c4b5a6978` (the placeholder of ADR-0073's Example). A, C and D are the measurements of ADR-0007's worked example.

| Run, round | Samples (`intervals_ns`) | Outcome | Trimmed mean | Against the first-run best = A |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| first, 0 | 135, 129, 131, 130, 133 | converged | 131.3 | different, higher (ratio ≥ 129 / 104 = 1.24) |
| first, 1 | 125, 119, 121, 120, 122 | converged | 121.0 | different, higher (ratio ≥ 119 / 104 = 1.14) |
| first, 2 | C of ADR-0007 | not converged | 100.5 | not eligible |
| first, 3 | A: 104, 99, 101, 100, 102 | converged | 101.0 | the first-run best |
| second, 0 | 135, 129, 131, 130, 133 | converged | 131.3 | different, higher |
| second, 1 | 125, 119, 121, 120, 122 | converged | 121.0 | different, higher |
| second, 2 | D: 100, 97, 99, 101, 98 | converged | 99.0 | not different (ADR-0007: D / A, interval about [0.95, 1.00]) |

- The bounds of "higher" are a derivation, not a draw: a trimmed mean of a resample lies between the smallest and largest value of its set, so every replicate ratio T(y*) / T(x*) lies between min(y) / max(x) and max(y) / min(x), here wholly above 1.02.
- The first-run best is round 3's A (C, lower, is not converged). No earlier eligible attempt reaches A, so the first-run round is 3. The second run reaches in round 2, so the second-run round is 2, below 3. Its round-2 `hypotheses.json` holds the entry below, so the case condition holds: **met**. Reported: 4 board timing runs in the first run's rounds 0 to 3, 3 in the second run's rounds 0 to 2; token spend not available.

```json
{"kind": "case", "case": {"run_id": "20261009t012331z-9f3a6c1e0b7d4a52", "round": 1, "hypothesis": "h1"}, "text": "NOT-DECIDED-HERE"}
```

- **A boundary case**: had the first run's round 1 held A and rounds 2 and 3 nothing that reaches it or is lower, the first-run round would be 1 and the pair is **not applicable** whatever the second run did. Had the second run's files of rounds 1 and 2 held only `cases_read` naming the first run's cases and no `case` entry, the pair would be **not met**.

## Alternatives considered

| Point | Alternative | Why not |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The first-run best = the attempt a later `best_ref` names | `best_ref` and `best.json` are not defined (OD-20); the metric would wait for them and inherit a rule made for another purpose. |
| 1 | The first-run round = the round of the first-run best | Noise among attempts that are not different lets the lowest trimmed mean fall late and lengthens the first run's count, favouring `met`. |
| 1 | Admit `not_converged` measurements | They widen the interval and make `not different` likelier on either side, so the bar and "reached" both get easier. |
| 1 | Pick the first of several timing runs or checks of an attempt | Decides OD-18 sub-question ⑳ for the metric; that question is about which execution counts for the run itself. |
| 2 | Re-measure the first-run best in the second run's session | A new run behaviour, a stored binary (OD-20) and a new record for the samples; hardware time (C-4). |
| 2 | Only "different, lower" counts as reached | Asks the second run to beat the first beyond the noise, which the S6 proposal does not; within the noise is where the first run's best is. |
| 3 | Pairs whose first-run round is 0 or 1 are `not met`, or are judged by the best at equal rounds | The first counts against the system a pair that cannot show anything; the second is a second metric for a case the S6 metric cannot judge. |
| 3 | A second run that reaches in round 0 is `not met` | It has no hypothesis record up to that round, so it shows nothing about cases either way; counting it against the system would judge the spread of bring-up. |
| 3 | A second run that ended early without reaching is not applicable | Hides a second run that stopped short; which runs enter the test is the owner's choice of pairs (③). |
| 4 | `cases_read` naming a first-run case | Holds whenever that run's cases were promoted (ADR-0073 decision 1); it tests the lookup, not the use. |
| 4 | Case references in any round of the second run | A reference after the round that reached cannot explain the reaching. |
| 5 | A token figure estimated from what is recorded | No landed record gives one (OD-19); an estimate would be a new ledger. |

## Consequences

- **The P2-8 code slice can be cut**: a pure computation over fixtures of the facts of decisions 1 to 5 — both runs' `instance.json` members of decision 1; per attempt its ref, its board timing run's `outcome` and `intervals_ns` and its board check's `outcome`; the second run's `hypotheses.json` of rounds 1 to the second-run round; the two `run_id`s — using the trimmed mean and the difference verdict that `hilclaw/harness/measurement.py` provides for ADR-0007 (an observation; this record binds no code). Until PLAN P1-8 defines the result files, it can be tested on such fixtures only; reading the files is a later step. Names, the form of its input and its output are that task's.
- **A-13 reading**: document mode. No member, value or version of `hypotheses.json`, `verdicts.json`, the case file, `eval.json`, `STATE.json`, `instance.json`, `spec.json`, `artifacts.json`, `events.ndjson`, the key record files, `best.json` or `technique_stats.json` changes, and no file or path is added to the data plane: the metric reads recorded facts, and its verdict is not a run record. Nothing was built or run, and no `claude` process was started.
- **Names checked on 2026-10-16 in `hilclaw/`, `tests/` and `ARCHITECTURE/`.** `not_applicable`, `not_met`, `eligible`, "first-run best", "first-run round" and "second-run round" occur as no member, value or name of a record. Considered and not taken: `B1` (a label of the literature review's proposal B1 and of a rule of ADR-0055) and `r1` / `r2` (placeholder run names in the walk-throughs of ADR-0018, ADR-0023, ADR-0028, ADR-0031, ADR-0034 and others, and synthetic `run_id` values in `tests/`), which a reader of a record about two runs would take for runs. "not applicable" and "not met" occur only as prose in other records, as here, and are no value of a file. The members and values this record reads — `intervals_ns`, `outcome` with `converged`, `pass`, `point` with `board`, `cases_read`, `case`, `run_id`, `round` and the `instance.json` members of decision 1 — are taken with the meanings of their records on purpose.
- **Not edited and now less exact** (follow-ups for the integrator): the Boundaries line "The S6 metric, its boundary cases, repetitions and control (P2-8)" of ADR-0059, ADR-0071 and ADR-0072, and ADR-0073 Boundaries "The S6 metric, its boundary cases, repetitions and control (P2-8), and the runtime acceptance of P2-9" and its Status "… and the S6 metric stay open" (now answered for one pair; repetitions and control remain); ADR-0073 decision 3 "which one the S6 criterion counts is its metric (P2-8, OD-30)" (answered: `case` entries); FR-18's "지표 정의의 확정 = OD-30" (the metric of one pair is now defined under OD-30; still true as a pointer, OD-30 stays `제안됨`); ADR-0050 decision 3 "for two measurements of different sessions and for more than two candidates stay with OD-13 and OD-1" (still true for a run; the metric now has its own rules for both); PLAN P2-8's "지표 정의는 OD-30 종결 필요" (the metric it needs is now recorded; PLAN belongs to the driver).

## Boundaries (not decided here)

- **An order of more than two measurements, and a comparison across sessions, for the run itself** (OD-13, OD-1; ADR-0050 decision 3): the metric's rules of decisions 1 and 2 are for the metric only.
- **Owner (sub-question ③)** — this record's reading: the spend is the owner's value judgement on the owner's subscription and hardware (the kind of judgement DRIVER §9 keeps for #5 A/B items), and the owner performs the test (PLAN P2-9): the number of pairs and how their verdicts are combined (counts of `met`, `not met` and `not applicable`); whether a control run with the case lookup off is required, and how such a run would be made; how much of the C-7 subscription and of hardware time (C-4) the acceptance test may spend. Also owner-only and untouched: #5 A and B items, C-6, OD-25, OD-3.
- **Objective types other than latency minimisation** and any combination of objectives (OD-1 residue, #5 A-4); a pair whose instance states a constraint (`memory_ceiling`: ADR-0050 decisions 4 and 5, ADR-0052 decision 7).
- **`best.json` and `best_ref`** (OD-20); **the baseline** (OD-28); **the incumbent and paired re-measurement**, and with them a drift-free comparison across sessions (OD-11 proposal, OD-13 residue sub-question ①); what a run does with a `not_converged` measurement (OD-13 sub-question ②); which execution of an attempt counts (OD-18 sub-question ⑳).
- **Round 0 and cases**: whether bring-up uses cases (ADR-0073 Boundaries), and the baseline (OD-28) — a baseline taken from an existing best would make a second run's round 0 reach the first-run best by construction, so every such pair would be **not applicable** by (iii); the record that decides OD-28 revisits (iii).
- **Promotion**: the case condition can hold only if the first run's rounds were promoted to `db/` before the second run's selections read its key; when and whether rounds are promoted is open (OD-29, OD-23, OD-30).
- **Environments where timing is hard or inexact** ([owner decision 2026-10-15](https://github.com/myungjoo/HIL-Claw/issues/5#issuecomment-6182984913), point 3): whether a pair measured in such an environment may be judged at all, and what such a verdict must show, is OD-13 sub-question ⑪ and is not decided here.
- **Budget axes `instance.json` does not state** (a token or hardware budget, OD-2, OD-19, #5 A-8; an early-stop target value, FR-3 ④): they cannot be pair conditions until a record states them.
- **Sim-only hardware**, where a sim measurement may be final (OD-26); **similar-key pairs** (OD-4 residue; S6 step 1's "유사한 hw_profile").
- **Where an acceptance verdict is stored**, if anywhere: it is not a run record, and this record places it nowhere; a place in the data plane would be a new file (A-13).
- **The token ledger** (OD-19, NFR-5, #5 A-8); **the result files** from which the facts are read (PLAN P1-8).
- **The runtime acceptance itself** (PLAN P2-9; D16): the owner runs the pair on a runtime machine; the runs of P2-5's check and of the nightly routine (P2-10, D17) are excluded by P2-9.
- Code, tests, fixtures and prompts (P2-8 and later); any change to an accepted record or to the `HIL-Claw-data` repository; any new external dependency.
