## Decision

A value is **cited** when a source is named with it. Everything else is **this record's own** design: the pair conditions, the qualification of an attempt, the choice of the first run's best and of its round, the treatment of drift, the verdicts and their conditions, the case condition, and what is reported beside a verdict. The names below (**first run**, **second run**, **qualifying attempt**, **first-run best**, **first-run round**, **second-run round**, **met**, **not met**, **not applicable**) are names of this record, not members or values of any file.

### 1. The pair, and the first run's best

- **The pair** is an ordered pair (**first run**, **second run**) of two runs, named by whoever applies the metric. The order is not derived from the `run_id`s: no component may depend on their order (cited: ADR-0027 decisions 3 and 4). The two runs form a pair when their `instance.json` state the same key — equal `key` members of `hw_profile` and `op_signature` (ADR-0018 decision 3, ADR-0031 decision 3); the attributes in their `record`s (`label`, and a hw_profile's `hw_source`) never change a key (ADR-0001 decisions 1 and 5) and are no condition — and equal values of `objective` (the target; ADR-0053), `tolerance` (ADR-0020), `candidates_per_round` (N; ADR-0028), `round_budget` (ADR-0032), `wall_clock_budget_s` (ADR-0033), `n_max` (ADR-0023), `coder_model` ([ADR-0136](ADR-0136-od8-first-case-coder-model.md) decision 2) and `injections` ([ADR-0075](ADR-0075-od30-injection-at-instance-start.md) decision 2). A member is equal when both files state the same value or both leave it out, except that a run whose `instance.json` states no `op_signature` has no key (ADR-0031 decision 5) and forms no pair.
- **Why these.** The key and the next five are the S6 proposal's "같은 키·target·tolerance·N·budget" (both budgets the record states). `n_max` is added because it bounds every measurement compared (ADR-0007 decision 4). `coder_model` and `injections` are added because each changes what the coder or the selection is given, so a difference in rounds could be theirs and not the cases'. Two runs that differ in any of them are **not a pair**: the metric gives no verdict and reports which member differs. Members not listed (`schema_version`, `source_commit`, `owner`, `project`, `draws`) are no condition; the source commits are reported (decision 5).
- **Only runs whose facts are defined.** The standing and the measurement of an attempt are defined today for the runs of ADR-0110 decision 1 and ADR-0140 decision 1. A pair with a run outside them is outside the metric until a record defines those facts for it (Boundaries).
- **Only latency minimisation.** The metric is defined for a pair whose `objective` states the one objective `latency_minimisation` and no constraint — the only objective an instance can state (owner, O1) and the only case a counted comparison covers today (cited: ADR-0071 decision 4 "Only comparisons without a memory ceiling are covered"). Any other objective or a stated constraint is outside it (Boundaries).
- **A qualifying attempt** of a run is an attempt with **the standing `verified` at `board`** (ADR-0110 decision 3) and **one measurement at `board`** by the rule of ADR-0140 decision 3 (exactly one end line with a completed result, no `failure` end line and no open identity), whose result has `outcome` `converged`. Its **samples** are that result's `intervals`, the samples of `latency_minimisation` (cited: ADR-0050 decision 3; ADR-0183 decision 4). Reasons:
  - *Board only, verified*: the condition of ADR-0140 decision 5 for a counted comparison, read for one side. A sim measurement counts for nothing (FR-1: board is the final measurement); a measurement isolated under FR-1 ⑶ has a board standing that is not `verified`; a broken timing run has no measurement (ADR-0063 decision 3). Whether sim counts on sim-only hardware is OD-26.
  - *One measurement*: no execution among several is chosen, as for a comparison (ADR-0140 decision 3). An attempt with several is simply not qualifying and is counted in the report (decision 5), so measuring again gains nothing. What counts for promotion, improvement and a run's best is the rest of OD-18 sub-question ⑳.
  - *Converged only, although a run may do otherwise*: ADR-0113 and ADR-0114 admit a not-converged measurement to a best, a baseline and `against`, and read a wide interval as making a change hard to show. Here a wide interval has another effect: `not_different` **grants** reaching (decision 2), so a wide interval on either side makes reaching easier. ADR-0184 is another rule for another purpose (below). **This is the metric's rule only** and changes nothing of those records.
  - *What it costs*: a first run that never converges has no first-run best, so its pair is `not applicable`, which says that this pair cannot judge S6, not that S6 failed; a second run that never converges is `not met`. The exclusion can also favour `met`: a not-converged attempt of the first run at the level of its best does not shorten the first-run round, and a low not-converged attempt does not lower the bar. So the rule is a choice of the cleaner measurement, not a guarantee against an unearned `met`; decision 5 reports what was left out, so a reader sees when it mattered.
- **The first-run best**, the first run's best for the metric: among the qualifying attempts of the first run, the one whose samples have the **lowest trimmed mean** (ADR-0007 decision 5), lower being better for this type (owner, O1). **This order of more than two measurements is the metric's own rule**: ADR-0050 decision 3 gives the direction of a pair and leaves what holds "for more than two candidates" to OD-13 and OD-1; how a run orders its own attempts, and its best, is not decided here. **Ties** (equal trimmed means) go to the lowest round, then the lowest candidate, then the lowest attempt, so that the first-run best is unique. A first run with no qualifying attempt has **no first-run best**.
- **It need not be the run's incumbent.** ADR-0184 decision 2 names as a coder call's base the verified attempt with the lowest **median**, not-converged ones included, for first-case N = 1 runs. The metric uses the trimmed mean because that is the policy's one number for a measurement and the T of the comparison it then applies (cited: ADR-0007 decisions 5 and 7); the two can name different attempts, and neither reads the other.
- **The first-run round**, the round in which the first run reached the first-run best: the lowest round of the first run that holds the first-run best or a qualifying attempt that **reaches** the first-run best by decision 2. It is at most the round of the first-run best.
- **Why the lowest trimmed mean, and why the first-run round is not simply the first-run best's round.** The pairwise rule of ADR-0007 decision 7 need not be transitive and names no best of many; the trimmed mean orders any set. But the lowest of several measurements that are not different from each other is partly chance: taking the first-run best's own round as the count would let noise late in the first run lengthen its count and favour `met`. Measuring both runs' counts by the same "reached" rule against the same first-run best removes that bias.
- **Nothing of `best.json` or `best_ref` is defined** (OD-20). The first-run best is computed from the recorded facts each time the metric is applied; a run's own best, the incumbent and the baseline (OD-28) are other things and are not read.

### 2. "Reached", and drift

- **A qualifying attempt X reaches the first-run best** when the rule of ADR-0007 decision 7, applied to the ordered pair (A = the first-run best's samples, B = X's samples), gives **`not_different` or `lower`**, the values of ADR-0140 decision 4 (`lower`: the whole interval below 1 / (1 + `delta`), an improvement for latency minimisation, cited: ADR-0050 decision 3). `higher` is not reaching. `delta`, `confidence`, `resamples`, the trim and the fixed seed are ADR-0007's, unchanged (decisions 5, 7, 8 and 9); this record adds no constant. The tool `timings.compare` reads one run's log and is not what is meant here; the rule is applied across the two runs' sample sets. **X does not reach** when its result's `contract_key` or `shape` differs from the first-run best's (the two are not about the same thing; cited: ADR-0183 decision 8, `shapes_differ`) or when the samples lie outside the domain of the policy's functions (ADR-0140 decision 3, `samples_out_of_domain`), since no interval exists; the reason is reported (decision 5).
- **Why `not_different` counts as reached**: the S6 proposal says so, and a second run that lands within the noise of the first run's best has done what the first run did. ADR-0007 decision 7's "never an improvement" speaks of improvement; reaching is not an improvement over the first-run best.
- **The second-run round**, the round in which the second run reached the first-run best: the lowest round of the second run that holds a qualifying attempt reaching the first-run best. A second run with no such attempt has **not reached**.
- **Drift: the recorded sample sets are compared as they are** (the OD-30 section asks for sub-question ② whether the first run's best must be measured again; the answer here is no). The two sets come from different sessions; "A drift between the two measurements is not cancelled by it" (cited: ADR-0007 decision 7; decision 10), and ADR-0050 decision 3 leaves what holds "for two measurements of different sessions" to OD-13 and OD-1. **Comparing them is the metric's own rule** and says nothing of how a run compares measurements across sessions. A slower second session makes reaching harder (an unearned `not met`), a faster one makes it easier (an unearned `met`); the verdict does not show that the kernels rather than the conditions differ. The exact key bounds the conditions only through its fields: two machines with equal values share a key (cited: ADR-0001 decision 5, as [ADR-0035](ADR-0035-od4-cpu-standin-hw-key-values.md) decision 7 reads it for `cpu_standin`), and nothing bounds the time between the runs.
- **Not adopted: a re-measurement of the first-run best in the second run's session** (OD-11's incumbent re-measurement, OD-13's paired re-measurement). It would need a run behaviour that no landed record gives, hardware time (C-4), and a place for the re-measurement's samples, which would be a new data-plane record. Boundaries.

### 3. The verdict of one pair

- **The round counts compared** are the first-run round and the second-run round, rounds counted from round 0 as the runs number them (ADR-0059 decision 5; ADR-0071 decision 6): reaching in round r is the round's number, and a round whose coder or check failed counts like any other, including a round 0 that closed without a verified attempt, after which a later round writes the baseline again ([ADR-0185](ADR-0185-od23-first-case-round-zero-ends-without-standing-attempt.md); ADR-0184 decision 4). **A limit**: such a failure in the first run lengthens its count and favours `met`, in the second run it does the opposite; decision 5 reports the first verified round of each run so that a reader sees it.
- **The verdict of a pair inside the metric (decision 1) is one of three values, a closed list**, decided in this order; two runs that are not a pair, or a pair outside the metric, get none:
  1. **not applicable** — (i) the first run has no first-run best; (ii) the first-run round is 0 or 1; or (iii) the second run reached the first-run best in round 0 (a second-run round of 0).
  2. **met** — the second run reached the first-run best with the second-run round < the first-run round, and the case condition of decision 4 holds.
  3. **not met** — every other case: the second run did not reach the first-run best, or reached it with the second-run round ≥ the first-run round, or reached it with the second-run round < the first-run round while the case condition fails.
- **Why (ii) is not applicable**: with a first-run round of 0, no second-run round is lower; with 1, only a second-run round of 0 is, and round 0 has no hypothesis record (ADR-0059 decision 5), so no `case` entry can be in it and the case condition could never hold. Such a pair cannot show what S6 asks. **Why (iii)**: a second run whose round 0 already reaches the first-run best has no round with a hypothesis record up to the second-run round, so no hypothesis can be shown to rest on a case. Not applicable is neither `met` nor `not met`; how it enters a judgement of several pairs is sub-question ③ (owner).
- **Not adopted for (ii): another metric for that case** (for example the best at an equal round count, from the OD-30 section). It would need a second comparison rule — "better at round k" between two runs — and a second verdict for a pair that the S6 proposal's metric cannot judge; when a first run's best comes that early, the case for faster convergence is not testable by that pair.
- **A second run that ended before reaching** is `not met` whatever ended it (budget, early target, stop, process death); the metric reads the rounds recorded. Whether such a pair, or a first run that ended abnormally, is used at all is the choice of pairs (③, the owner).
- **What one verdict shows**: `met` shows one pair in which the second run, resting on a case of the first, reached the first run's best in fewer rounds. It does not show that cases cause faster convergence: model output is not deterministic, and one pair can be chance (OD-30 sub-question ③). How many pairs, how their verdicts are combined, and a control are the owner's.

### 4. The case condition

- **The case condition holds** when at least one hypothesis in the `hypotheses.json` of a round k of the second run, with 1 ≤ k ≤ the second-run round, has a `basis` entry of kind `case` whose `case.run_id` is the first run's `run_id` (ADR-0073 decision 2). A case of the first run is recognised by that `run_id` alone — the `run_id` its file name carries (ADR-0072 decision 1) and the reference repeats; its `round` and `hypothesis` are any.
- **Why `case` entries and not `cases_read`**: the selection of every round ≥ 1 is given every valid case of its key (ADR-0073 decision 1; ADR-0173 for the first case's means), so once the first run's rounds are promoted `cases_read` names its cases in every round whatever the selection did with them — it would test the lookup, not the use. A `case` entry says a hypothesis rests on that case, which is what the S6 proposal's reason asks ("없으면 빨라진 것이 사례 덕인지 말할 수 없다"). Requiring both adds nothing: every `case` entry's case is in the same file's `cases_read` (ADR-0073 decision 2).
- **Why rounds 1 to the second-run round**: a reference after the round that reached cannot have helped it reach; round 0 has no hypothesis record.
- **Not checked by the metric**: that the named case exists in the key directory and holds the named hypothesis — a rule between files of ADR-0073 decision 2; and how the hypothesis fared.

### 5. What is reported beside the verdict

None of this changes the verdict, and none of it is stored by this record (Boundaries):

- the first-run best (round, candidate, attempt) and the first-run round; the second-run round or "not reached"; the attempt that reached; the rounds and hypotheses of the `case` entries found, or which value of decision 3 applied and why.
- **The board measurement count** (NFR-5) of each run up to its count — rounds 0 to the first-run round for the first run, 0 to the second-run round for the second, or to the run's last recorded round where it has no such round: the number of board timing runs of those rounds, that is end lines at the `step` `board_run_timing_v2` that hold a result, whatever its `outcome`, an open step counting for none (ADR-0183 decision 2; ADR-0063 decisions 2 and 3).
- **What was left out**: per run, up to its count and in all, the number of attempts with a line at that `step` that do not qualify, by reason (not converged; only `broken` results; several completed results; a `failure` end line or an open identity; not `verified`), and the attempts that did not reach for a reason of decision 2 other than the interval. A verdict over mostly excluded attempts says little, and the reader sees that here.
- **The first round of each run that holds an attempt with the standing `verified` at `board`** (decision 3), and the runs that each run's `cases_read` names: a first run that itself read cases is a different bar, and which pairs are used is the choice of pairs (③).
- **The token spend** up to the same rounds: **no landed record gives it** (OD-19, NFR-5, #5 A-8; [ADR-0187](ADR-0187-od23-coder-output-log-outside-data-plane.md) leaves "the cost and token figures of a coder call (NFR-5)" out). The metric reports it as not available until a record defines it; it invents no figure.
- **Whether each run is found imprecise** at the end of its log ([ADR-0104](ADR-0104-od13-imprecise-timing-mark.md) decision 4; its scope, ADR-0103 decision 1): the owner asked that numbers from such an environment be marked (ADR-0113 Context, O1). The metric adds nothing to the finding.
- Whether the two `instance.json` state the same `source_commit` (ADR-0022) and the same `draws`: a harness change between the runs is a further limit of the comparison (ADR-0007 decision 10), and a different number of verification draws changes how strong a board `pass` is; neither is a condition.

### Worked example (synthetic values; samples in nanoseconds, ADR-0183 decision 4)

Both runs: the same key, N = 1, one attempt per candidate, every attempt with one board timing run and the standing `verified` at `board`; no further round. The round-0 attempt is the run's baseline attempt (ADR-0119 decision 2). First run `20261009t012331z-9f3a6c1e0b7d4a52` (the example of [ADR-0027](ADR-0027-od8-run-id-form.md)), second run `20261020t093000z-0f1e2d3c4b5a6978` (the placeholder of ADR-0073's Example). A, C and D are the measurements of ADR-0007's worked example.

| Run, round | Samples (`intervals`) | Outcome | Trimmed mean | Against the first-run best = A |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| first, 0 | 135, 129, 131, 130, 133 | converged | 131.3 | `higher` (ratio ≥ 129 / 104 = 1.24) |
| first, 1 | 125, 119, 121, 120, 122 | converged | 121.0 | `higher` (ratio ≥ 119 / 104 = 1.14) |
| first, 2 | C of ADR-0007 | not converged | 100.5 | not qualifying |
| first, 3 | A: 104, 99, 101, 100, 102 | converged | 101.0 | the first-run best |
| second, 0 | 135, 129, 131, 130, 133 | converged | 131.3 | `higher` |
| second, 1 | 125, 119, 121, 120, 122 | converged | 121.0 | `higher` |
| second, 2 | D: 100, 97, 99, 101, 98 | converged | 99.0 | `not_different` (ADR-0007: D / A, interval about [0.95, 1.00]) |

- The bounds of `higher` are a derivation, not a draw: a trimmed mean of a resample lies between the smallest and largest value of its set, so every replicate ratio T(y*) / T(x*) lies between min(y) / max(x) and max(y) / min(x), here wholly above 1.02.
- The first-run best is round 3's A (C, lower, is not converged). No earlier qualifying attempt reaches A, so the first-run round is 3. The second run reaches in round 2, so the second-run round is 2, below 3. Its round-2 `hypotheses.json` holds the entry below, so the case condition holds: **met**. Reported: 4 board timing runs in the first run's rounds 0 to 3, 3 in the second run's rounds 0 to 2; one attempt of the first run left out as not converged, which also makes the first run found imprecise (ADR-0104 decision 4); token spend not available.

```json
{"kind": "case", "case": {"run_id": "20261009t012331z-9f3a6c1e0b7d4a52", "round": 1, "hypothesis": "h1"}, "text": "NOT-DECIDED-HERE"}
```

- **A boundary case**: had the first run's round 1 held A and rounds 2 and 3 nothing that reaches it or is lower, the first-run round would be 1 and the pair is **not applicable** whatever the second run did. Had the second run's files of rounds 1 and 2 held only `cases_read` naming the first run's cases and no `case` entry, the pair would be **not met**.

## Alternatives considered

| Point | Alternative | Why not |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The first-run best = the attempt a later `best_ref` names | `best_ref` and `best.json` are not defined (OD-20); the metric would wait for them and inherit a rule made for another purpose. |
| 1 | The first-run best = the base of ADR-0184 (lowest median, not-converged included) | Its scope is first-case N = 1 runs and the coder's starting point; admitting not-converged makes reaching easier (decision 1), and ADR-0007 Alternatives (point 5) did not take the median as the summary. |
| 1 | The first-run round = the round of the first-run best | Noise among attempts that are not different lets the lowest trimmed mean fall late and lengthens the first run's count, favouring `met`. |
| 1 | Admit `not_converged` measurements, as ADR-0113 does for a run | They widen the interval and make `not_different` likelier on either side, so the bar and "reached" both get easier. A pair that never converges gets `not applicable`, not a guess. |
| 1 | Pick the first of several timing runs or checks of an attempt | ADR-0140 decision 3 refuses to choose for a comparison, and ADR-0110 decision 2 counts every ended check; the metric does the same. |
| 1 | Leave `coder_model` and `injections` out of the pair conditions | A difference in rounds could then be the model's or the injected hypotheses', and the verdict would not say which. |
| 2 | Re-measure the first-run best in the second run's session | A new run behaviour and a new record for the samples; hardware time (C-4). |
| 2 | Only `lower` counts as reached | Asks the second run to beat the first beyond the noise, which the S6 proposal does not; within the noise is where the first run's best is. |
| 3 | Pairs whose first-run round is 0 or 1 are `not met`, or are judged by the best at equal rounds | The first counts against the system a pair that cannot show anything; the second is a second metric for a case the S6 metric cannot judge. |
| 3 | A second run that reaches in round 0 is `not met` | It has no hypothesis record up to that round, so it shows nothing about cases either way; counting it against the system would judge the spread of bring-up. |
| 3 | A second run that ended early without reaching is not applicable | Hides a second run that stopped short; which runs enter the test is the owner's choice of pairs (③). |
| 4 | `cases_read` naming a first-run case | Holds whenever that run's cases were promoted (ADR-0073 decision 1); it tests the lookup, not the use. |
| 4 | Case references in any round of the second run | A reference after the round that reached cannot explain the reaching. |
| 5 | A token figure estimated from what is recorded | No landed record gives one (OD-19); an estimate would be a new ledger. |

## Consequences

- **The P2-8 code slice can be cut**: a pure computation, tested on fixtures, over the facts of decisions 1 to 5 — both runs' `instance.json` members of decision 1; per attempt its ref, its standing at `board` and its end lines at `board_run_timing_v2`, with `contract_key` and `shape`; the second run's `hypotheses.json` of rounds 1 to the second-run round; the two `run_id`s — using the trimmed mean and the interval that `hilclaw/harness/measurement.py` provides for ADR-0007 (an observation; this record binds no code). P1-8's completion gives the files read; `events.ndjson` holds the results (ADR-0104 decision 3 lists where each input is kept). Names, the form of its input and its output are that task's, and so is which moment's `instance.json` values are compared, since the file may change after the start (ADR-0023 decision 6).
- **A-13 reading**: document mode. No member, value or version of `hypotheses.json`, `verdicts.json`, the case file, `eval.json`, `STATE.json`, `instance.json`, `spec.json`, `artifacts.json`, `events.ndjson`, the key record files, `best.json` or `technique_stats.json` changes, and no file or path is added to the data plane: the metric reads recorded facts, and its verdict is not a run record. Nothing was built or run, and no `claude` process was started.
- **Names checked on 2026-10-31 in `hilclaw/`, `tests/` and `ARCHITECTURE/`.** "qualifying attempt", `not_met`, "first-run best", "first-run round" and "second-run round" occur nowhere else. Considered and not taken: "eligible attempt" (the earlier draft's name; ADR-0113 and ADR-0114 give "eligible" another meaning), `B1` and `r1` / `r2` (labels and placeholder run names elsewhere, which a reader of a record about two runs would take for runs). `not_applicable` is a value elsewhere in the repository, on purpose not used: the words here are "not applicable", no value of a file. "not applicable" and "not met" otherwise occur only as prose. The members and values this record reads — `intervals`, `outcome` with `converged`, `standing` `verified`, `not_different`, `lower`, `higher`, `cases_read`, `case`, `run_id`, `round` and the `instance.json` members of decision 1 — are taken with the meanings of their records on purpose.
- **Not edited and now less exact** (follow-ups for the integrator): the Boundaries line "The S6 metric, its boundary cases, repetitions and control (P2-8)" of ADR-0059, ADR-0071, ADR-0072, ADR-0073 and ADR-0112, the mention of the S6 metric in ADR-0075 Boundaries, the Status lines of ADR-0059, ADR-0071, ADR-0072, ADR-0073 and ADR-0075 that say the S6 metric stays open, and the line "The S6 metric and its tool" of ADR-0173 (now answered for one pair; repetitions and control remain); ADR-0073 decision 3 "which one the S6 criterion counts is its metric (P2-8, OD-30)" (answered: `case` entries); FR-18's "지표 정의의 확정 = OD-30" (the metric of one pair is now defined under OD-30; still true as a pointer, OD-30 stays `제안됨`); ADR-0050 decision 3 "for two measurements of different sessions and for more than two candidates stay with OD-13 and OD-1" (still true for a run; the metric now has its own rules for both); PLAN P2-8's "지표 정의는 OD-30 종결 필요" (the metric it needs is now recorded; PLAN belongs to the driver).

## Boundaries (not decided here)

- **An order of more than two measurements, and a comparison across sessions, for the run itself** (OD-13, OD-1; ADR-0050 decision 3): the metric's rules of decisions 1 and 2 are for the metric only.
- **Owner (sub-question ③, HQ-0081)** — this record's reading: the spend is the owner's value judgement on the owner's subscription and hardware (the kind of judgement DRIVER §9 keeps for #5 A/B items), and the owner performs the test (PLAN P2-9): the number of pairs and how their verdicts are combined (counts of `met`, `not met` and `not applicable`); whether a control run with the case lookup off is required, and how such a run would be made; how much of the C-7 subscription and of hardware time (C-4) the acceptance test may spend. Also owner-only and untouched: #5 A and B items, C-6, OD-25, OD-3.
- **Objective types other than latency minimisation** and any combination of objectives (OD-1 residue, #5 A-4); a pair whose instance states a constraint (`memory_ceiling`: ADR-0050 decisions 4 and 5, ADR-0052 decision 7).
- **`best.json` and `best_ref`** (OD-20); **the baseline** beyond ADR-0119 (OD-28); **the incumbent and paired re-measurement**, and with them a drift-free comparison across sessions (OD-11 proposal, OD-13 sub-question ①); what a run does with a `not_converged` measurement beyond ADR-0113, ADR-0114 and ADR-0184, including the owner's G3 (OD-13 sub-question ②; if a record changes how such comparisons count, it does not change this metric, which already leaves them out); which execution of an attempt counts for a run (the rest of OD-18 sub-question ⑳).
- **Round 0 and cases**: whether bring-up uses cases (ADR-0073 Boundaries), and a baseline taken from an existing best in a resumed or branched run (OD-28, outside ADR-0119) — it would make a second run's round 0 reach the first-run best by construction, so every such pair would be **not applicable** by (iii); the record that decides it revisits (iii).
- **Promotion**: the case condition can hold only if the first run's rounds were promoted to `db/` before the second run's selections read its key. For first-case runs [ADR-0133](ADR-0133-od23-first-case-round-promotion.md) gives that at each round end, a key stored ([ADR-0134](ADR-0134-od26-first-case-key-records-store.md)); elsewhere it is open (OD-29, OD-23, OD-30).
- **Environments where timing is hard or inexact** ([owner decision 2026-10-15](https://github.com/myungjoo/HIL-Claw/issues/5#issuecomment-6182984913), point 3): whether a verdict of a run found imprecise carries a warning, how it is shown, and whether its cases are reused is OD-13 sub-question ⑪ (다) and (라); decision 5 only reports the finding.
- **Budget axes `instance.json` does not state** (a token or hardware budget, OD-2, OD-19, #5 A-8; an early-stop target value, FR-3 ④): they cannot be pair conditions until a record states them.
- **Runs outside ADR-0110 decision 1 and ADR-0140 decision 1**: their standing and measurement, and so whether the metric covers them.
- **Sim-only hardware**, where a sim measurement may be final (OD-26); **similar-key pairs** (OD-4 residue; S6 step 1's "유사한 hw_profile").
- **Where an acceptance verdict is stored**, if anywhere: it is not a run record, and this record places it nowhere; a place in the data plane would be a new file (A-13).
- **The token ledger** (OD-19, NFR-5, #5 A-8); **result files** beside the log (`sim.json`, `board.json`; ADR-0104 decision 3 leaves them to a later record), which would hold the same facts.
- **The runtime acceptance itself** (PLAN P2-9; D16): the owner runs the pair on a runtime machine; the runs of P2-5's check, of the nightly routine (P2-10, D17) and of a test run (D25, until decided otherwise) are excluded by P2-9.
- Code, tests, fixtures and prompts (P2-8 and later); any change to an accepted record or to the `HIL-Claw-data` repository; any new external dependency.
