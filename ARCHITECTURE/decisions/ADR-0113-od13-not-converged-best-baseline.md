## Decision

### 1. Scope and terms

- **Scope**: a measurement held by a completed result — `outcome` `"converged"` or `"not_converged"` — of a call of `sim.run` or `board.run` about one attempt of a run that ADR-0061 decision 1 covers (cited): a run whose hw_profile names `cpu_standin` in the local configuration of [ADR-0038](ADR-0038-od18-cpu-standin-execution-points.md) decision 1, or names the mock. Both execution points are in the scope.
- **To stand as a best**: to be the measurement whose samples stand for the run's best in a comparison or a report. **To stand as the baseline of round 0**: to be the measurement whose samples stand for the baseline of FR-1 in the comparisons of later rounds. These phrases are this record's, not names of any file or member.
- **Eligible**: a measurement is eligible to stand if nothing about its timing outcome bars it. How a best is chosen from eligible measurements, how they are ordered and which baseline a run uses are not part of eligibility (decision 3). A rule that prefers one eligible measurement to another does not make the other ineligible.

### 2. The answer: the mark does not make a measurement ineligible

- **A measurement whose result has `outcome` `"not_converged"` is eligible to stand as a best and to stand as the baseline of round 0, as a measurement whose result has `outcome` `"converged"` is.** The mark alone never makes a measurement ineligible, and eligibility needs nothing first: no re-measurement and no flag that must be cleared. **This is about eligibility only.** Whether a rule that chooses a best among eligible measurements prefers converged ones (for example "a not-converged one only if no converged one is available", or the mark as a tie-break) is part of that rule, which is the rest of OD-1 (#5 A-4, owner-only), and is not decided here (decision 3; Alternatives (c1)). The two questions get one answer, for the reasons below.
- **Why** (this record's own):
  - *ADR-0007 already deals with the noise where it matters.* A not-converged measurement "enters decision 7 like any other" and its wide interval makes it "rarely called different from anything" (cited: ADR-0007 decision 4). So where a candidate is compared against an incumbent best (whether it is belongs to the choice rule, decision 3), a noisy candidate seldom displaces the incumbent, and a noisy incumbent is seldom shown to be beaten. NFR-3 asks that variance be taken into account "반복 측정/신뢰구간 등으로" (cited: Requirements). This record reads that as an interval and not a cut-off, and the effect above is that of an interval. A second bar on top of it would count the same noise twice.
  - *O1 asks the run to go on under ADR-0007 as it stands.* Where timing is noisy, most measurements may end not converged. If such a measurement could never stand, a run there could have no baseline and no best, and could not "continue and optimize on relative numbers". O1 also expects a best to be shown in that environment ("with each result or best shown").
  - *FR-1 needs a baseline from round 0.* If the baseline measurement of round 0 does not converge and cannot stand, the run either has no basis for evaluate or must measure again, and measuring again needs what is still open (Alternatives (b) and (c2)).
  - *ADR-0007 decision 4 calls it "usable".* Its Alternatives rejected "A measurement that does not converge is unusable" because it "would have to be taken again, which costs hardware time (C-4) and needs a retry policy (OD-10)" (cited). Barring it from standing would bring that cost back by another route.
  - *It needs nothing that is still open* (Alternatives).
- **Same answer for both, and why.** A baseline is the base of the comparisons of later rounds, and where a best is the base of later comparisons (whether it is belongs to the choice rule, decision 3), it is so in the same way. In each role the measurement enters decision 7 as one side of a pair, and its spread does the same there. Neither role allows the noise to do more harm than the other: a noisy base makes it harder to show any change, in either direction.

### 3. What the answer does not touch

- **Other bars stay as they are** (cited, nothing added): a broken result holds no measurement and so has nothing that can stand (ADR-0009 decision 8; ADR-0063 decision 3); a result that fails verification never enters the best (FR-9; #5 A-6); a memory ceiling that is *over* makes a candidate infeasible (OD-1 frame, owner answer HQ-0009).
- **How a best is chosen and ordered** (the rest of OD-1, #5 A-4; OD-15; OD-29), **which baseline a run uses** (OD-28), **how a best is stored** (`best_ref`, OD-20) and **which of several results of one attempt at one point counts** (OD-18 sub-question ⑳) are not decided. The answer holds under any of them: none of them is an input to it, and it is not an input to them beyond saying that the mark does not make a measurement ineligible. **Whether a later choice rule uses the mark among eligible measurements** (for example as a tie-break) is part of that rule, and is not decided here. **Its counterpart for the baseline** — whether a converged measurement is preferred where more than one measurement could be the baseline — goes with the choice of baseline (OD-28) and with which of several results counts (OD-18 sub-question ⑳), and is not decided here either.
- **A baseline that is not such a result.** If OD-28 chooses a base that is not a measurement of the scope (for example, a vendor library kernel measured some other way), this record says nothing about it.
- **The paired re-measurement** (OD-13 sub-question ①) is not decided. If it is adopted, a re-measured incumbent is another measurement, and this record applies to it as to any other.

### 4. A run found imprecise

- A measurement that is not converged is found imprecise on the measured ground (cited: ADR-0103 decision 3), and its run is found imprecise from that measurement's end line on (cited: ADR-0103 decision 5; ADR-0104 decision 4). **So a run whose best or baseline is a not-converged measurement, read from its run tools' end line under ADR-0104 decision 3, is a run found imprecise by the time that best or baseline exists.** Every report of its numbers then falls under O1's warning, with each best shown. Letting such a measurement stand hides nothing from the user.
- This is O1's "continue and optimize on relative numbers" read for the best and the baseline: the run keeps its best and its baseline, and the warning says that improvements over them "may be hard to establish". Decision 2 makes that literal: a noisy base widens every later interval it enters.
- ADR-0104 decision 6 notes that "A number named in a best or a decision log gets a finding only if that record lets a reader find the end line the number came from" (cited). That still holds, and this record adds no link from a best to an end line. The finding of the run, which is what O1's warning needs, does not depend on that link.
- No rule, constant or ground of ADR-0103 or ADR-0104 changes.

### 5. Limits

1. **A not-converged best is sticky.** Where a candidate is compared against a noisy best (whether it is belongs to the choice rule, decision 3), the two are seldom called different, so a run can keep a noisy best for many rounds even when a candidate is in fact better. O1 accepts this ("improvements may be hard to establish"). The registered remedy, the paired re-measurement of ①, is open.
2. **A lucky noisy measurement.** With 5 to 10 samples the bootstrap interval is approximate (cited: ADR-0007 decision 10), so a noisy measurement can be called different by chance. Where a candidate is compared against the best (whether it is belongs to the choice rule, decision 3), such a verdict can make that measurement a best. This holds for converged measurements too, and the mark does not make it more likely: a wider spread makes a "different" verdict less likely.
3. **Absolute values.** A not-converged best has a trimmed mean like any measurement (cited: ADR-0007 decision 5). Its absolute value is less trustworthy, and that is the warning of O1, not a bar here.
4. **Outside the scope** — a simulator, a board, or another configuration — ② stays open. Their noise and values are OD-13 sub-question ③.
5. **A best or baseline carried into another run** (a resumed or branched run, S3; reuse across runs, FR-18) is not covered: whether the finding travels with it is OD-13 sub-question ⑪ (다), and how such a run is represented is OD-8 sub-question ①.

## Consequences

- **No code slice follows from this record alone.** Eligibility under it is `outcome` being `"converged"` or `"not_converged"`, which every completed result has (ADR-0063 decision 2), so a predicate would add nothing. What follows is a constraint on later work: the code that sets the baseline of round 0 and keeps the best (PLAN P2-1; OD-23 for the component) must not treat a measurement as ineligible because its result is `"not_converged"`. Whether its choice rule prefers converged measurements is the rest of OD-1. No module is bound.
