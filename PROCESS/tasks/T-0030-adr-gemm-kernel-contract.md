## Why
A kernel cannot be written, built into a harness, calibrated or timed before one thing is known: **how it is called**. Three accepted records leave exactly this open and point at each other:
- ADR-0001 (OD-4) makes `layout` a required key field of `op_signature` and gives it no value: "B-4 gives no layout"; the example uses `NOT-DECIDED-HERE`, so the real directory names of the first instances do not exist yet.
- ADR-0002 (OD-6) decision 6 (a): the spec **carries** the contract of an operation kind in `task.contract` (`language`, `prototype`, `operands`) "and does not define it"; its example has `NOT-DECIDED-HERE` for the prototype and the operands.
- ADR-0003 (OD-16) Consequences: the P1-4 calibration kernels depend on "the kernel prototype".

The open points are **registered**: OpenDecisionProposals OD-6 "하위 질문 ①" ("미결: 그 값(하네스 쪽 계약 — PLAN P1-3), 첫 GEMM의 prototype·피연산자 저장 순서") and OD-4 "제안으로 남는 부분" ("첫 인스턴스의 layout 값(row-major는 통합자 가정 그대로)"). Both register rows end with "layout·커널 prototype 등 출처 없는 값은 미정".

Things that wait for it: the three C calibration kernels of PLAN P1-4; the kernel-running rest of PLAN P1-2 (`cpu_standin` adapter, timing collection — T-0013 / T-0019 Follow-ups: "needs the kernel calling convention"); the C gate of PLAN P0.5-1 (nothing in C to build); the real names under `db/` of the twelve first `op_signature`s; the committed spec example of PLAN P1-5.

Owner decision D6 (DRIVER §9) lets the driver close **design-level** ODs by a decision record (ADR) + a context-isolated reviewer's Approve + an owner notice with a 7-day objection window. This task writes that record for **one part of the rest of OD-6 (sub-question ①: the values of the operational contract of the kind `gemm` for kernels written in C) together with the first `layout` value that OD-4 left open**, and keeps the ARCHITECTURE documents consistent with it. It ticks **no PLAN checkbox** (prerequisite slice of P1-2 / P1-3 / P1-4 / P1-5).

## Acceptance Criteria
What is **fixed** and must be taken as input, not re-decided:
- #5 A-7 ②: stand-in kernels are C compiled with clang as the single toolchain; OpenMP and SIMD intrinsics are allowed. C-7: the same behaviour on Linux, macOS and WSL.
- #5 B-4: the first operations are the Llama 3.2 1B decoder projections gate/up 2048→8192, down 8192→2048, QKV 2048→3072, O 2048→2048 (lm_head excluded), M ∈ {1, 32, 512} as an instance parameter, dtype fp32 (bf16 weights upcast); only shapes are used, no weights; nntrainer is a kernel-code reference only; OpenBLAS `cblas_sgemm` is a **candidate** baseline (OD-28, open).
- ADR-0001: for `gemm`, `shape` has exactly `m`, `k`, `n` — "the product of an m×k and a k×n matrix is an m×n matrix"; for a projection `in`→`out` over M tokens, `m` = M, `k` = `in`, `n` = `out`; `layout` is a *name* (`[a-z][a-z0-9_]{0,31}`) and a key field.
- ADR-0002: `task.contract` = `language` (name; `c`), `prototype` (text: "declaration of the entry point the kernel must define, in that language, exactly as the harness calls it"), `operands` (array of `{name, role, dtype, shape}`, role `input` | `output` | `inout`, every shape value one of the `op_signature` shape values). A spec has one `op_signature`, so one value of M.
- FR-9 / ADR-0003: the harness, not the coder, calls the kernel; the same prototype serves coder output and the hand-written calibration kernels.

What the ADR **decides**. The content of each decision is the executor's to work out and justify from the fixed inputs; the reviewer's Approve is what confirms it. For every point give the choice, the alternatives considered and one or two lines of reason, and mark every value no source gives as this record's own:
1. **Scope and terms**: what "the operational contract of a kind" consists of (the `layout` value plus the values of `task.contract`), what it is bound to (kind `gemm`, kernel language `c`, the one layout value), and that it binds both sides — what the caller guarantees and what the kernel must do.
2. **The first `layout` value**: its name and its meaning — for every operand, the element order, contiguity and anything else about storage (no padding, no strides, …), complete enough that no operand entry needs a member beyond those of ADR-0002 (see the A-13 note in `## Why`). State that the `layout` vocabulary stays open (ADR-0001 decision 6).
3. **The operation**: which operand is the m×k matrix, which the k×n matrix and which the m×n result; that it is the plain product, or which further terms exist (scaling factors, accumulation into the output, transposition flags, bias) — each either excluded or argued. Address the point "weight operand as k×n versus [out, in] storage" explicitly, with alternatives.
4. **The C prototype of the entry point**: the symbol name, return type, parameter order and types, qualifiers; whether the dimensions are parameters or constants known from the spec; the exact text as it goes into `task.contract.prototype`. Say how the name is chosen so that a later kind does not collide, without defining a later kind.
5. **The operand entries** of `task.contract.operands` for this prototype (name, role, dtype, shape as a function of `m`, `k`, `n`), and one complete `task.contract` object for gate/up at M = 1 that satisfies the rules of ADR-0002 and of `hilclaw/spec.py`.
6. **Guarantees and obligations at a call**: at least — overlap of operands (aliasing), content of the output on entry and which elements the kernel must write, whether inputs may be modified, the alignment the kernel may assume, who owns the memory and whether the kernel may keep pointers or state between calls, whether it may be called repeatedly in one process (ADR-0007 decision 2), what counts as the kernel's result (the output operand only). Decide what can be argued; anything that would fix the harness mechanism is a boundary.
7. **What the translation unit provides**: that the source defines this entry point with external linkage, and what else a kernel source may or may not contain as far as the contract needs to say it (for example no `main`). Do not fix how the harness loads the kernel.
8. **Change of the contract**: `op_signature` keys do not contain the prototype, so say what holds when the contract of (`gemm`, `c`, this layout) changes while kernels written for the old one are stored — either a rule that can be argued from ADR-0001 decision 5 / 6, or a registered sub-question. Do not add a field.
9. **The real `op_signature` records of the first instances**: with the layout fixed, the twelve records of B-4 (four shapes × three values of M) are complete. Give the rule, and for at least gate/up at M = 1 the canonical form, the digest and the directory name, **computed with `hilclaw.keys`**, replacing the placeholder of ADR-0001 decision 7 for the op side only (the hw side keeps its placeholders).

What the ADR must **not** decide — name each as a boundary in the record, with the place where it is decided:
- The reference oracle of `cpu_standin` (#5 A-7 "still open"; PLAN P1-3 "오너 확정 필요") and the tolerance vocabulary or any tolerance value (#5 B-7).
- How verification inputs are drawn (distribution, seed, number of draws), whether shapes outside the `op_signature` are drawn (OD-16 sub-question ①), and corner-case inputs.
- The harness mechanism: linking versus loading versus a separate process, the driver program, the timer, calls per repeat, input reuse (PLAN P1-3; ADR-0007 Boundaries); sandbox, timeouts and resource limits (OD-14).
- Build settings: compiler flags, `-march`, OpenMP runtime and thread count, the members of `task.build`, whether the coder may choose settings (OD-6 sub-question ②; T-0013 Follow-ups).
- The hw side: `hw_source`, `revision`, `host` strings and the clang version string of `cpu_standin` (OD-26 and the task that registers the profile); `task.objective` (OD-1 / #5 A-4); a technique of the stand-in catalog; a `run_id`.
- The coder's output: file names and form under `…/coder/…/output/`, how many source files a kernel has (OD-23, isolation means of OD-8).
- The baseline (OD-28) — the record may note whether the prototype makes a comparison with `cblas_sgemm` straightforward, and may not choose a baseline. The contract of the SDPA family and of any kind other than `gemm`; any kernel language other than C; fp16 / q4 variants.
- Any change of the spec schema, the key schema, `hilclaw/spec.py` or `hilclaw/keys.py`; any code, test, C file, CI change, committed example file, or any change to the `HIL-Claw-data` repository.
