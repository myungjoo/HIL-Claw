### Validity of the member (ADR-0023 decisions 3 and 4)
- `n_max` becomes a defined top-level member of version 1: it is checked by `parse` and `validate` and is **no longer in `Instance.others`**.
- **Absent**: the content is valid and states no bound. `schema_version` stays 1; `SCHEMA_VERSION` is unchanged.
- **Present**: an integer **from 5 to 2^53 - 1** (`hilclaw.keys.MAX_INTEGER`, which the module already holds as `_MAX`), written as a JSON number in decimal digits without sign, fraction or exponent. In terms of the module's parser: exactly an `int` (a `bool` is none, a `float` is none) with 5 <= value <= `_MAX`. Anything else makes the content invalid as a whole: `InvalidInstanceError`, nothing returned - not the hw_profile, the tolerance or the source commit either. In particular no value: `true`, `false`, `null`, a string (`"10"` included), an array (`[10]`), an object, a number with a fraction or an exponent (`10.0`, `1e1`, `5.0`, `1E1`), `-0`, `0`, every negative number (`-10`), the integers 1 to 4, an integer above 2^53 - 1 (`9007199254740992`).
- **The bounds are inclusive and each is pinned by a test**: 5 and `MAX_INTEGER` are values; 4 and `MAX_INTEGER + 1` are none. No upper limit below `MAX_INTEGER` is introduced: 10, 11, 30, 31 and 1000 are all values (decision 3: "No upper limit below the integer range"); nothing in the module compares the member with 10.
- **Pitfalls to avoid, each pinned by a test**: `isinstance(value, int)` accepts `True` - the check is on the exact type; `10.0 == 10` and `float.is_integer` must not make a float a value; `int(value)` must not be the rule (it accepts strings, floats and booleans); `-0` is read by the JSON parser as the integer 0 and is refused by the lower bound, not by its sign.
- **The value handed out** is the `int` the file states. Nothing is clamped, rounded or replaced.
- **Order**: the rules for the whole text (an integer too long for the interpreter anywhere in the text stays the existing text rule), then `schema_version` (another version is still `UnsupportedVersionError` before any other member is looked at, a broken `n_max` included), then the members of version 1. The first rule found broken is reported and names its place (`n_max`), by the module's `_fail` conventions.
- Every other top-level member stays open and uninterpreted (`nmax`, `N_max`, `n-max`, `nMax`, `n_min`, `repeats` included): passed over, returned in `others`, and the content then states no bound - never the default (decision 6, "A misspelt name").

- The validator does not check that any measurement was taken under the bound, that the harness of the running source accepts it (a harness whose `N_MIN` is above a stated bound refuses it - decision 6), or that a machine can allocate that many samples (decision 3).
