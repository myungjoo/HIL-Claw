## Acceptance Criteria
What is **fixed** and taken as input, not re-decided:
- #4 §1 [합의]: every turn starts with a clear context; one small `STATE.json` is the entry point and only artefacts it refers to are loaded; no whole-directory scan; the most expensive side effect is journaled at once. #4 §3b [합의]: a round restart is resumable per substep; an expensive result is appended to `events.ndjson` as soon as it exists; a fresh turn skips expensive substeps that are finished.
- ADR-0004 / ADR-0006: one `events.ndjson` per run, appended by the tool layer, never rewritten; a line is self-contained with `schema_version`, `time`, `kind`, `data` and optionally `round` / `candidate` / `attempt`; no sequence or identifier member in the envelope; the line of an expensive result carries the result; an appended line survives process death, not machine loss before the commit (#5 A-3); no concurrency control and no atomicity with any other file.
- ADR-0010: a tool is a function from one JSON object to one JSON object; a result that reports a failed experiment is a result, a tool failure means no outcome; the server keeps no state a later call relies on.
- FR-3: the budget vocabulary (four axes; any exhausted → the run ends, best kept). NFR-1: resume at the exact point after compaction or process death. C-4: the board is a shared, limited resource. C-2: one run at a time in P1. NFR-2: history is the audit record.

What the ADR **decides**. The content of each decision is the executor's to work out and justify from the fixed inputs; the reviewer's Approve confirms it. For every point give the choice, the alternatives considered and one or two lines of reason; mark each value as **cited** or as **this record's own**:
1. **Scope and terms**: the record is about a *journaled step* (a step whose repetition costs a limited resource or whose result must survive a process death) and about the *spend* of a run. Define the terms it uses. It is not the lock, not a retry policy, not enforcement.
2. **What is written around a journaled step**: whether a line is written before the step starts and one after it ends (the registered `intent` / `result` proposal), only after, or another form; argued against the failure the proposal names (death between the measurement and the record of it) and the opposite one (death after the "before" line and before any measurement). State which party writes them (the tool layer, ADR-0006) without choosing hook versus tool function (OD-18 rest).
3. **How two lines of one step are tied and how a repeated call is recognised**: the identity of a step — what it is built from (for example the `round` / `candidate` / `attempt` numbers of ADR-0002 plus what else distinguishes two steps of one attempt, or a hash of the request), where it is carried (inside `data`, since the envelope has no identifier member — or argue a change of the envelope, which would be a new envelope version under ADR-0006 decision 8), and its form. Use the minimum of new event kinds or `data` members the rule needs; name them, and say that every other kind and payload stays open (OD-8 sub-question ⑧).
4. **What a resumed turn does**: for each state the log can show for a step (no line, started and not finished, finished), what the turn may conclude and do — skip and take the result from the log, run it, or neither. For "started and not finished" say what is known and what is not, and do **not** choose the recovery (retry, wait, give up): that is OD-10 / the retry part of OD-17; state only what the journal guarantees to whoever decides.
5. **The role of `phase`** (#4 §3b): whether the marker in `STATE.json` remains, and if so which of the two is authoritative when the marker and the log disagree. Do not list the marker's values and do not define the member (PLAN P1-8).
6. **Accounting of the spend — the resolution of #8 M7**: where the authoritative record of an amount spent is (a line of the log, a stored value in `STATE.json`, or both with a stated precedence), what `budget_spent` in `STATE.json` is in relation to it (#7 G4: a mutable scalar loses the spend of a dead round), and **how a fresh turn obtains the total without a cost that grows with the length of the run** — or an argued statement that reading the one log file is within #4 §1, with the bound that makes it so. If a snapshot rule is chosen, state it completely (when a snapshot is taken, where it is kept, what is replayed after it, what happens when the snapshot and the log disagree). Neutral to the axis: an amount is "a quantity of some budget axis"; no axis list, no unit, no measuring party.
7. **What a spend line must hold for point 6 to work**, as a rule and not as a payload schema: for example that the amount is on the line that records the finished step, and what is recorded for a step that was started and never finished (its spend is unknown — say how the accounting treats it without inventing a number).
8. **Rebuilding**: which parts of `STATE.json` this record makes derivable from the log and which it does not speak of (`next_action`, `best_ref`, gate status are **not** this record's); whether "the whole `STATE.json` is a view" (#7 G4) is adopted, rejected or left open — leaving it open is acceptable and probably right.
9. **Limits**: what the rules do not protect against (two turns running at once — the lock; machine loss before the commit; a tool that spends without writing a line; a clock), each with the place where it is decided.

What the ADR must **not** decide — name each as a boundary in the record, with the place where it is decided:
- The rest of OD-17: `lock{owner, heartbeat}`, fencing by a compare-and-swap push, and the declarative retry policy per tool. Board failure and recovery, reservation (OD-10, #5 B-2).

- The member table of `STATE.json` and of `instance.json`, the values of `phase`, `next_action`, `best_ref` (OD-20), the result files of an attempt (PLAN P1-8). Reading other versions (OD-11).
